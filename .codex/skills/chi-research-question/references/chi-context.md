# CHI-Specific Context for Research Questions

ACM CHI (Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems) is the flagship HCI venue. A CHI-ready research question sits at the intersection of **people, technology, and context**, and maps onto a recognized contribution type.

## CHI contribution types (Wobbrock & Kientz taxonomy)

1. **Empirical** — new findings from studies of people using technology (experiments, surveys, interviews, field studies, log/telemetry analysis, ethnography). Most common CHI contribution. RQ pattern: "How does {technology/feature} affect {measurable human outcome} for {population} in {context}?"
2. **Artifact** — a novel system, technique, or interface, built and evaluated. RQ pattern: "How can {novel interaction technique/system} support {task/need} for {population}?" — the evaluation sub-question must still be empirical and measurable.
3. **Methodological** — new methods or measures for studying interaction. RQ pattern: "How can {phenomenon} be validly measured/studied {in new condition}?"
4. **Theoretical / Understanding** — frameworks, concepts, thick qualitative description of practices. RQ pattern: "How do {population} experience/appropriate/make sense of {technology} in {context}?"
5. **Dataset / Benchmark** — RQ pattern: "What are the characteristics of {interaction data} across {population/context}?"
6. **Survey / Meta-analysis** — RQ pattern: "What does existing evidence show about {relationship}, and where are the gaps?"

## Common CHI methods to suggest (be specific in Step 4)

- **Quantitative:** controlled lab experiment (within/between-subjects, factorial), crowdsourced experiment, A/B or field experiment, large-scale survey with validated scales (SUS, NASA-TLX, UEQ, PANAS), log analysis, computational modeling of behavior (Fitts' law, KLM)
- **Qualitative:** semi-structured interviews + thematic/grounded analysis, contextual inquiry, diary study, ethnography/autoethnography, participatory or co-design workshops, design probes, think-aloud usability study
- **Mixed:** sequential explanatory (quant → qual), convergent, research-through-design with formative + summative evaluation

## Feasibility calibration by project scale

| Scale                     | Realistic RQ scope                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Course project (2–3 mo)   | Small lab/online study n≈12–30, survey n≈100–200, interview study n≈8–15, or heuristic/comparative evaluation of existing systems |
| Master's thesis (6–12 mo) | Medium experiment n≈24–60, prototype + evaluation, diary study 2–4 wk, mixed-methods                                              |
| PhD chapter / CHI paper   | Novel system + rigorous evaluation, multi-study package, longitudinal field deployment, large-scale log analysis                  |

Do not recommend multi-year longitudinal deployments, clinical trials, or access-restricted populations to time-boxed students.

## Hot and durable CHI themes (for originality scoring)

Human-AI interaction and trust in AI; LLM-based agents and copilots; accessibility and assistive technology; AR/VR/mixed reality; health, wellbeing, and mental health tech; privacy, security, and dark patterns; sustainability/eco-feedback; HCI4D and low-resource contexts (e.g., Global South user populations); creativity support tools; social computing, misinformation, and moderation; children, older adults, and neurodiverse users; embodied and tangible interaction; future of work and remote collaboration.

Originality boosters: understudied population × established technology; established measure × new technology (e.g., trust scales applied to LLM agents); Global South or non-WEIRD context replication with cultural adaptation; longitudinal or in-the-wild versions of lab-only findings.

## Ethics flags (annotate in the limitation line)

Vulnerable populations (children, patients, refugees), deception, sensitive data (health, location, private messages), AI-generated content shown as real, and low-resource participant compensation all require early IRB/ethics-board attention — note this explicitly.

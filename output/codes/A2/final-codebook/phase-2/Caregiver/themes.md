# Phase 2 caregiver themes

The caregiver analysis is organized as two themes. The first explains how the agent's initiative becomes acceptable to caregivers. The second explains how caregivers interpreted the streak as a record of their care work rather than as motivation for necessary medication. Neither theme claims improved adherence or clinical effectiveness.

## 1. Caregivers accept agent coordination that remains selective and accountable

**Central organizing concept:** Caregivers accept the agent when it follows existing care roles, intervenes selectively, exposes uncertainty for human checking, and leaves medication authority and final responsibility with people.

**Constituent subthemes:** Prompts follow existing care roles; Escalation stays selective and consented; Exceptions focus caregiver attention without claiming adherence; Verification preserves human authority.

Caregivers did not accept the agent simply because it could remind or notify. They accepted a particular coordination process. The initial prompt went to the person already responsible for the next task. Family involvement remained limited to unresolved events and required an understood agreement. A missing confirmation prompted inquiry rather than a missed dose judgment. Prescriptions, clinicians, and family checking continued to govern medication decisions. These conditions made the agent accountable to the care network.

### Prompts follow existing care roles

The agent became useful when its direction matched the household's existing division of medication work. It could remind an older adult who took medicine independently, or the caregiver who administered a dose. Voice and medicine specific information made the next action noticeable during competing work. Proposed prescription and stock information followed the same rule: it should reach the person responsible for verification or supply. Those proposed features were not deployed and support design expectations only.

### Escalation stays selective and consented

Caregivers accepted escalation when the first actor had not resolved the task, the older adult knew that family could be involved, and only relevant medication information moved. Retry, time, and a selected relative kept the handoff proportional to the problem. Continuous updates or unrelated access would turn family support into excessive involvement.

### Exceptions focus caregiver attention without claiming adherence

Timed reminders allowed caregivers to stop holding every dose time in continuous attention. Selective alerts then directed them toward events requiring a question. This process did not establish whether medicine was swallowed because action and confirmation could diverge. An acceptable alert therefore identified the medicine and the reason for concern while leaving the event open to family interpretation. False or unexplained alerts could recreate vigilance and spread unnecessary worry.

### Verification preserves human authority

Caregivers compared the configured schedule with prescriptions, medicine strips, established routines, doctors, and pharmacists. Repeated agreement allowed ordinary reminders to require less checking, while a prescription change or conflicting record reopened verification. Families also retained fallback routines and could stop using an agent that produced errors, excessive alerts, or maintenance work. The agent could coordinate an authorized routine, but it could not prescribe, change medicine, or replace relational care.

## 2. Caregivers interpret streaks as records of routine care work

**Central organizing concept:** Caregivers interpreted the streak as a visible record of otherwise quiet medication coordination, while refusing to treat the score as a reason for taking necessary medicine.

**Constituent subthemes:** Family visibility acknowledges care work; Medicine remains independent of the score.

The data does not support a claim that streaks encouraged medication taking. Caregivers repeatedly placed the medicine above the score, and one said that the schedule would continue whether a score existed or not. What the streak offered was visibility. For a caregiver coordinating several medicines across a day, accumulated progress could show that the work had been maintained. When shared within the care network, that trace allowed family members to notice the caregiver's contribution instead of leaving it as invisible background labor.

### Family visibility acknowledges care work

Caregivers described the streak as showing that medicines were being given regularly and that sustained coordination had been maintained. Basic visibility within the family could therefore acknowledge scheduling, prompting, confirming, and checking. This was recognition of care work, not a claim that the caregiver or the older adult needed a reward to perform it.

### Medicine remains independent of the score

A broken streak first raised a factual question: was the medicine missed, or was only the confirmation absent? Caregivers prioritized resolving that question and continuing the medication routine. The score remained secondary, private to the involved family, and unable to establish ingestion. If it created pressure, blame, competition, or anxiety, it ceased to serve even its limited role as a record of care work.

## Adversarial boundary check

The first theme could be misread as evidence that caregivers broadly accepted autonomous decision making. The data supports a narrower claim. Caregivers accepted configured reminders and bounded escalation when they could understand, verify, and revoke them. The second theme could be misread as evidence that a streak measures care quality. Confirmation does not establish ingestion, and a visible streak captures only work represented through the prototype. It makes some care work visible while leaving other care work unrecorded.

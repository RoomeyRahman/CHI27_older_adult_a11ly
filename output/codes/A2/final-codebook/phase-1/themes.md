## Interpretive frame: relational interdependence in Bangladesh

The first theme makes the collectivist angle empirical rather than cultural shorthand. Medication work moves through a care network because different people hold different capacities: an older adult may recognize and take a familiar tablet, kin may preserve the prescription and supply, pharmacists may interpret handwriting, and doctors retain authority over changes. Reminding likewise follows presence, while checking can communicate care and create pressure. P10 articulated this relational orientation directly: "we don't live in isolation. My family ensures my medicine stock is full, and my community friends keep my spirits high. Technology should complement that" (P10, Phase 1, L84). Independent self-management and refusal remain inside the theme as limits. The finding is a situated account of relational interdependence in these Bangladeshi households, not a claim about every household or any entire region.

## From current tools to future design expectations

The third and fourth themes form a contrast rather than two independent lists. Current practices show what already works and where coordination breaks; elicited future features are acceptable when they repair that specific break without displacing the people who currently make the practice accountable.

| Current practice or tension | Elicited future expectation | Design opportunity | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic alarms attract attention but may not identify the medicine or required action. | A spoken Bangla cue names the medicine, timing, and relation to food, with a visual match where useful. | Make prompts semantically actionable while preserving direct older adult participation. | Voice and visual preferences were commonly elicited; one older adult also required privacy around others. |
| Multi-step apps, small print, handwriting, and shared or button phones redistribute interaction to kin or pharmacists. | Use minimal interaction, family-supported setup, and information that trusted people can verify against the prescription. | Design direct use and proxy configuration as complementary modes. | Prescription capture was elicited in Phase 1 and remained a design concept; it was not implemented in the later deployment. |
| Distance and simultaneous busyness leave caregivers uncertain and can make repeated checking burdensome. | Repeat first, allow time, then notify one caregiver about a meaningful exception in a visible way. | Coordinate a bounded handoff rather than broadcast continuous compliance. | A missing confirmation remains uncertain and cannot prove that medication was missed. |
| Fixed places, notes, prescriptions, ordinary alarms, and family questions already work in some households. | Add technology only where it extends these arrangements, with kin configuring and clinicians retaining medication authority. | Make adoption optional and preserve refusal, existing tools, and family presence. | Several older adults and one caregiver reported no current need for more technology. |
| Stock depletion and changed packaging interrupt action even when time is remembered. | Participants proposed low-stock notices to the family member who buys medicine and help interpreting prescription changes. | Support supply and interpretation handoffs, not reminders alone. | These were elicited expectations; prescription capture was not implemented in the later deployment. |

## Gamification boundary for Phase 1

Phase 1 contains no direct evaluation of streaks, points, scores, badges, rewards, or other game-like features. It would therefore be inaccurate to add an older adult gamification preference as a Phase 1 theme. The data instead establishes four constraints that a later gamification analysis must respect: taking medicine matters more than recording it, repeated prompting can create pressure, family involvement should remain selective, and technology should complement relational care. Whether older adults experienced a streak as encouragement, pressure, competition, loss, or a relational occasion belongs to Phase 2 lived evidence.

# P15, Phase 2: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Participants/P15.md`. Older adult, 80, man; the same participant as Phase 1 P15 (many medicines, follows the written prescription, family and doctors when required). Deployment device not recorded (CLAUDE.md 3.2). Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned. No consent-exclusion record is filed. Variant guide, mostly conditional; evidence-grade rule as in the Phase 2 P01 memo.

## What this account is about

An 80-year-old man taking eight to ten medicines who places authority in the written prescription: 'The prescription is the authority for me. The system should follow that, not make its own decision.' He used the agent 'with some help from my family' and did not depend on it before: 'I am used to following the prescription.' The agent is mainly for him, and family 'can also know if there is some issue'; he 'would not want unnecessary information to be shared' and does not want 'somebody doing everything for me.' He raises a possibility no other account names: 'sometimes there can be a technical problem or I may not respond even though I have taken it.' Family checking changed 'A little. They may check the system if there is a problem. But normally I still manage myself.' A false alarm 'can create unnecessary concern.' The score: 'I take it because the doctor prescribed it'; 'One broken streak should not create panic.' Trust: family helped him see the entries matched the prescription; he would recheck on any change. Memory: 'an additional reminder'; 'The written prescription already gives me the routine.' His one substantive want is stock: 'I have missed doses before when the medicine stock ran out or the medicine was unavailable to buy.' Closing: 'Technology can remind and organise. Doctor decides the medicine.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'The prescription is the authority for me.' Does the written prescription, not the doctor in person or the family, sit above the agent in this household, and does that make the agent a reader of an existing document?
2. 'there can be a technical problem.' Is he the only participant to attribute a possible silence to the system rather than to himself?
3. 'when the medicine stock ran out or the medicine was unavailable to buy.' Are missed doses here a supply problem, which relocates the agent's useful advocacy to stock rather than to adherence?

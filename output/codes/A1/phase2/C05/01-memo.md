Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Caregiver/C05.md`. Caregiver, 20, daughter-in-law living with her husband's family; she gives her father-in-law and mother-in-law their medicines. Evidence-grade rule as in the Phase 2 P01 memo; the account mixes lived routine with conditionals.

**Household configuration note.** In this account the agent's reminder went to the caregiver, not to the older adults: 'Mostly it reminded me, because I am the one managing their medicines.' She confirms and gives. The allegiance question therefore has a different shape here: the agent's first principal is the caregiver who administers, and the older adults are 'okay with it' because 'the main thing is getting the medicine at the right time.' No other Phase 2 account has this configuration so plainly.

## What this account is about

A young daughter-in-law for whom the agent is a reminder in her own household work: 'If I am busy doing household work, the reminder helps me remember that it is time.' It became 'part of the routine.' She wants it 'very noticeable when I am busy. A call would be good.' Veto: 'The final decision should still be with me and the family.' Silence: she would not assume they had taken it; 'maybe I was busy or the reminder was missed, and I would check.' A record discrepancy: 'I would check what actually happened before trusting the record.' Changes: check with the doctor or family; 'it should not decide which medicine they should take.' Family checking on her: 'I think it is normal for family members to check, especially when it is about elderly people.' Score: noticed, not important; 'I would want to know why it was missed.' Trust: paid attention to every reminder at first, then comfortable. Memory: 'I do not have to keep thinking about every exact time when I am busy'; she would go back to remembering. Principal: 'the whole family, but especially for the people taking the medicine and for me because I am the one managing it.' Closing: 'caring for elderly people is a daily responsibility... it gives me peace of mind.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'Mostly it reminded me.' When the caregiver is the confirming user, does the agent's allegiance to the older adult pass entirely through her?
2. 'the family knows that I am taking care of them... it is normal for family members to check.' Is the daughter-in-law herself the object of family checking, and does she accept it as normal for elder care?
3. 'A call would be good.' Does household work make the phone's ordinary notification insufficient, so that the agent must interrupt her to serve them?
4. 'before trusting the record.' Does the caregiver, as the one who presses, hold the record below her own knowledge of what happened?

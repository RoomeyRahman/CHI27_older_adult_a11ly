## What this account is about

A working daughter who checks the medicine of her parents and her aunt describes the agent as 'mainly helping with my parents and my aunty's medicine' and therefore 'helping me also'. Because she is not always home, 'it cannot always depend only on me', and another family member should know of an important miss. A warning 'should explain what the problem is.' The change she reports: 'Before, if I was not at home, I would call them and ask. Now the reminder can do some of that work. But I would still check with them, especially my mother.' What she keeps: 'I still want to know if they took the medicine. The difference is I don't have to keep remembering every single time myself.' Decisions: doctor and family, grounded in a past episode where her father 'had a problem with a medicine and the doctor changed it.' Score: 'I would not want that to make my mother anxious'; basic progress shared, not every detail. Trust: compare with the prescription, 'our main reference'; 'I don't have to keep checking the clock.' Direction: 'A normal reminder can stay with the person taking the medicine and me'; repeated misses, a wrong medicine, or a change go to the family. The main problem before 'was timing' when she was at work.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'it cannot always depend only on me.' Does the caregiver want the agent to distribute her own load to other kin, so that the agent's second recipient is a substitute for her absence?
2. 'I don't have to keep checking the clock.' Is what the agent relieved a form of clock-watching that was her invisible work?
3. 'the person taking the medicine and me.' Does she place herself inside the normal reminder's circle by default, and is that the daughter's assumed standing?

## What this account is about

A software engineer who set up the agent for his mother, who 'forgets quite often'. The shift he narrates is her uptake: 'After my mother started responding to the voice reminder herself. Before that I was thinking I will have to manage everything. Then she started hearing it and taking the medicine. I only check when there is something to check.' His formula: 'Less pressure, not less responsibility.' Before, 'Somebody would ask her. Sometimes me, sometimes my father, sometimes another family member. There was no fixed person.' Now 'the app does the first reminder. So we don't have to keep asking her at night, did you take it, did you take it.' Silence: 'Earlier maybe I would [assume]. Now I would think maybe she did not hear it or maybe she forgot. So I would check'; 'no confirmation itself tells me I may need to check.' Asking after a notification 'is still caring.' Changes: 'I don't want the app to keep using the old information. We update it.' Streak: 'a nice extra thing'; 'good, you are keeping it properly'; a break means check why; 'a small motivation, not another source of pressure'; shared within the family; he would 'rather see my mother's progress' than have points himself. Probation: checked names and times against what she was taking; 'For some days I still kept the old written information nearby'; trusted once 'my mother was actually responding'. Memory: 'The medicine time feels more fixed now. My mother also seems to expect the reminder.' Consent: 'When the person using it agrees. In our case my mother is okay with me knowing.' Failures include 'if it changes something without telling us' and 'too much work to maintain'. Closing: 'less pressure but family is still involved'; 'it should help us, not make another job for us.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'After my mother started responding to the voice reminder herself.' Is the caregiver's shift from managing to checking triggered by the older adult's own uptake rather than by any configuration?
2. 'did you take it, did you take it.' Is the repeated night-time asking the caregiver's version of the 'yes yes' loop, now removed?
3. 'For some days I still kept the old written information nearby.' Is the paper record a probationary backstop on the caregiver side as on the older-adult side?
4. 'Less pressure, not less responsibility.' Is this the caregiver's counterpart to 'reduces the pressure on the family without removing the family from it'?

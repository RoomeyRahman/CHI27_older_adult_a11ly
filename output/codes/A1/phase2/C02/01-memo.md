## What this account is about

A son and grandson who 'mostly looks after these things' describes the agent as mainly for his parents and grandfather and 'also helping me.' Sharing a miss within the family is not a problem because 'We already remind each other normally.' A big alarm 'without explaining is not enough.' Change: 'Earlier we mostly depended on memory and family members reminding each other. Now the agent can remind them directly. So there is less need for me to keep asking every time.' Care: 'it reduces some work, but it doesn't mean we stop caring.' Decisions: doctor or pharmacist; 'The agent can tell me that something changed, but a person should confirm it'; a change should come with 'what changed and why.' Score: 'medicine is not a competition'; a streak at zero should not stress everyone. Trust: compare with the prescription because 'my grandfather takes three to four medicines at different times'; 'In the beginning I would check everything myself.' What it relieved: 'I don't have to keep thinking, okay, now is the time for this medicine, now that one.' Direction: 'Normal reminder can stay between the person and the agent, or me if I am managing it'; repeated misses to him or another member. 'Medicine is not something where the system can make guesses.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'or me if I am managing it.' Is the caregiver's inclusion in the normal circle conditional on his managing role, a step more qualified than C01's 'and me'?
2. 'a person should confirm it.' Does he give the agent a reporting role about change and reserve confirmation for people?
3. 'the system can make guesses.' Is the older adults' refusal to guess mirrored on the caregiver side as a demand on the system?

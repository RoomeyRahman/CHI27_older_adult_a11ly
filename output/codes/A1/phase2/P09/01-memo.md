# P09, Phase 2: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Participants/P09.md`. Older adult, 68, woman, rural household; the same participant as Phase 1 P09 (son and daughter-in-law manage; she cannot read). Household smartphone shared or proxy-operated during deployment. Translated from Bangla and heavily edited: the file is organized under the guide's own section titles, and every turn is a single clean paragraph. I read it as a faithful summary of positions rather than as interactional detail. She addresses the interviewer as 'baba' throughout, which I keep in extracts. No consent-exclusion record is filed. Evidence-grade rule as in the Phase 2 P01 memo.

## What this account is about

A woman who cannot read and depends on her children to name and bring the medicine describes the agent as a ringing that the whole household hears: 'If the alarm rang and I was doing something, my daughter-in-law could also hear it and remind me. So it became a family thing, I suppose.' The machine 'cannot do everything'; the children bring and explain, and a new medicine must be explained by them because 'I don't understand the long names on the paper.' Loud is better 'because I may not see anything on the phone.' Escalation: ring, ring again, then the children check; 'If it keeps ringing because I did not take it, then I understand I forgot.' Checking changed a little: 'Now the ringing reminds me first. But they still ask sometimes.' Care is unchanged: 'my children caring for me is still the main thing. The ringing is just helping them remember also.' The score means little: 'I don't understand these scores very much'; 'Medicine is more important than the score.' Trust: her son told her when it should ring and they watched. Memory: 'Before I just kept the times in my head... Now when it rings, I know, okay, medicine time.' Everyone together; 'Keep it simple.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'my daughter-in-law could also hear it.' Does a shared household phone make the reminder audible to the caregiver directly, so that the agent's sharing happens through sound rather than notification?
2. 'it became a family thing, I suppose.' Is the hedge a recognition that the agent was shared by circumstance rather than by decision?
3. 'The ringing is just helping them remember also.' Is the agent's first beneficiary, in her account, the children's memory?
4. 'I may not see anything on the phone.' Does her non-reading fix the agent as sound only, and place every visual mechanism (score, heat map) outside her use?

## Absences

The son's and daughter-in-law's views; whether any notification reached them; what the announcement said; any decline; the heat map, which she could not have read.

## Interactional conditions

Conditional probes throughout; frames offered on care, dependence, replacement. Lived anchors: L14, L22, L58, L102, L106, L110, L142.

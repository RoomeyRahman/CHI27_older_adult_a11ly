Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Participants/P16.md`. Older adult, 76, woman; the same participant as Phase 1 P16 (husband notes down her medicines; she follows written notes). Deployment device not recorded (CLAUDE.md 3.2). Translated from Bangla, edited into single-paragraph turns under the guide's section titles, each in quotation marks in the source. ## What this account is about

A short account from a woman whose husband writes her medicine notes. The agent was 'mainly' for her, and her husband 'was also involved' because 'he already notes down my medicines and reminds me if I forget, it is natural that he knows.' The notes stay primary: 'I would check my notes first'; 'It did not replace my notes. I still prefer having them.' The husband's reminding continues: 'He still reminds me sometimes. It is helpful when he does. The system just makes it easier.' Escalation: remind again, then the husband; not every time, because she may already have taken it. Prescription change: notes must change too; 'I would not want the system to decide that by itself.' Score: 'the number is not important to me'; 'I don't want to worry about a score.' Probation: compare with the notes; the husband can check at the beginning. Closing: both of them; 'He only needs to know if I am actually missing the medicine or if there is some problem'; 'My notes are working well for me. The reminder is just extra help.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'it is natural that he knows.' Does the husband's existing record-keeping make the agent's sharing with him unremarkable, so that allegiance follows the ledger the household already keeps on paper?
2. 'my notes have to be changed also.' Are the notes and the agent two records of the same schedule, both requiring update, and who updates each?
3. 'I still prefer having them.' Is the paper record trusted above the agent as P15's prescription is?

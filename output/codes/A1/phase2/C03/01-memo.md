## What this account is about

A 21-year-old software worker set up the agent for his parents intending to 'set it up for them and then leave it.' Instead 'my mother started listening for the reminder and my father also knew that if he does not confirm then I may get a notification. So it became more of a family thing.' The agent's connection to his parents 'made sense because the medicines are not only my responsibility. We all check for each other.' Visibility: 'It did not feel like it was secretly doing something. I knew what information was being shared.' He wants only basics: 'whether they confirmed it, and maybe if they missed it.' Silence: 'Now I would treat no response as something to check'; 'we know earlier that we should check.' Checking became 'more specific'; he asks less for normal doses; 'I still feel responsible because they are my parents. If I get a missed reminder and ask them, that is still care.' A change: 'we don't just trust the old information'; 'we talk about it instead of just ignoring it.' Streak: 'a little funny', then 'something we noticed'; 'you didn't miss it this week'; a break means check whether he 'actually missed the medicine or just forgot to mark it'; shared within the family because 'we already discuss their medicines'; he needs no points himself. Probation: compared with the prescription and known timings, 'did not just leave everything to the app from the first day.' Relief: 'I don't keep watching the clock for their medicine. I can focus on my work.' Condition for advocate: 'When the person using it agrees that the family can be involved.' Failure: 'makes changes without us knowing'; entering everything manually 'would become another job.' Closing: 'it reduces the pressure on the family without removing the family from it.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'my father also knew that if he does not confirm then I may get a notification.' Does the father's knowledge of the notification path change what confirming means, making the press an act toward his son?
2. 'So it became more of a family thing.' Does the caregiver narrate the shift as something that happened to his intention to leave the app alone?
3. 'Now I would treat no response as something to check.' Has the agent converted silence from an unnoticed default into a signal that requires interpretation, and is that a new work for the caregiver?
4. 'When the person using it agrees.' Does the caregiver, like P14, place consent above risk as the condition for the agent's move to family?
5. 'reduces the pressure on the family without removing the family from it.' Is this the account's formula for acceptable allegiance?

## What this account is about

A woman who manages five medicines herself from her prescription and is reminded by unspecified 'people'. The agent became normal: 'When the reminder comes, I check my medicines.' Involvement of others is bounded to a miss: 'only when I have missed something.' The veto: 'The assistant is for reminding, not for forcing me.' Silence: 'the family should check instead of assuming.' Checking: 'Now the reminder comes first. So other people do not have to keep asking me'; asking is care and 'The assistant is just helping them remember to ask.' A prescription change: she would check and change the information, and tell those who help 'so they don't remind me about the old medicine.' Score: small encouragement; shared with those who help, 'not everyone'; a break means 'check why'; 'I don't want the points to become more important than the medicine'; strangers no: 'That is personal. Family is enough.' Probation: she compared names and times with her prescription 'Since I have five medicines, I wanted to make sure nothing was mixed up.' Memory: 'I still know my medicines by seeing them. But now I don't have to remember the exact time all the time.' Advocate: 'When I agree to it. If I say they can help me, then it is okay to tell them when I miss a medicine.' Closing: reminders help 'people who forget sometimes.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'When I agree to it.' Is consent the condition she names for the agent's move to family, rather than risk or repetition?
2. 'The assistant is just helping them remember to ask.' Is the agent's role toward caregivers to remind them to check, mirroring its role toward her?
3. 'the person who helps me.' Does the unnamed helper indicate a household in which help is not fixed to one kin position?
4. 'not for forcing me.' Is the tool role stated as a limit on coercion?

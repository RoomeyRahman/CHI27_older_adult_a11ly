## What this account is about

The phase's clearest refusal of family involvement, from a man who manages his own medicines and is comfortable with phones and computers. The agent 'is mainly helping me because I manage my own medicines. I don't require anyone's help normally.' The announcement was clear and the agent 'doesn't need to interfere beyond that.' He should 'remain in control'; 'The system should remind me, not make medical decisions for me.' Notification of family: 'For me, not necessarily... I would rather handle it myself or contact the doctor.' Checking: 'There was not much checking before... It is not replacing any family routine.' Authority: 'The important person would be the doctor'; the doctor reviews every three months, 'so any change should come from the doctor' and the reminder 'also needs to be updated.' Score: 'I don't think I need a score'; 'the actual medicine is important, not the number'; no need to share 'unless there is some actual problem.' Probation: compare with prescription and packages; 'I would still keep the prescription because that is the proper reference.' Memory: 'My memory is suitable for me'; the system 'makes the routine a little safer.' Closing: 'Primarily me'; 'normal medicine reminders should remain with me'; and a design point for others: 'it should be usable by everyone. Especially people who are not equally educated.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'I don't require anyone's help normally.' Is this the household in which the care network is smallest, so that the agent's plural principal collapses toward one person plus the doctor?
2. 'the reminder system also needs to be updated.' Does he, like P11 and P14, see the agent's knowledge as contingent on an update someone must make after the doctor's review?
3. 'usable by everyone. Especially people who are not equally educated.' Does he speak for others as a reviewer of the design, and does that distance him from the target user as P11 did?

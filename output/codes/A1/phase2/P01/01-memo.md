# P01, Phase 2: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Participants/P01.md`. Older adult, 72, man, businessman; the same participant as Phase 1 P01, interviewed after the two-week deployment. Sons are the family involved. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

**Evidence-grade rule for this phase (CLAUDE.md Section 3.3).** Deployed and used: reminders with name and timing; logbook, streak, heat map; family notification when a dose was unconfirmed; a spoken announcement of whom the agent served; a request-and-grant prompt the older adult could decline. Not built, tested by description only: the risk-graded weakening veto, patterned interpretation of silence, probationary mode as a feature, shared family scores. Several Section 2 and Section 5 probes are phrased as though these were experienced. Every code carries `[lived]` where the extract describes a deployed feature in use, `[elicited]` where it agrees with a probe, `[hypothetical]` where it reasons about a described design, and `[believed-capability]` where the participant attributes to the agent something the build did not do. The agent's autonomy is stated only as the deployed list supports.

## What this account is about

P01 describes the deployment in the same voice as his Phase 1 account: settled, proportionate, with his sons as the family. He noticed the announcement of whom the agent served and liked knowing; he calls it transparent and adds the condition he set in Phase 1, 'I don't want every small thing to go to everybody.' He narrates one shift: an evening with people at home, a forgotten dose, a reminder unanswered, the agent asking whether to involve his son, his allowing it, his son coming to ask. He frames allowing as trust, not lost independence: 'I still take the medicine myself. My son is only helping me remember... We already do this.'

He used the decline: 'If I knew I had taken the medicine and the agent did not know, then I stopped it. There is no reason to worry my sons for something that is not actually a problem.' That is the Phase 1 record-below-the-act condition, lived. Asked about the veto weakening as risk rose, he describes irritation at being asked again and then acceptance; the build had repeat reminders, not a risk-graded veto, so his account is of repetition and is tagged so. On silence he says what he said before the deployment: 'Silence does not always mean I forgot.' On checking, he says the agent 'reduced that checking a little' and that he does not miss the repeated asking but does want his sons to ask 'normally'; the notification 'gives them a reason to check'; care 'Changed, yes. Not reduced.'

The streak: his sons noticed and joked; a break when people were at home disappointed him and his son said start again; sharing is for encouragement, not pressure. Probation: he watched the reminders against his own knowledge for some days; the agent 'is helping my memory, not replacing everything'; 'Sometimes before the reminder comes, I already know it is nearly time.' He answers 'Both, both' to whom the agent works for, and gives the sequence he wants: remind, wait, then tell the son; 'That is how we normally do things anyway.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'We already do this.' Is the request-and-grant prompt read as a formalization of an existing family act, and is that why granting costs him nothing?
2. He stopped the notification when the agent 'did not know' he had taken it. Is the decline used to correct the record rather than to refuse the family?
3. 'Changed, yes. Not reduced.' What changed: who asks first, or how often his sons must ask?
4. 'Sometimes before the reminder comes, I already know it is nearly time.' Is this the habituation the seeded tension predicts, and is it his claim or the probe's?
5. 'That is how we normally do things anyway.' Is the agent's acceptable sequence the household's existing sequence, and does that make the agent's allegiance derivative of the family's arrangement?
6. Irritation at being asked again, then acceptance. Is that the same two-step P05 gave for a daughter's asking in Phase 1?

## Absences

Which son; whether the sons saw the heat map on his phone or were told; what the announcement actually said; any occasion of a false alarm reaching his sons; anything about the logbook as a record he consulted. The interviewer never asks what happened when he declined, or whether his sons knew he had.

## Interactional conditions

Section 2's probes presuppose a weakening veto and an agent that 'understood' silence; Section 5's presuppose probation and retraining. Answers to those are graded as elicited or believed-capability. Section 1's 'giving up your independence or an act of trust' offers the frame he adopts.

# C08, Phase 2: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Caregiver/C08.md`. Caregiver, 25, grandchild and child managing a grandmother's and a mother's medicines (the mother has diabetes); the older adults use button phones. The header carries a real personal name, which does not enter this slot (CLAUDE.md 3.4). Household pairing unfiled; no pairing asserted. Translated from Bangla, edited into single-paragraph turns in quotation marks. No consent-exclusion record is filed. Evidence-grade rule as in the Phase 2 P01 memo; mostly conditional.

## What this account is about

A grandchild who supervises two older adults but 'cannot always be there': 'sometimes I am not at home. Normally I am the one supervising.' The agent 'should support me, not replace me.' Silence: remind again, 'Sometimes they may be doing something or I may be asleep'; then tell him. Change: 'Before I had to remind them myself. Now the system can remind them first. So it reduces some of the work for me'; 'It makes the checking easier. It does not replace the care. We still need to bring the medicine.' Prescriptions are hard to read 'because of handwriting. So I would want a person to confirm it.' Score: 'I don't think the score itself is very important'; basic progress useful to him. Probation: check against the medicine strip and prescription 'because they take several medicines'. Memory: 'I don't have to keep thinking about every medicine time when I am busy.' Direction: 'They can just hear the alarm and take the medicine. I only need to know if there is a problem.' Design: 'An alarm with the medicine name' because 'If it just rings, they may not know which medicine it is'; alarm rather than smartphone because 'they use button phones'; generic names because 'Brand names can be different.' Closing: 'Alarm, medicine name, correct timing. And if they miss it, tell me.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'or I may be asleep.' Is the carer's own unavailability, including sleep, a named cause of silence, so that the agent's escalation covers the carer's lapses as well as the older adults'?
2. 'they use button phones.' Does the older adults' device fix the agent's channel as an alarm the carer configures, so that the older adults never touch the interface?
3. 'if they miss it, tell me.' Is the carer the single recipient of every escalation in this household?

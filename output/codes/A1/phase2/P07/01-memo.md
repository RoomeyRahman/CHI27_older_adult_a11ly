# P07, Phase 2: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Participants/P07.md`. Filed as P07; header 'Female, 71 years, widow', consistent with Phase 1 P07 (son co-resident manages medicines, daughter calls). Own smartphone during deployment. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned. No consent-exclusion record is filed. Variant guide, mostly conditional; evidence-grade rule as in the Phase 2 P01 memo.

## What this account is about

A widow whose son manages her medicines describes the agent as one more channel through which he knows: 'when my son started getting the information if I had not confirmed. Then he would ask me. Sometimes I had taken the medicine but forgot to press the button. So he would ask, and I would say yes yes, I took it.' The 'yes yes' of Phase 1 reappears, now as her answer to a question the agent prompted. Her acceptance of his checking is specific to him: 'Not by my son. I feel safe when he checks. If somebody else was checking everything, then maybe I would not like it.' The phone 'does not know everything'; the family 'understands the situation'. She likes being asked and admits irritation: 'Sometimes I get irritated, yes, because they ask again and again. But I know they are caring for me.'

The streak counted days she did not at first understand; her son said 'keep going' and 'don't break it'; she told him 'I am trying'; 'It became something they could see.' A break would feel bad, and she does not want 'the phone making me frightened'. The score is the family's to see and no one else's: 'My medicine is my matter, even if my family knows.' Trust came through the son's setup and a joint check. Memory: three or four medicines confuse her, so it helps with which is for which time; she became 'a little more aware' of time but 'still like my son to know'. Her closing: 'Reminder for me, information for family when there is a real problem.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'I feel safe when he checks.' Is the acceptability of the agent's notification bound to the recipient, so that the same information to another person would be unwelcome?
2. 'I would say yes yes, I took it.' Is the 'yes yes' now a reply within a loop the agent started, and does it still carry the Phase 1 gentle deflection?
3. 'I told him I am trying.' Does the streak give her something to answer for, and to whom?
4. 'My medicine is my matter, even if my family knows.' Is this the older adult's ownership held alongside family knowledge rather than against it?

## What this account is about

A self-managing man who does not depend on his family for medicine describes the agent as 'another layer of checking when something is going wrong'. The announcement: 'I actually liked that it told me directly... better than something happening in the background.' The shift: home late from the theatre, evening medicines not taken, 'The agent reminded me and I did not respond immediately. Then it asked about involving my family. I allowed it because I knew I was late and there was a possibility I would forget. My son then checked with me.' Not lost independence: 'I have been doing it for eighteen years.' He declined 'once or twice' when he had taken the dose, 'because there was no reason to call my son and make him think I had missed something.' He accepts described pushback with judgment: 'There has to be some judgment in the system.' Silence: 'Silence does not necessarily mean I have forgotten. Sometimes I am simply busy.' Checking: his family checked little before; 'With the agent, they don't have to ask as much'; 'I don't want my medicine routine to become a family project'; 'not every reminder needs to become a conversation'. A prescription change left the agent 'still reminding me based on the previous schedule until the change was updated'; he checked with son then doctor; the agent 'should help keep everyone aligned, but it should not decide'. The streak: his son 'was more interested than I was'; a break while travelling was 'slightly irritated', son said 'start again'; the score must not become 'another performance measure'. Probation: compared with what he knew, tested at home and then outside. Memory: 'Maybe it has reinforced the timing.' Closing: 'Both, but primarily me'; 'I don't want the system to be too aggressive, but I also don't want it to stay silent if there is an actual health risk.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'I allowed it because I knew I was late and there was a possibility I would forget.' Is the grant a self-assessment of risk, so that the older adult is the one deciding when he is unreliable?
2. 'make him think I had missed something.' Is the decline protecting the son's picture of him rather than his own privacy?
3. 'I don't want my medicine routine to become a family project.' Is this the counter-case to the collectivist reading, from a man whose Phase 1 account already kept the family at a distance?
4. 'until the change was updated.' Does the stale-schedule episode make visible that the agent's knowledge is only as current as the person who updates it?

## What this account is about

The announcement told him clearly who the agent was for, so he 'also know why they are getting something.' The shift: busy at home, heard the reminder, deferred, forgot; 'It reminded me again and then there was the option to involve family. I allowed it. Someone at home asked me if I had taken the medicine. Then I remembered.' Trust, not lost independence: 'My family already helps me remember sometimes. So this is just another way.' He declined when he had taken it: 'I don't want my family to think I missed it when I didn't.' Described pushback: 'If I keep saying no and still don't take it, then family should know.' Silence: 'Sometimes I just cannot take the medicine at that exact moment.' Checking: 'Now the agent does that part first'; family reminding is 'helpful. I am used to that'; the notification helps them 'know when I need help.' Prescription: he cannot always read it and needs someone to explain; 'doctor first.' Streak: family asked how many days; a break felt a little bad; 'start again.' Probation: watched for some days, checked medicine information, asked family about unclear phone things. Memory: 'I still forget sometimes. So it has not completely replaced my memory.' Direction: 'One family member is enough. If everyone gets it, then everybody may start asking me.' Balance: 'not too quick to involve family. But if I really forget, then don't stay quiet.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'I also know why they are getting something.' Does the announcement's value for him lie in explaining the family's behaviour, not the agent's?
2. 'One person can ask me and then it is done.' Is direction to one person a way of limiting how many times he must account for himself?
3. 'help them know when I need help.' Does he frame the notification as a request for help rather than a report?

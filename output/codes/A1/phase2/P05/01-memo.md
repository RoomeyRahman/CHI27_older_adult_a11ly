# P05, Phase 2: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Participants/P05.md`. Older adult, 68, woman, retired teacher; the same participant as Phase 1 P05 (husband, working daughter, one tablet). Household smartphone shared or proxy-operated during deployment. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned. No consent-exclusion record is filed. Evidence-grade rule as in the Phase 2 P01 memo.

## What this account is about

The teacher's account after two weeks is the most reflective in the phase so far. Her daughter set it up; she understood the announcement as 'for me, but also my family could know', and reads the whole thing as 'another way of checking' that the family already did. The shift she narrates is the record's error: she did not press after taking, her daughter asked, 'and then we understood that the family side can be useful, but the phone does not know everything.' She wants the system 'Together. For me definitely together. The app can remind me about time, but my daughter knows the prescription. My husband knows my daily routine. The app doesn't know those things unless somebody puts them there.' Trust rose 'because my daughter was checking it. If only the app was deciding things, no.'

Checking changed: her husband now asks 'whether the reminder came' and her daughter 'can also see if something is not confirmed'. Irritation survives: 'Sometimes I get irritated when they ask again and again, but I know why they ask. It is because they are worried. The app doesn't replace that feeling.' The streak: 'why is it counting, I am taking only one medicine', then 'It became a small thing we talked about'; 'I don't need a big celebration for taking one tablet'; it made her more regular 'Maybe a little... you don't want to lose it. But the main reason is still habit.' Trust was tested with her daughter watching whether it was 'actually helping or just making another problem'. Memory: 'a second reminder'; 'a more fixed point'; 'I don't want to become dependent on the phone completely.' Her closing: 'technology should help quietly.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'the phone does not know everything.' Is the family side made useful precisely by the record's error, so that the shift is triggered by the agent being wrong about her?
2. 'unless somebody puts them there.' Does she locate the agent's knowledge in the people who configure it, and its allegiance with them?
3. Her husband asks 'whether the reminder came' rather than 'did you take it'. Has the husband's question moved from her act to the agent's, and what does that do to his checking?
4. 'The app doesn't replace that feeling.' Is the worry behind the family's asking what the agent cannot carry, and is that the irritation's other face?
5. 'help quietly.' Is quiet help the register she wants for an agent, against the 'big medical thing' C01 refused?

## Absences

The daughter's and husband's views; what the announcement said; whether she used the decline; the heat map. The question on declining was not asked directly.

## Interactional conditions

Frames offered: transparent or interfering; independent or together; prove itself; replaced memory; advocate for family. Section 2's 'becoming more urgent' presupposes a weakening veto. Her answers exceed most frames.

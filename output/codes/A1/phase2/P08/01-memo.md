## What this account is about

A short, plain account from a woman who takes her medicine herself and depends on her son to know it. The agent's place is stated in her first answer: 'I take the medicine myself, but my son helps me with the medicine. So if the assistant helps both, then it is useful.' The change is 'little by little': 'Before my son would tell me, this one now, this one later. Now if the reminder comes, I know it is medicine time. My son still helps when needed.' The son need not be told every time: 'Those can come to me. Then I take it myself.' Her refusal to guess is the same as Phase 1: 'I would ask my son. If he is not there, then I would wait and ask someone. Why will I guess.' His asking is care: 'He is my son. Of course it feels like care.' A change must reach him 'Otherwise he may bring the old one.' The score: 'Medicine is not a game.' Trust: her son showed her first and she checked 'whether it was telling the right thing'; now 'when it speaks, I know it is medicine time.' Memory: 'Before I mostly remembered from food time. Now the reminder also tells me. So I don't have to think so much.' She wants Bangla voice, big writing, sun and moon pictures, the packet to compare, and the son told before medicine finishes. 'The reminder helps with remembering.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'this one now, this one later' against 'when it speaks, I know it is medicine time.' Has the son's spoken sequencing been partly taken over by the agent's speech, and does she experience that as relief?
2. 'Why will I guess.' Is the refusal to guess the same rule that keeps the agent out of deciding?
3. 'Otherwise he may bring the old one.' Is visibility of a change a supply-chain need for the son as much as a safety one?
4. 'Medicine is not a game.' Is the counter-case to gamification stated flatly here?

# P02, Phase 2: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/Participants/P02.md`. Older adult, 65, man, village; the same participant as Phase 1 P02, one medicine daily, after the two-week deployment on a household smartphone shared with or operated by a relative (demographics file). Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned. No consent-exclusion record is filed. Evidence-grade rule as stated in the Phase 2 P01 memo: lived, elicited, hypothetical, believed-capability, checked against the deployed list.

## What this account is about

P02's account runs parallel to P01's in structure and in several phrasings; I record that in the run report for the team to check against audio. Its content is his: the announcement felt 'a little strange' then good, 'this is for my medicine'; his family already knows, so involving them 'is not very different'; he does not want every small reminder to go to everyone. The shift he narrates is a late return from outside, a reminder unanswered, the agent asking about family, his allowing it, someone asking him. He used the decline when he had taken the dose but not confirmed it, 'I don't want my family to worry unnecessarily'; asked again, he was irritated then conceded, 'Medicine is important'. Control is the first chance.

Checking has moved: 'Now the phone can do that part'; he does not miss being asked every time; 'They are family'; the notification 'can start a conversation'. The streak: family noticed and said keep going; a break disappointed him a little and they encouraged; 'The medicine is important, the score is not the main thing.' He tested the agent against his routine for days, and his family 'helped with the phone part. Younger people understand these things better', which is the Phase 1 setup delegation in the deployment. Memory: 'maybe it has made the habit stronger.' He answers 'Both'. His conditions repeat Phase 1: vibration rather than speech outside, simple Bangla, family may see and others not, stop trusting on wrong information or too much disturbance.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'this is for my medicine.' Does he hear the announcement as naming the object rather than the principal, and what does that say about how the ledger was understood?
2. 'Someone from the family asked me.' Unnamed, as in Phase 1; is the unassigned reminding of his household preserved by the agent's notification going to 'family'?
3. He declined when he had taken but not confirmed. Is the decline the older adult's tool for keeping the record honest to the act?
4. 'made the habit stronger.' Is this the reinforcement reading, and is it his or the probe's?
5. Vibration outside, speech at home: is the agent's discretion in public the same boundary he drew in Phase 1 between family and community?

## Absences

Who operated the shared phone; what the announcement said; whether the family saw the heat map; any false alarm; the logbook. The interviewer never asks who 'someone from the family' was or what happened after he declined.

## Interactional conditions

As in P01: Section 2 presupposes a weakening veto and an agent that understood silence; Section 5 presupposes probation and retraining. Frames offered: intrusive or transparent; independence or trust; replaced or retrained. Answers graded accordingly.

# Cross-phase affiliation synthesis: Theory Alignment and reflexivity

## Theory Alignment block

**Primary frameworks doing the work.**

1. *Articulation work and invisible work* (Star and Strauss, ledger row committed). Constructs: articulation work, invisible work, the visibility trade-off. It organizes X01, X02, X03, X21: medication work in these households is coordination labour divided across kin, and the agent absorbed one part of it, the first reminder and the caregiver's clock-watching, without absorbing the decision. It predicts that making that labour visible changes it, and the data bear this out in the caregivers' account that checking became "more specific" and in the relocation of the husband's question from the act to the agent.
2. *Interdependence and relational autonomy* (Bennett et al.; Fine and Glendinning; Mackenzie and Stoljar, committed). Constructs: interdependence as access, ceding control as agency. It explains X08 and X25: granting the agent's move to kin was accounted for as trust because nobody hands the tablet, and consent was named as the condition by the two roles alike. It carries the seeded tension of delegated dependence as agency, substantiated by six lived grants.
3. *Contestability by design* (Alfrink et al., committed) with *seamful design* (Ehsan et al.) as the retained rival. Constructs: proposals a family can inspect, contest, and refuse; the seam where allegiance changes. They convert X12, X13, X16, X17 into design knowledge: the decline is a lived contestation, the imagined veto is grounded in a document, and the announcement is the seam shown rather than hidden.
4. *Principal and agent framing; single-principal alignment* (LaCroix; Milli et al.; Hadfield-Menell et al.; Gabriel, committed) as the literature written against. X1 and X5 show a plural principal whose first member varies by household, older adult in most, administering caregiver in two, and whose order is stated by every participant as "for me first, then both".

**Rivals considered.** *The logic of care against the logic of choice* (Mol, ledger row open) was tested against both phases. It fits the reception of checking as care and the refusal to treat medicine as a competition (X09, X24), and it fits the households in which the agent's first principal is the carer. It does not fit the self-managers (X11) or the participants who ground every bound in a document and a doctor (X13, X19), whose reasoning is closer to a logic of correct instruction than to either of Mol's logics. I therefore report Mol as supported for the framing of checking and unsupported as a spine for the whole, and leave the ledger decision to the drafting task. *Goffman's presentation of self* was considered for ceremony and dignity (X16): the "exposed. Not very bad" account is face-work, but one account cannot anchor it, and the politeness reading adds nothing the descriptive account lacks. Not loaded. *Self-determination theory* was considered for streak grief (X24); the data locate the affect in the household rather than in the individual's competence need, and the theory is left in its ledger supporting role. *Behavior-change and habit-loop models* remain the position X24 argues against: the streak's pull was admitted by a few and subordinated by all, and its affect was supplied by kin.

**Work the theory does here.** It predicts that the agent would inherit a division of labour rather than create one (borne out), that ceding control would be accounted for as trust (borne out), that revocation would be exercised where the record misrepresents the person (borne out in the decline), and that a single-principal design would be renegotiated by the household (borne out in the grants and in the caregiver-as-principal households). It explains the one asymmetry it did not predict, consent named as a condition only when a system had to be configured, as the moment invisible arrangement became visible work.

## Methodological commitments and study context

Stated in `phase1/master/05-reflexivity.md` and `phase2/master/05-reflexivity.md` and held here. The two phases are not pooled; the comparison is the analysis. Household pairing is unfiled, so the symmetry is between roles across the corpus, not within households, and the report must say so wherever a caregiver's account and an older adult's account describe the same kind of episode.

## Positionality

I came to the cross-phase step expecting the five practices to appear on both sides, because the codebook was designed to find them, and I guarded against manufacturing the symmetry by leaving the practice column empty in 22 of 40 Phase 2 master codes and 13 of 25 cross codes, and by recording the two asymmetries (consent, the streak) as asymmetries. I expected surveillance and did not find it; I expected streak grief and found it made small by kin; I expected the caregivers to want more information and found them wanting less. The finding that surprised me most, that the agent's first principal in two households is the administering caregiver, came from the caregivers' side only, and the older adults of those households are not in the corpus as speakers.

## Where the data resisted

The data resisted a story in which the agent's allegiance is decided: it was inherited from the household's division in every account and the grants confirmed rather than moved it. The data resisted a clean older-adult and caregiver opposition: both roles used the same proportion vocabulary and the same care-and-irritation sentence. The data resisted the four-dimension vocabulary in one place: participants named consent and explanation as conditions, which the dimensions hold under visibility and ceremony without naming. And the data resisted the paper's interest in shifts of allegiance as events: the one lived shift mechanism, the request-and-grant prompt, was granted by everyone who narrated it, and the only refusals were of the family's involvement altogether, by men who had no care network doing the work.

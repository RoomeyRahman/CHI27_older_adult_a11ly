# Reflexive thematic analysis: care networks and an agent's allegiance

Slot A1 (Claude Code). Companion to `FINAL-CODEBOOK.md`. Every extract is verbatim from a filed transcript, attributed by participant id, phase, and line; the interviewer is never quoted. Evidence grade is stated in the sentence that introduces each Phase 2 extract: lived (done or seen during the two-week deployment), elicited (a position taken under an offered frame), hypothetical (an answer to a conditional), or believed-capability (a behaviour attributed to the agent that the build record excludes).

## 1. Analytic approach

Reflexive thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke), critical realist: accounts are taken seriously and not at face value. Inductive coding first; the affiliation codebook, with its five practices of assignment, contestation, gifting, revocation, and ceremony, as a deductive second pass, applied to Phase 1 for practices among kin and to Phase 2 for the same practices directed at the agent, and left empty where nothing fit.

The dataset is a two-phase qualitative household study in Bangladesh with a two-week prototype deployment between the phases, run between January and June 2026 (`/output/Method.md`; CLAUDE.md Section 3). Phase 1, formative home interviews: 16 older-adult transcripts (P01 to P17, no P14) and 8 caregiver transcripts (C01 to C08). Phase 2, post-deployment interviews: 17 older-adult transcripts (P01 to P17) and 8 caregiver transcripts (C01 to C08). The 25 participants are 17 older adults and 8 family caregivers (`/output/Method.md`). Two source discrepancies are surfaced, not resolved: Method says one caregiver took part only in Phase 1, while the corpus holds eight caregiver transcripts in each phase; and P14 has no Phase 1 transcript, which the user is asked to confirm. Household pairing (which caregiver belongs to which older adult) is unfiled, so no paired episode is asserted anywhere in this analysis. Transcripts were spoken in Bangla and reach the analyst in English translation, lightly cleaned and in several Phase 2 files heavily edited into one paragraph per turn; the translations were not cleaned further, and kinship terms and honorifics are left as filed. Caregiver files carry real names in their headers; none enters this slot, and caregivers are cited by id only.

Sequence: each transcript analysed in full through five passes (exhaustive coding table, trimmed register, sub-themes, themes, adversarial check) before the next; then a master synthesis per phase; then the cross-phase affiliation synthesis over the two masters. Single analyst; no inter-rater reliability is proposed, and the audit trail, memos, reflexivity notes, and the independent second slot stand in its place. No saturation is claimed.

Coding trajectory, with real numbers. Phase 1: 1101 candidate codes across 24 transcripts (10 to 94 per transcript), 509 retained (7 to 30), 66 parked, 40 master codes, 12 sub-themes, 4 themes. Phase 2: 888 candidates across 25 transcripts (23 to 50), 519 retained (12 to 28), 2 parked, 40 master codes, 12 sub-themes, 4 themes. Cross-phase: 25 affiliation codes, 11 sub-themes, 5 themes. Per-unit figures are in the run report and in each `03-code-register.md`.

## 2. Reflexivity and positionality

Full statements: `phase1/master/05-reflexivity.md`, `phase2/master/05-reflexivity.md`, and the Theory Alignment block in `cross-phase/05-reflexivity.md`. In brief: I read as a researcher primed by the project to find negotiation of allegiance, and guarded against manufacturing it by leaving the practice column empty in 22 of 40 Phase 2 master codes and 13 of 25 cross-phase codes, and by recording two asymmetries (consent, the streak) as asymmetries rather than matches. I expected surveillance and found none described; expected streak grief and found it made small by kin; expected caregivers to want more information and found them wanting less. The Phase 2 guide presupposes four mechanisms that were not built, and I graded every answer to those probes as belief or hypothesis before coding.

## 3. Overview of the thematic structure

Phase 1 (RQ1): MT1 medication work partitioned so that taking stays the older adult's act while knowing, reading, and deciding are distributed across kin, pharmacist, and doctor; MT2 lapses located in the household's attention and in supply rather than in memory, with the household's question as the check on an unreliable self-report; MT3 delegation and checking accounted for as prudence, relief, and care, in roles that arrived without decision and are contested only in small refusals; MT4 aids and agents admitted only where the family is not, configured by the young, subordinate to the doctor, and made to speak the medicine's name.

Phase 2 (RQ2, RQ3): T1 the agent enters as one more way of a checking the family already did, and becomes shared when kin check through it; T2 the first reminder moves to the agent while asking remains care, and the record yields to the person; T3 authority stays with doctor, document, and kin: the agent reminds what was decided, knows only what was entered, and must make change visible; T4 the streak as a small family object, trust by testing and through kin, memory reinforced, and the caregiver relieved of the clock.

Cross-phase: X1 allegiance assigned along the household's existing division; X2 checking through the agent received as care, contested as irritation, refused by the self-manager; X3 revocation and the record; X4 ceremony as legibility; X5 roles, conditions, and outcomes carried across the move.

The argument the themes make together: the agent's allegiance was inherited, not decided. Households that already divided reminding, knowing, buying, and deciding across kin seated the agent in the one seat that was empty, the first reminder where nobody was present, and let it become shared through the kin who checked it. Every bound they set on it (below the doctor, told of a miss only, one kin after a first chance, visible in its changes, revocable when its record was wrong) restates a rule they already applied to each other. What the deployment added was small and relational: a kin's question where an agent's message arrived, a streak the family talked about, a caregiver's clock stopped. What it did not add was a decision the family had not already made.

## 4. Phase 1 themes (RQ1, contribution C1)

### 4.1 MT1. Medication work partitioned: taking kept, knowing distributed

The older adult keeps the act and gives up the name. "Taking is easy. Knowing which medicine is little difficult" (P08, Phase 1, L101). The partition is stated as a matter of competence, not deficit: what P08 can do is the thing that matters, and what she cannot do is held by a son who reads. The same division appears with three holders at once: "He keeps the prescription also. He knows what doctor said. My wife knows which tablet I take morning and night. I know by looking at the strip." (P04, Phase 1, L51). Recognition by strip and place is the older adult's own knowledge, and it breaks when a packet changes: "If you put three strips in front of me I can tell which one I take because I see it every day. But the name I cannot say." (P03, Phase 1, L71). Custody of the record moved to a child after a loss: "I had some papers before but I lost one also. Then son said you don't keep these papers give it to me. Now he keeps." (P07, Phase 1, L31). Above the kin sit the pharmacist known for years and the doctor as the gate: "It should help us remember and understand. Doctor is still doctor." (C01, Phase 1, L401). Peers are granted talk and never authority: "But I don't blindly follow somebody's medicine advice. For my own medicine, I discuss with my family." (P01, Phase 1, L171).

Boundary and variation: three men who manage alone (P11, P15, P17) and a couple who share written notes (P16) show the division is assigned, not given by age; "Yes, my memory is very sharp." (P17, Phase 1, L27) is the competence claim at its plainest.

So what. This is the human affiliation baseline the agent later enters: assignment among kin is by kind of knowledge, and it is what makes the agent's later seat, time only, intelligible. It complicates the adherence-tool assumption of a single user who knows their regimen; here nobody holds the whole regimen and the household does. RQ1; C1; two-source rule cleared across all 24 units.

### 4.2 MT2. Lapses in attention and supply; the question as the check

Doses are missed when attention is elsewhere, never because someone forgot as a trait: "She knows she has to take it but the time goes away." (C01, Phase 1, L89). Supply fails as often as attention: "I miss only when the stock of my medicines run out or if they are unavailable to buy near me." (P15, Phase 1, L34). The older adult's own report is unreliable and a question restores it either way: "Even if I know myself, when someone asks you remember. Sometimes I say yes I have taken, then I realise no no I haven't." (P05, Phase 1, L59). The 'yes yes' that answers a repeated asking does not secure the act: "She says it again and again. I tell her yes yes I know. Then sometimes I still forget the bag." (P05, Phase 1, L123). Recovery is rule-governed: "I come home and ask my wife. If I am not sure I call son. I don't take double. No no. That is not good." (P06, Phase 1, L75). Reminding is nobody's job and everybody's: "Sometimes everyone reminds everyone. Like did you take your medicine, did you take yours. It is kind of normal in the family." (C02, Phase 1, L105). It fails when everyone is busy at once: "If one person forgets somebody else usually remembers. But sometimes everyone is busy. That is when the problem happens." (C03, Phase 1, L197).

Boundary and variation: reception of checking runs from safety, "He is my son. I feel safe when he checks." (P07, Phase 1, L175), through irritation reframed as help, "Sometimes I get irritated also because she asks so much. But later I think okay she is helping." (P05, Phase 1, L223), to a refusal of constant watching, "But don't make it like somebody is watching me all the time." (P04, Phase 1, L167).

So what. The failure the households describe is a distribution of attention, and the repair is a question from whoever is present. That is why checking is care as well as oversight in this corpus: the question does work the person cannot do alone, and the older adults say so. It reframes a missed dose from an individual memory failure to a household attention event, which is the premise on which an agent's decision about whom to tell becomes the relevant design question. RQ1; C1 and the empirical ground of C3.

### 4.3 MT3. Delegation as prudence and relief; roles that just happened; small refusals

Older adults account for delegation as relief: "Yes. It makes it easy. I don't have to think. She knows what doctor gave me." (P07, Phase 1, L287). Caregivers describe roles nobody decided: "It mostly just happened. I started helping because they needed it, and then it became my routine. [...] We don't really sit and decide these things formally." (C01, Phase 1, L357). The caregiver's problem is knowledge in absence: "If I can call I call. If I cannot then I just have to trust that she will take it. There is no proper way to know." (C01, Phase 1, L109). Contestation is small and inside the delegation: "Yes one time packet was different. I said this is not mine. Then my son said same medicine only packet is different. Then I took it" (P08, Phase 1, L121).

So what. Gifting (delegation as prudence) and the absence of ceremony (roles that happened) are the human baseline for the agent's later grant episodes and for the unannounced way sharing occurred in Phase 2. The seeded tension of delegated dependence as agency is present already among humans, in the older adults' own accounting. RQ1; C1.

### 4.4 MT4. Aids and agents admitted where the family is not, configured by the young, below the doctor, speaking the name

Aids set by others fail by adding a task or by not saying what they are for: "I can set one but then maybe she is doing something and ignores it. Or sometimes the alarm is there but she doesn't know which medicine it is for." (C04, Phase 1, L89). The agent's place is stated before it exists: "At home wife already tells me. Outside there is no one. So phone can tell there." (P06, Phase 1, L267). Setup goes to the young: "Son or wife. They should put the medicine. I can only take when it tells me. If I have to change the setting myself then no." (P06, Phase 1, L263), and trust follows the setter: "If son puts everything then I will trust it more. I know he checked it." (P04, Phase 1, L179). The record is subordinate to the act: "Taking medicine is the main thing. Pressing button should be secondary." (P05, Phase 1, L211). Escalation is bounded: "First reminder then maybe after some time again. If still not taken then tell the family member." (C02, Phase 1, L265). Checking must be visible to the older adult: "I don't think it should secretly check. They should know. It is for helping them." (C01, Phase 1, L325). The agent is below the doctor and confined to information: "But again it should not act like a doctor. It should tell you information and then you can ask doctor." (C02, Phase 1, L245).

So what. The four dimensions the paper names were stated by participants before the build: direction (to one kin, after a delay), visibility (no secret checking), revocability (an aid ignored), and the tool role below the doctor. These are elicited conditions, not use; Phase 2 tests them. RQ3 formative; C3.

## 5. Phase 2 themes (RQ2 and RQ3, contributions C1 to C3)

### 5.1 T1. One more way of a checking the family already did; shared when kin checked through it

Lived: the announcement was strange, then clarifying. "When it said it is working for me, I understood it better. I knew, okay, this reminder is for me. And when it said it is also working with my family, I understood that also." (P10, Phase 2, L13). For another older adult it explained the family's behaviour: "if it is working with my family, then I also know why they are getting something." (P13, Phase 2, L12). Its cost was named once and sized small: "if it says something in front of everyone, then I feel a little, what do you call it, exposed. Not very bad." (P10, Phase 2, L17).

Lived: six older adults narrate the request-and-grant prompt as an episode. "The agent reminded me first. I did not respond because I thought I would take it after finishing the news. Then later it asked for permission to involve the family, something like that. I agreed. After that my son came and asked me if I had taken it. So that was the shift." (P10, Phase 2, L21). The grant is accounted for as trust because the son only checks: "My son is not putting the tablet in my mouth. He is just checking. So it felt more like trust." (P10, Phase 2, L25). Another grant is a self-assessment of risk: "I allowed it because I knew I was late and there was a possibility I would forget. My son then checked with me." (P11, Phase 2, L22). The grant's product is a kin's question that produced the dose: "I allowed it. My husband asked me if I had taken the medicine. Then I remembered and took it." (P12, Phase 2, L20).

Lived, from the other side: the sharing happened through kin's use. "Earlier I was thinking I will set it up for them and then leave it. But after using it, my mother started listening for the reminder and my father also knew that if he does not confirm then I may get a notification. So it became more of a family thing." (C03, Phase 2, L29). An older adult dates it the same way: "it became part of the routine because my daughter was also checking it." (P03, Phase 2, L29). These are not filed pairs; they are the same mechanism from two roles.

Hypothetical and elicited: proportion and direction restate Phase 1. "Son is enough. Wife may also know because she is at home, but no need to alert everybody." (P04, Phase 2, L69). Direction acquired a reason: "One family member is enough. If everyone gets it, then everybody may start asking me. One person can ask me and then it is done." (P13, Phase 2, L130). Three participants named consent where others named risk: "When the person using it agrees. In our case my mother is okay with me knowing if she misses a medicine because I already help her. But I don't think it should automatically tell the whole family everything." (C04, Phase 2, L189). In two households the agent's first principal is the administering caregiver: "Mostly it reminded me, because I am the one managing their medicines. If I am busy doing household work, the reminder helps me remember that it is time." (C05, Phase 2, L17).

Boundary and variation: the older adults of the caregiver-as-principal households are absent as speakers; their acceptance is reported, "They are okay with it. For them, the main thing is getting the medicine at the right time." (C05, Phase 2, L89). The self-managers (T2) are the counter-case.

So what. Assignment of the agent's allegiance followed the household's prior division and was rarely decided; the grants all went to the kin who already checked. Against single-principal alignment, the principal here is plural and ordered, and its first member varies by household. The consent condition is the Affiliation Ledger's premise stated by participants without the term, and the only practice with no human antecedent in the corpus. RQ2; C1 (typology of assignment and gifting), C2 (direction as a dimension); lived for the grants, hypothetical for the conditions.

### 5.2 T2. The first reminder moves to the agent; asking remains care; the record yields to the person

Lived: one part of checking moved. "Before, someone at home might ask, did you take your medicine. Now the agent does that part first." (P13, Phase 2, L52). The husband's question changed its object: "Earlier my husband would ask directly, did you take it. Now sometimes he asks whether the reminder came. My daughter can also see if something is not confirmed." (P05, Phase 2, L67). From the caregiver side the night-time loop was removed: "Now the app does the first reminder. So we don't have to keep asking her at night, did you take it, did you take it. If she misses it, then we check." (C04, Phase 2, L85), and checking narrowed: "We ask less for the normal doses because the reminder is there. But if there is a missed confirmation, then we check. So the checking is more specific now." (C03, Phase 2, L85).

Elicited: asking is care and irritation in one sentence, and the agent cannot carry the worry. "Sometimes I get irritated when they ask again and again, but I know why they ask. It is because they are worried. The app doesn't replace that feeling. It just gives another reminder." (P05, Phase 2, L75). A son's question is a different act from a message: "If my son comes and says, baba, did you take it, that is different from a phone message. It feels more caring." (P10, Phase 2, L57). Care changed and did not shrink: "It has reduced the small checking, but it has not reduced the care. My family still looks after me. They buy the medicine, they make sure I have water, they ask about my breathing. The agent is doing one small part." (P10, Phase 2, L61). The caregivers claim the same channel as care: "If I get a missed reminder and ask them, that is still care. The app is just helping us do it." (C03, Phase 2, L93), and distinguish work from care: "it reduces some work, but it doesn't mean we stop caring." (C02, Phase 2, L60).

Lived: silence is busyness, deferral, a forgotten press. "If I am working, I may hear it later or not hear it. Sometimes I take the medicine and then forget to press the button. So no response does not always mean I did not take it." (P06, Phase 2, L51). The record's error triggered one shift: "I had actually taken it, I just forgot the phone part. Then we understood that the family side can be useful, but the phone does not know everything." (P05, Phase 2, L19). For caregivers, no confirmation became a signal to check earlier rather than something to assume: "Earlier we might just think he forgot to answer. Now I would treat no response as something to check. Maybe he is busy, maybe he did not hear it, maybe something else happened." (C03, Phase 2, L61). The decline was used on the same ground: "Sometimes I knew I had taken the medicine but I had not responded to the agent. Then I stopped the family alert because there was no need to worry them." (P10, Phase 2, L31). The failure participants fear is a worried kin: "if it tells my son I missed medicine when I actually took it every day, then he will worry for no reason." (P06, Phase 2, L63).

Believed-capability: eight participants answered probes that presupposed a weakening veto. "I thought, I already told you, why are you asking again. But then I understood why it was doing that. If I keep saying no and actually I have not taken the medicine, then it becomes dangerous." (P10, Phase 2, L35). The mechanism was not built; this is reasoning about a described design and grounds no use claim.

Boundary and variation: the self-managers. "I just don't need someone checking every time. If my son asks occasionally, that's fine. But I don't want my medicine routine to become a family project." (P11, Phase 2, L62). "It is mainly helping me because I manage my own medicines. I don't require anyone's help normally." (P17, Phase 2, L14). Where no kin does medication work, there is no allegiance to negotiate, and the plural principal collapses to the older adult and the doctor.

So what. Checking through the agent inherited the household's reception of checking, care and irritation together, and both roles say what moved was work, not care. Contestation of the agent, lived, is the decline; contestation of the family's involvement, elicited, is the self-manager's, and it marks the boundary of the collectivist reading. The record's subordination to the act, a Phase 1 rule, governed the agent's record too, and the one lived shift triggered by the record's error shows why. RQ2; C1 (contestation, revocation), C3 (the case against silent inference from silence); the pushback material is belief only.

### 5.3 T3. Authority with doctor, document, and kin; the agent knows only what was entered; change must be visible

Elicited: the phone lacks situated knowledge. "She knows my daily routine. Phone does not know if I have eaten or if I am outside. So her reminder is different." (P04, Phase 2, L93). "The phone can tell time. My son knows the medicine name. My wife knows when I ate and when I came home. So all three, you can say, have different information." (P06, Phase 2, L23).

Hypothetical: the doctor or the document decides. "The prescription is the authority for me. The system should follow that, not make its own decision." (P15, Phase 2, L43). "I would not take it directly. I would ask my son. If he is not there, then I would wait and ask someone. Why will I guess." (P08, Phase 2, L49). The agent may report and a person confirms: "The agent can tell me that something changed, but a person should confirm it." (C02, Phase 2, L64). Revocation is a standing right grounded in a document: "If something is wrong, I should be able to stop it. The assistant is for reminding, not for forcing me." (P14, Phase 2, L45).

Lived: the agent was stale after a change until someone updated it. "The agent was still reminding me based on the previous schedule until the change was updated. That was a little confusing. So I checked with my son and then with the doctor." (P11, Phase 2, L70). "The agent reminded me about the old timing, but then my son checked with the doctor. So in that case it helped us remember what we were doing before, but it did not decide for us." (P10, Phase 2, L65). A caregiver found mismatches and checked them: "there were times when something did not match what we knew from the prescription. Then I would check it instead of just accepting it." (C03, Phase 2, L49).

Elicited and hypothetical: a change must reach everyone, with its reason. "Because everybody needs to know if the routine changes. Otherwise my wife may remind me about the old one." (P04, Phase 2, L105). "If something changes, he should know. Otherwise he may bring the old one." (P08, Phase 2, L113). Caregivers add explanation as a condition on warnings: "it should explain what the problem is. Just giving a big warning without telling me why would not be very helpful." (C01, Phase 2, L36).

So what. The tool role is the only role granted without condition in either phase; coach and advocate are conditional. The agent's knowledge has a human updater, and ceremony of change is maintenance work with concrete failure modes named by participants. Design implication, traceable to P04 and P08: a change in schedule is a household event, not a settings event, and the agent must announce what changed and why to everyone who reminds or buys. RQ3; C2 (visibility, revocability, ceremony), C3.

### 5.4 T4. The streak as a small family object; trust by testing and through kin; memory reinforced; the caregiver relieved of the clock

Lived: the streak became a family topic. "My daughter was happy. She said, see, you are doing it regularly. My husband also asked how many days it had been. It became a small thing we talked about." (P05, Phase 2, L95). "My son would say, oh, baba is doing better than all of us." (P10, Phase 2, L71). The son was often more interested than the older adult: "My son was more interested than I was. He asked how long I had kept the streak." (P11, Phase 2, L80). For a caregiver who administers, the streak was her own: "It showed that I had been keeping up with everything. With five or six medicines across the day, seeing that regularity is nice." (C06, Phase 2, L103).

Lived: streak grief, made small by kin. "I was a little upset because I had kept it for many days. But my son said, okay, it happened, take the next one properly. So after that I did not worry too much. If I was alone, maybe I would have felt more annoyed. With family, they made it into a small thing." (P10, Phase 2, L79). The counter-case is carried by more participants than the pull: "First I would see if I really missed medicine. Sometimes I may take it but not press something. So don't worry just because the number changed." (P08, Phase 2, L137); "Medicine is not a game. If it gives some encouragement, okay." (P08, Phase 2, L149); "I don't take medicine because of the points. I take it because the doctor prescribed it." (P15, Phase 2, L103). Sharing is bounded: "I would not want the score to become another thing they keep checking all the time though." (P10, Phase 2, L83), and personal beyond the family: "My medicine is my matter, even if my family knows." (P07, Phase 2, L115). Shared family scores were not built; this is condition, not use.

Lived: trust was tested against what the household already held. "I also checked whether the reminders were coming at the right time when I was at home. Then I tested it when I was outside, because that is where my routine usually becomes less regular." (P11, Phase 2, L102). "I compared it with my alarm and my normal routine." (P12, Phase 2, L104). "For some days I still kept the old written information nearby." (C04, Phase 2, L149). And through kin: "If my son puts the medicine and time, then I know it is from my prescription. If the phone itself starts deciding things, no, I would not trust that." (P06, Phase 2, L27). Probationary mode was not built; the testing was participant-initiated during a real deployment.

Elicited: memory reinforced, not replaced. "I still remember the medicine. I have been taking it for many years. It is more like a second memory." (P06, Phase 2, L147). The honest centre admits both: "Now I still know the usual time, but I also wait for the reminder sometimes. So maybe I am depending on it more. But at the same time, the repeated timing has made me remember the dose time better. Sometimes I know it is almost time before the phone says anything." (P10, Phase 2, L97). The counter-case: "But I still forget sometimes. So it has not completely replaced my memory." (P13, Phase 2, L108).

Lived: the caregiver's clock stopped. "I don't keep watching the clock for their medicine. I can focus on my work. If something needs my attention, then I get the notification." (C03, Phase 2, L165). "Before, if I was working and the time was passing, I might suddenly think, did my mother take her medicine. Now I know there is a reminder." (C04, Phase 2, L165). The formula for acceptable allegiance, from both roles: "it reduces the pressure on the family without removing the family from it." (C03, Phase 2, L205); "Now the phone does the first reminder. Family still checks when needed. So it helps without taking over." (P04, Phase 2, L265).

So what. The outcomes are relational and small. Gamification worked as a relational trigger and not as a behavioural lever: the household supplied the affect, and the counter-case (check why; not a game) outnumbers the pull. Trust through self-verification is claimable as lived; memory restored rather than replaced is the participants' own account against Phase 1's competence claims, with P13 as the counter-case. The articulation work the agent absorbed is the caregiver's as much as the older adult's, and it was named by its removal; whether the agent absorbed the decision as well as the labour is answered no in every account. RQ3; C3.

## 6. Relations between themes

T1 and T3 pull together: the agent's allegiance follows the household's division (T1) because the household ranks knowledge and authority above the agent (T3). T2 and T4 pull against each other at one point: T2 says asking is care and the agent cannot replace the feeling behind it, while T4 says caregivers were relieved of the worry that produced the asking; the resolution participants give is that what was relieved was timekeeping, not care, and the caregivers' own distinction between work and care is the seam. Phase 1 MT2 and Phase 2 T2 pull together across phases: the household's question as the check on an unreliable self-report is exactly what the agent's notification produced. Phase 1 MT4 and Phase 2 T3 are the same conditions before and after use, and the deployment confirmed them without adding a new one except consent and explanation.

## 6a. Cross-phase affiliation synthesis

Full table and matrix: `cross-phase/01-symmetry.md`, `cross-phase/03-matrix.md`. The phases are not pooled; where they disagree both readings stand.

**Assignment.** Among humans: reminding by presence, knowledge by kind, record in a child's custody, buying with kin, the doctor as gate, bounded repetition then one kin. Toward the agent: the first reminder taken where kin are absent; kin as configurers and updaters; the agent seated as a third holder of time only; told of a miss, a long silence, or finishing stock; one kin by knowledge, presence, or role; six lived grants. What changed: assignment was inherited rather than decided, direction acquired a reason (so that not everyone asks), and consent was named as a condition where Phase 1 had only arrangements that happened. Caregivers assumed a place inside the normal circle that older adults granted more conditionally.

**Contestation.** Among humans: irritation, scolding, the 'yes yes', small refusals, competence claimed against aids. Toward the agent: irritation at repeats and at a described pushback, held inside care; a caregiver's check of mismatched information; the self-managers' refusal of a family project. What changed: contestation moved to the agent's asking and was resolved the same way, understood rather than refused; the agent's information became a new object, contested once; the self-manager's refusal is of the network, not the agent.

**Gifting.** Among humans: delegation as prudence and relief; checking as safety. Toward the agent: granting the request as trust because nobody hands the tablet; wanting kin to know; a granddaughter welcoming the check on herself. What changed: from standing arrangement to granted request, same grammar; the notification rather than the score became the occasion for talk.

**Revocation.** Among humans: aids left unused, alarms that do not compel, technology declined. Toward the agent: the decline used by six older adults to spare kin worry and correct the record; a veto imagined by both roles and grounded in the prescription or the notes. What changed: revocation became a built act whose ground shifted from the older adult's standing to the kin's picture; every veto beyond the decline is imagined.

**Ceremony.** Among humans: roles that just happened; checking to be visible to the older adult. Toward the agent: the announcement heard, strange then clarifying, exposing a little, to be given to the older adult first; a change that must reach everyone with its reason; a warning that must explain. What changed: ceremony became speech and maintenance; the taking of a role stayed unannounced in both phases.

No practice is absent toward the agent. Two are thin (revocation lived only as the decline; contestation of information lived once), and two are asymmetries with no clean human antecedent (consent; the streak). The three roles read off the symmetry: tool granted without condition; coach accepted as small encouragement from kin and refused as pressure; advocate accepted under three triggers after a first chance, toward one kin, with the older adult's agreement, and refused by the self-managers.

## 7. Discussion

Against the theory ledger. Articulation work ([106]) organizes the finding that the agent absorbed one part of coordination labour, the first reminder and the caregiver's clock, without absorbing the decision; its prediction that making invisible labour visible changes it is borne out in checking becoming "more specific" and in the husband's question moving from the act to the agent. Interdependence and relational autonomy ([13], [30], [72]) carry the grants: ceding the agent's move to kin was accounted for as trust by every older adult who narrated it, which substantiates the seeded tension of delegated dependence as agency, with the self-managers as the bounded counter-case. Contestability by design ([6], [7]) converts the decline and the document-grounded veto into design knowledge, and seamful design ([28]) reads the announcement as a seam shown rather than hidden. Single-principal alignment ([62], [79], [38], [32]) is what the data write against: the principal is plural, ordered "for me first, then both", and its first member is the administering caregiver in two households. The logic of care ([80]) was tested and is supported for the reception of checking and unsupported as a spine, because the self-managers and the document-grounded vetoes reason by correct instruction rather than by either of Mol's logics; the ledger row stays open with that result recorded. Circumspection ([11], [57]) is confirmed for trust through self-verification. Behavior-change models ([31], [108]) remain the position the streak findings argue against.

What this extends. Caregiver-dashboard work has studied systems that report; here the agent had to decide whom to tell and when, and the households answered with a proportion (a miss, a long silence, stock) and a direction (one kin, after the older adult's first chance) that they already applied to each other. The design principle in the participants' words is C03's: reduce the pressure on the family without removing the family from it. The mechanisms that make a change of role visible and negotiable are, on this evidence, the spoken announcement, a request the older adult can grant or decline, and an explicit ritual of change that reaches everyone who reminds or buys; the ones that make it dignity-preserving are the first chance, the person's check before the record's verdict, and a score the family may see and nobody outside.

What generalizes and what does not. The claims are about Bangladesh and these 25 participants. What travels is the contrast: an agent designed for a single principal entering a household in which medication work is already collective is renegotiated into a plural principal along the household's own division. Households, shared budgets, and classrooms face the same question when an agent with initiative serves several people; the answer these households gave, inheritance of allegiance plus proportion, direction, and consent, is a hypothesis for those settings, not a finding about them.

Limitations, stated flat. One cultural setting and one language, translated and edited. Snowball recruitment through the team's networks. A two-week deployment. Interviews as the only Phase 2 record; no logs are filed, so nothing here is corroborated by system data. Four designed mechanisms tested by description only, and a guide that presupposed them, so that eight participants narrated as lived a pushback the build record excludes. Household pairing unfiled, so older adults' and caregivers' accounts are second-hand about each other and never paired. Several Phase 2 transcripts are heavily edited and two pairs (P01 and P02, P12 and P13) are structurally near-identical; the user should verify them against audio. In two households the older adults are absent as speakers.

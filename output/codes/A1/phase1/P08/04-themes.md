# P08, Phase 1: Passes C, D, and E

## Pass C: sub-themes

Over the 30 retained codes only.

### ST1. Supply and knowledge held by the son, who knows what the doctor gave
- Constituent codes: `Son brings, daughter-in-law if absent`, `Shows old packet, son knows`, `Son knows what doctor gave`, `Tells them when finishing`, `Same medicine rebought unseen`, `Son is the channel to the doctor`, `Son breaks the half tablet`
- Definition: the son buys, knows what the doctor gave, rebuys the same medicine without a visit, takes her to the doctor and retells, carries her symptoms to the doctor, and breaks the half tablet; his wife substitutes for errands; she specifies by showing the old packet and tells them when a strip is finishing.
- Excludes: `'We forget to buy' [tr]`, the failure of this supply, placed with the stock failures.
- RQ1.

### ST2. Taking as hers, from a box, at food time, whatever is brought
- Constituent codes: `'Taking is easy. Knowing is difficult' [tr]`, `'Whatever they bring, that I take' [tr]`, `Box she keeps; son keeps some`, `Son arranges; she takes when he is out`, `Food time as cue`
- Definition: she takes the medicine herself from a box she keeps, at food time, when the son is out as well as when he has set it out, and describes her part as taking whatever is brought; the partition between taking and knowing is stated seven times.
- Excludes: `Knows packet by sight`, which is recognition rather than the act.
- RQ1.

### ST3. Recognition by packet and refusal of the unfamiliar until confirmed
- Constituent codes: `Knows packet by sight`, `New packet: ask first`, `'I said this is not mine' [tr]`, `'Why will I guess. Better ask' [tr]`, `'No need also. They tell me' [tr]`
- Definition: she recognizes her medicine by packet and purpose, cannot read the small letters and does not need to because she is told, asks rather than guesses, and refused a changed packet until the son confirmed it.
- Excludes: `Would learn if told slowly`, her willingness to know more, placed with reception of help.
- RQ1.

### ST4. The daughter-in-law as witness and catcher
- Constituent codes: `Daughter-in-law asks if taken`, `Daughter-in-law caught wrong tablet`, `Asks daughter-in-law if she saw`
- Definition: the co-resident daughter-in-law asks whether she has taken it, once caught her about to take the night tablet in the morning, and is asked whether she saw her take a dose when she cannot remember.
- Excludes: `Son brings, daughter-in-law if absent`, the daughter-in-law's errand role, placed with supply.
- RQ1.

### ST5. Deference on rules and doses, with discretion kept over pain medicine
- Constituent codes: `'They told me not to do that' [tr]`, `Stops pain medicine, keeps pressure`, `Neighbours' talk, ask son`
- Definition: the no-double rule is held as the family's instruction, the pressure medicine is kept because the son relays the doctor's word, the dose is never changed, neighbours' talk is passed to the son; her own discretion operates over the pain medicine alone.
- Excludes: nothing retained.
- RQ1.

### ST6. Stock failures shared and reproached
- Constituent codes: `'We forget to buy' [tr]`, `'you should have told before' [tr]`, `'still somebody should bring' [tr]`
- Definition: medicine has run out before anyone bought more, narrated in the plural; the son's reproach attributes the failure to her telling, and she reports it without resentment; any aid to knowing would leave the supply problem untouched.
- Excludes: `Tells them when finishing`, her stock method when it works.
- RQ1; bears on RQ3 through the supply limit on any reminder design.

### ST7. Help as children's due and technology as the son's tool
- Constituent codes: `'They are my children' [tr]`, `'Maybe for my son' [tr]`, `Would learn if told slowly`, `'I cannot use application' [tr]`
- Definition: asking her children for medicine costs her nothing because they are her children; an app is rejected outright and then admitted only as a simple spoken Bangla reminder the son would set; the technology's user is the son, who also forgets; she would learn more if told slowly.
- Excludes: nothing retained.
- RQ1 for the moral account; RQ3 for the technology.

Sub-theme count: 7.

## Pass D: themes

### T1. Getting and knowing the medicine as the son's work, taking as hers
- Organizing concept: the participant partitions the work so that supply and knowledge are the son's and the act is hers, and her competence lies in recognizing her packet and refusing what she does not recognize until told.
- Constituent sub-themes: `Supply and knowledge held by the son, who knows what the doctor gave`, `Taking as hers, from a box, at food time, whatever is brought`, `Recognition by packet and refusal of the unfamiliar until confirmed`
- Definition and boundary: 'Taking is easy. Knowing which medicine is little difficult' is the theme's statement, and 'I said this is not mine' its sharpest act. The boundary is that 'whatever they bring, that I take' and the refusal of a changed packet sit together: she takes what is brought and refuses what she does not recognize, and the two are one disposition, not a contradiction.
- Extracts: L101, L29, L121, L381.

### T2. The household checks her act and her supply, and the failures are shared
- Organizing concept: two co-resident kin check different things, the daughter-in-law the act and the son the supply and the rules, and when checking fails the failure is narrated as the household's.
- Constituent sub-themes: `The daughter-in-law as witness and catcher`, `Deference on rules and doses, with discretion kept over pain medicine`, `Stock failures shared and reproached`
- Definition and boundary: the daughter-in-law is a witness to the act ('if she saw me take'), which the son, who is out, cannot be. The boundary is the son's reproach, which attributes the stock failure to her telling; she reports it without resentment and later says 'Sometimes he forgets also. He has work', so the attribution is not settled in the account.
- Extracts: L309, L173, L153, L369.

### T3. Help asked as a child's due, with any technology imagined for the son
- Organizing concept: help from one's children is a due rather than a favour, and a technology for this household would be the son's tool because the work it would touch is his.
- Constituent sub-themes: `Help as children's due and technology as the son's tool`
- Definition and boundary: single sub-theme theme, kept apart because the moral account and the technology reading share one ground, that the work is the children's, and because the evidence grade is mixed (reported for the moral account, hypothetical for the technology). Boundary: her willingness to learn more if told slowly cuts against reading her as uninterested in knowing.
- Extracts: L269, L261, L253, L273.

Theme count: 3.

Counter-readings. T1: the alternative is that the partition is not hers but the household's arrangement imposed on a woman with little schooling, and 'whatever they bring' is the voice of that arrangement. The account cannot exclude it; but the refusal of the changed packet and the rule 'Why will I guess' are hers, and a purely imposed arrangement would not produce a refusal. The reading holds with the boundary noted. T2: the alternative is that the daughter-in-law's checking is oversight of a mother-in-law by a younger woman, and its reception is unspoken because it cannot be spoken. I cannot exclude this; she never evaluates the daughter-in-law's checking, only reports it, and the matrix notes say so. T3: the alternative is that 'Maybe for my son' is a polite deflection of a technology she has just rejected. It is possible; but she gives a reason ('I am used to this way. My son brings and tells me. I take') that is a description of the work, not a deflection.

Parked code closest to the line: `Age on son's authority`. It stays parked because a single opening line cannot carry a code, but it is the account's first index of where knowledge about her sits and should be remembered at master level if the pattern recurs.

Change forced by Pass E: none structural. What I hold least firmly is the reception of the daughter-in-law's checking, which is unspoken.

# C02, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Caregiver/C02.md`. Caregiver, woman, a software engineer living with her father, mother, and grandfather; she does 'most of the things' for their medicines and appointments. Her mother is diabetic and takes two medicines per phase of the day; her grandfather three or four at different times. The transcript carries her real name at L5 (CLAUDE.md Section 3.4); it does not enter this slot and L5 is never extracted. Household pairing to an older-adult participant is not filed. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned, unpunctuated in places; not repaired. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

## What this account is about

A working daughter who keeps track without checking daily: 'I don't sit and check every medicine every day... We just know the routine. If there is a new medicine then I check it properly.' The household's reminding is mutual and unassigned: 'If I am not there my father or my mother reminds the other person. Sometimes everyone reminds everyone. Like did you take your medicine, did you take yours. It is kind of normal in the family.' Its record is memory and a fixed place, with things written down only when a sickness brings many medicines.

The episode at the centre is a wedding: her diabetic mother forgot her medicine, and after midnight her health worsened. 'We were all busy... The medicine was not in front of her. So she forgot... Nobody reminded her at that time.' The medicine was with them; the failure was that in a household where everyone reminds everyone, nobody did. A second episode is a medicine that caused her mother a deficiency until another doctor changed it.

For an agent she is precise about direction and volume. The reminder is for the parents; she wants to be told only a real miss, after a repeat, not ten notifications a day, not an automatic call, because at work 'I cannot keep checking my phone every few minutes.' She would prefer to manage the settings but wants both to see it, and when asked whether her parents should be told of a change she says: 'there should be some update. Like medicine changed. So I know also. But I don't want to control everything they do. They are adults.' And then: 'Helping. Not controlling. That is important.' Trust in medicines comes from people you know, 'Compared to something online where you don't know who is saying it', and she would check an app's reading against the pharmacist or doctor, never follow it blindly; 'Doctor is different.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'Everyone reminds everyone.' Is the reminder a property of the household rather than a role, and does the wedding show what happens when a property has no owner?
2. 'Nobody reminded her at that time.' Is the lapse the mother's or the household's, and does she narrate it as either?
3. 'I don't sit and check every medicine every day.' Is checking calibrated to novelty, and what does that make of a system that checks every dose?
4. 'They are adults.' Who is the adult being protected: her parents from her, or her from the role of controller?
5. 'Compared to something online where you don't know who is saying it.' Is the agent an online voice, and where does that put its trust?
6. 'It should only tell me when there is actually a problem.' Is the caregiver's attention the scarce resource the agent must ration?

# P01, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P01.md`. Older adult, 72, man, businessman in a small city, lives with sons. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned at source. No consent-exclusion record is filed for this participant. 

## What this account is about

This is an account of a man who still runs a business, still holds his own schedule in his head, and describes his sons as the people he 'discusses' with rather than the people who look after him. The register is confident and short. Answers are brief, often one sentence, and the interviewer works to open them. The medication routine is anchored spatially (one place at home) and habitually (long familiarity with the medicines), and the participant frames both as reasons he needs no method. What he identifies as the difficulty is not memory as a faculty but the routine breaking: travel, guests, conversation, business. Forgetting appears in his own words, but always as a consequence of social and spatial disruption.

The family enters in three distinct registers, and I want to keep them apart. First, as consultation: the son is the person with whom a decision about a delayed dose is taken jointly. Second, as procurement: the elder son brings medicines from the pharmacy, so P01 does not go himself. Third, as a reminding presence that 'may' ask whether he has taken it, described as helpful but incidental. The pharmacist appears as a known person, part of the local network, who interprets prescriptions. A laughing club supplies talk about medicine but is explicitly denied decision authority: 'I don't blindly follow'.

What surprised me: the readiness with which he accepts a family notification, with a proportionality condition ('not for every small thing'), and the way he states that technology must not displace the family, unprompted in substance even though the interviewer leads on the form. The account is a settled public version of the household, and it reads clean. I treat that cleanness as itself the thing to read.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. Why does he name 'my sons', 'my son', 'my elder son', and 'my eldest son' at different points? Is the consultative role held by one son and the procurement role by another, or is the English flattening a single referent?
2. When he says 'We talk and then take the decision', who is 'we', and what does joint decision-making do for his standing that a solo decision would not?
3. Is 'I don't like to do everything secretly or alone' a statement about preference, or a statement about what kind of older adult he is, addressed to a researcher?
4. Why is the laughing club granted talk but denied authority? What distinguishes family from community as sources of medicine knowledge here?
5. What does 'the pharmacist knows us' do: is familiarity the licence for asking, so that asking does not cost him?
6. He accepts a son notification with a proportionality condition. What counts as a 'small thing', and who decides?
7. Why does he separate carrying the medicine (not a problem) from remembering the timing (the problem)? Is he defending competence in the parts he controls?
8. The son sets the phone up; he then uses it 'myself'. Is setup a delegable stage that leaves use intact as his own?

## Absences

He never mentions his wife or any woman in the household. He never names a doctor and says he has no regular provider. He does not describe any episode of a missed dose in specifics, despite being asked for the last one. Nothing about cost, about buying, or about stock, except that the son brings it. Nothing about side effects. The interviewer never asks how the sons came to hold their roles, whether anyone checks quietly, or whether he has ever refused help.

## Interactional conditions

The interviewer supplies the reminder-app premise from L185 onward and leads on several forms: 'So basically you depend on your memory' (L49), 'Your family is kind of a backup?' (L85), 'So you wouldn't want the technology to replace your family involvement?' (L221). Answers after these are tagged `[elicited]`. All talk about an app, reading prescriptions, or notifications is about a described idea, not a used one, and is tagged `[hypothetical]`. The forgetting frame at L61 to L71 is partly supplied by the 'what feels difficult' question, but the content (routine disruption) is his.

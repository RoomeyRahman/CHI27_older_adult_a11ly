# P05, Phase 1: Passes C, D, and E

## Pass C: sub-themes

Over the 30 retained codes only.

### ST1. A single tablet internalized as habit, with lapses of timing rather than omission
- Constituent codes: `Habit from years`, `Lateness, not omission`, `Prescription internalized`, `Keeps a strip in her bag`, `Routine change the hardest`
- Definition: one tablet learned from the prescription over years and now held in the head, with the only admitted error being lateness when the routine changes, and a strip now carried in the bag against that.
- Excludes: `Says yes, then realises no`, which concerns the report of a dose rather than its timing.
- RQ1.

### ST2. Taking kept to herself; reminding and tracking given to family
- Constituent codes: `Takes it herself`, `Reminded, not handed`, `Dependence partitioned`, `'better somebody knows' [tr]`
- Definition: the act of taking is hers and nobody hands her the tablet; papers, buying, appointments, and changes are the family's, and at this age someone else knowing is presented as the right arrangement.
- Excludes: `Daughter files medical papers`, the specific holding, placed with the daughter's roles.
- RQ1.

### ST3. The daughter as keeper of papers, stock, and changes
- Constituent codes: `Daughter files medical papers`, `Daughter tracks stock and buys`, `Daughter particular about changes`, `'Two people hear better' [tr]`
- Definition: the daughter files the medical papers, counts and buys the strips unasked, guards the rule that nothing changes without the doctor, and accompanies her to hear what one person might miss.
- Excludes: `Children worry and check`, which is about the checking of her, not the holding of the work.
- RQ1.

### ST4. Checking that restores memory, received with irritation and then as help
- Constituent codes: `Says yes, then realises no`, `'yes yes I know' [tr]`, `Children worry and check`, `Irritated, then reads it as help`
- Definition: the husband's and daughter's asking restores a memory her own report missed; the daughter's repeated instruction is met with an affirmative that does not secure the act; the checking is explained as children's worry and received first with irritation, then as help.
- Excludes: `Reminded, not handed`, which is the boundary of the family's act rather than its reception.
- RQ1.

### ST5. Peer talk noted, family checks suitability
- Constituent codes: `Peer talk listened, not acted`, `Family checks suitability`, `Peer's tablet cues hers`
- Definition: former colleagues and neighbours supply talk and an occasional indirect cue; the family, knowing her history and doctor, checks whether anything heard is suitable for her.
- Excludes: nothing retained.
- RQ1.

### ST6. Reading competence retained, apps declined as unneeded
- Constituent codes: `Reads; small print with glasses`, `Button phone by choice`
- Definition: she reads as a teacher would, bounded by print size and by doctors' handwriting, keeps a button phone as sufficient, and locates the barrier in apps rather than in herself.
- Excludes: nothing retained; two-code sub-theme defended because it carries her relation to text and to devices, which no other sub-theme does, and which conditions everything in ST7.
- RQ1.

### ST7. Conditions on an agent: configured by the daughter, secondary to the medicine, never deciding alone
- Constituent codes: `Setup by another, one thing on screen`, `Trust via daughter's check`, `App must not decide alone`, `Daughter told only for a real miss`, `'Pressing button should be secondary' [tr]`, `Two reminders better than none`, `Details to daughter`, `'Otherwise family is easier' [tr]`
- Definition: an agent set up by the daughter and trusted because she checked it, showing one thing, telling the daughter only for a real miss, never deciding medicine by itself, treating a missed press as secondary to the act, tolerated alongside the husband's reminder, sending details to the daughter, and preferred to the family only if simpler than the family. All hypothetical.
- Excludes: `Button phone by choice`, reported practice.
- RQ3, with RQ2 bearing through who configures, who is told, and the refusal of autonomous decision.

Sub-theme count: 7.

## Pass D: themes

### T1. Taking kept as her own act while papers, stock, changes, and outside advice pass through the daughter
- Organizing concept: the participant partitions the work so that the act and the knowledge of her one tablet are hers, while everything that tracks, records, procures, or evaluates passes through the daughter, and she presents this partition as prudent rather than as dependence.
- Constituent sub-themes: `A single tablet internalized as habit, with lapses of timing rather than omission`, `Taking kept to herself; reminding and tracking given to family`, `The daughter as keeper of papers, stock, and changes`, `Peer talk noted, family checks suitability`
- Definition and boundary: the partition is stated in her own words at L63 and L227. The boundary is the husband, who reminds and sometimes buys and accompanies but does not hold the file; and the participant's reserve, that she would manage the tablet alone and call the daughter only on a change.
- Extracts: L35, L63, L43, L103.

### T2. Checking received as children's worry and as help, after irritation, because her own report of a dose can be wrong
- Organizing concept: the family's checking works because it catches a report she herself gets wrong, and she receives it in two steps, irritation and then gratitude, explaining it as what children do now rather than as a judgment on her.
- Constituent sub-themes: `Checking that restores memory, received with irritation and then as help`
- Definition and boundary: single sub-theme theme, kept apart because it is the account's answer to care-or-oversight and the answer is two-sided in one participant's words. Boundary: the irritation is stated once and reframed in the same turn; whether the reframing is a settled evaluation or a repair for the interviewer is not decidable from the transcript.
- Extracts: L59, L123, L127, L223.

### T3. An agent acceptable only as configured by the daughter and secondary to both the medicine and the family
- Organizing concept: any tool enters through the daughter's hands and is judged by whether it is simpler than the family, keeps the act above the record, and leaves decision with people.
- Constituent sub-themes: `Reading competence retained, apps declined as unneeded`, `Conditions on an agent: configured by the daughter, secondary to the medicine, never deciding alone`
- Definition and boundary: ST6 is the reported baseline (reads well, button phone by choice, one demo not taken up); ST7 is the hypothetical answer. The boundary is evidence grade, and the theme joins them as before and after of one disposition. 'Otherwise family is easier' is the theme's centre: technology competes with family as a means and must be simpler to win.
- Extracts: L155, L187, L211, L251.

Theme count: 3.

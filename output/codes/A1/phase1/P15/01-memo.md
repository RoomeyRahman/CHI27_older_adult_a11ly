# P15, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P15.md`. Older adult, 80, man; eight to ten medicines across three slots; follows the written prescription; uses a phone. The shortest transcript in the phase (about 550 words), from the medicine-and-technology script, with short answers. No consent-exclusion record is filed. Deployment device is not recorded for this participant (blocker 8 in CLAUDE.md).

## What this account is about

An eighty-year-old on a heavy regimen who describes one method and holds to it: 'I have been following the written prescription for a very long time.' He takes his medicine by himself, needs nobody's help except a doctor's when severely ill, and misses a dose only when the stock runs out or the medicine is unavailable nearby; asked whether he ever misses from memory, 'No, never.' What he does with technology is telephone the doctor, and the one thing he uses it for is to negotiate: 'I use technology to ask the doctor to reduce the redundant medicines to make my life easier.' He does not go beyond the prescription and has no view on reminder tools.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. The prescription is his method and his limit ('I do not go beyond it'). Is the written paper doing what other participants' families do?
2. He asks the doctor to reduce medicines. Is this the only account in the phase in which the older adult negotiates the regimen itself rather than its timing?
3. 'Nobody's helps is required. Sometimes doctors help is required if needed.' Is family absent from the account, or absent from the script's questions?
4. Missing only when stock runs out: is supply, here as for P08, the failure point that a reminder cannot reach?

# P17, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P17.md`. Older adult, 65, man, retired from a senior post at a bank (the employer is named in the transcript at L15 and is a re-identifying detail; it does not enter this slot); eight medicines twice daily; phones and computers. Short transcript (about 600 words), medicine-and-technology script. The filed transcript carries two stray '[cite: 3]' markers at L51 and L53, an artifact of its preparation, surfaced in the run report. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

## What this account is about

A man who manages eight medicines by recognizing the packages and states his method as a point of pride: 'my memory is very sharp'; 'I solely rely upon my memory'; 'I have never missed any dosages. It's always my first priority.' He does exercise one discretion, skipping medicines 'not required for that particular illness' when unwell. He uses phones and computers and understands interfaces. Asked whether technology would help him follow prescriptions, he answers about the country: 'I don't think any software based systems have started in our country. We use the hard copies.' He endorses a reminder system as fitting 'the age of technology' and asks that any tool be built with the less educated in mind, 'so that it is usable by all.' The doctor reviews every three months and the medicines are effective.

The account is a competence performance, and it is also the only one in the phase to speak for a public, the country and the less educated, when asked about tools.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'My memory is very sharp.' Said after the interviewer's 'you mostly rely on your memory': is it a report or a claim of standing?
2. He skips medicines when ill by his own judgment. Is this the professional's discretion, and how does it sit beside 'I do not go beyond' in P15?
3. 'In our country': is the question about himself answered about a nation because the tool is for others?
4. 'Usable by all', 'those who are not equally educated': is his endorsement of tools a civic position rather than a personal want?

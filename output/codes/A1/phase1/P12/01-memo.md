# P12, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P12.md`. Older adult, 71, woman, lives with her family including her husband; one medicine daily at midnight; a smartphone with a standing alarm. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned, unpunctuated in places; not repaired. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

## What this account is about

The only participant so far who already uses a technology for medicine: a phone alarm set once and ringing every night at twelve. The account is therefore about what an alarm does and does not do. It reminds; it does not compel. 'Sometimes I am busy and I hear it but then I say okay after some time. Then sometimes I forget also.' And it is outranked by a person: 'sometimes mobile alarm is there but I ignore it. If family says take your medicine then I take it.' The family's asking works where the alarm does not, and she does not analyse why; she reports it.

The family here is unassigned ('Sometimes my husband. Sometimes someone else at home asks') and its asking has a double function she states exactly: 'If I already took it then I say yes. If not then I take it.' The question is a check that resolves either way.

Her lapses are travel and absorption: outside, talking, watching something, the alarm rings and she defers. When travelling she carries the medicine and forgets where in the bag it is. Prescriptions she cannot read; the pharmacy reads them. The doctor is for problems with the medicine, the pharmacist for understanding it.

For a future agent she wants a voice rather than text, because 'There are many notifications. Voice I hear', a repeat after some minutes but 'not every one minute. That will be irritating', one family member told if she does not take, and she does not feel monitored by that 'if it is family. Family already asks.'

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. The alarm is ignored and the family voice obeyed. What does the family's asking carry that a sound does not: a witness, an obligation, or simply a second cue at a different moment?
2. 'If I already took it then I say yes. If not then I take it.' Is the family's question valued precisely because it does not presume a lapse?
3. She fears being irritated by frequent repeats and wants voice over text because of notification clutter. Is the agent competing for attention in a device already full of demands?
4. 'Not really if it is family. Family already asks.' Is monitoring redefined by the identity of the monitor rather than by the act?
5. Battery: she keeps it charged. Is the alarm's reliability her responsibility in a way a person's is not?

## Absences

The husband appears once as one of the askers. No children are mentioned. No episode is specified. Nothing about who set the alarm originally or who buys the medicine. The interviewer never asks how the alarm came to be set or why she ignores it.

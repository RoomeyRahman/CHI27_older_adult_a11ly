## What this account is about

A grandson who supervises: 'If I don't supervise them directly, sometimes they miss doses.' His instruments are the instructions on the strips and the prescription, with no notes. Misses are frequent and located in his absence: 'especially when I'm not at home or when I'm busy with work. Sometimes even when I'm asleep, a dose gets missed.' The older adults use button phones and are 'not very interested in smartphones'. What he wants is an alarm that states the name of the medicine, a way to confirm by phone whether a medicine is correct instead of making appointments, and a system that uses generic rather than brand names, 'because brand names often differ.'

This is the account in which the caregiver's presence is stated most plainly as the condition of the dose, and the only one to name a specific technical obstacle in the medicine supply itself, brand names.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'If I don't supervise them directly.' Is supervision the caregiver's word for the check, and does 'directly' mark presence as the mechanism?
2. 'Sometimes even when I'm asleep.' Is the dose bound to his waking as well as his presence, and what does that make of a night dose?
3. Button phones, 'not very interested'. Is the older adults' non-adoption described as disinterest rather than incapacity, and whose device would the agent live on?
4. Brand names differ. Is the confusion he names a supply-side problem that no reminder reaches, as with P06's brand change and P08's stock?

# P10, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P10.md`. Older adult, 78, man, urban Dhaka, lives with family including a son and daughter-in-law. Diabetes and a breathing difficulty; two medicines, three doses. Both a button phone and a smartphone the family set up. A morning walking group at the park. The transcript is short (about 1,000 words), structured by protocol headings, with an interviewer's observation note at the top and stage directions; it is more heavily edited than most, with full sentences and dashes, and the interviewer's closing turn is evaluative ('It is wonderful how your family and community walking group keep an eye out for you'). Translated from Bangla. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

## What this account is about

An urban older man whose routine is anchored in prayer, the morning walk, and the tray beside his chair: 'I sit right here after meals, so I see the tray and remember.' The family sets the water out and reminds him 'if I look busy', and he insists, as several others do, 'But I take the medicine myself.' The walking group also asks, 'Uncle, did you take your tablet today?', so the check comes from two directions, and his summary is 'between my own habit and the people around me, we manage.'

Where the mind slips is timing, and he attributes it to occupation and age together: relatives, the evening news, tiredness from breathing trouble, 'Old age, perhaps.' What he would accept is a message reminder, so that he does not have to rely purely on memory, kept simple for old eyes and ears.

The closing turn, prompted by the interviewer's evaluative remark, supplies a cultural thesis: 'in our culture and society, we don't live in isolation. My family ensures my medicine stock is full, and my community friends keep my spirits high. Technology should complement that.' This is the account's most quotable line and its most led. I retain it with the leading recorded.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'we manage' is plural. Is the unit that manages the household plus the walking group, and does he see himself as one member of it rather than its patient?
2. 'reminds me if I look busy': the cue for the family's check is his visible state, as for P02. Is checking by sight of the person a general form here?
3. The walking group asks him directly ('Uncle'), unlike P04's indirect peer cue. Does the address term make the group's check kin-like?
4. 'Old age, perhaps.' He offers age as a cause and hedges it. How much of the deficit frame is his and how much the interviewer's 'what causes you to forget'?
5. 'Technology should complement that.' Complement what: the family's stock-keeping, the friends' spirits, or the not living in isolation? Is the agent being offered a place inside an existing arrangement rather than a role?
6. The observation note records no written aids and two phones. He confirms the first and describes the second as family-configured. Whose device is the smartphone?

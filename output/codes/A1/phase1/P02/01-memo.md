# P02, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P02.md`. Older adult, 65, man, village, lives with family, one daily medicine, feature phone with family smartphones in the house. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned at source. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

## What this account is about

A man with one medicine a day, a morning walking and exercise group, and a household of mixed ages, giving an account of a routine so light that the interviewer has to work to find difficulty in it. What he offers as difficulty is disruption: the walk runs long, guests arrive, he travels and forgets to carry the tablet. He repeatedly distinguishes two things the interviewer runs together: being reminded, which he wants, and being given the medicine, which he refuses ('I can take it myself. I just need to remember'). That distinction organizes the account. He will accept help with timing and refuses help with the act.

The household reminding is diffuse. No one is responsible; 'whoever is around' asks, 'we help each other', and the asking is triggered by seeing him busy. He receives it as family helping. He draws an information boundary at the family: the community walks with him, asks after the sick, helps with errands, but 'community people don't need to know my medicine details', and a reminder outside should not speak loudly. Roles beyond the family are tidy: the pharmacy reads prescriptions, the doctor handles medical problems, the family handles daily things; 'Everybody has their own role.'

What surprised me was L481: asked whether the app should replace family reminders, he inverted the premise. The family is also busy, so the phone reminds him when they cannot. The app is a backstop for the family, not a replacement of it and not a replacement of him. That reading is his, unprompted in content, and it should not be lost under the interviewer's 'support rather than replace' summary a turn later.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. Why does he insist twice that he takes the medicine himself before the interviewer has suggested otherwise (L13, L45)? What is being pre-empted?
2. When he says 'we take it to the pharmacy' and 'we show it to them', who is the 'we', and does the plural mark a joint act or a household habit of speech?
3. The family asks 'especially if they see that I am busy'. Is checking triggered by a visible cue rather than by schedule, and what does that make the checker's role?
4. Why is loudness outside a concern when the household knowing is not? What defines the circle inside which medicine is speakable?
5. He wants a delay before family is alerted, 'some time before the family gets worried'. Is the concern his standing, or the family's peace?
6. 'Don't make us feel like we don't understand anything.' Who is 'us', and what past experience of being talked down to is this drawn from?
7. He accepts one trusted member changing settings but 'not everybody'. What distinguishes trust to know from trust to change?
8. Does the light regimen (one tablet) make the account atypical, or does it isolate the mechanisms more cleanly because nothing else is going on?

## Absences

He never names which family members live with him, nor any specific person: no son, daughter, wife, or grandchild is mentioned. No episode of a missed dose is specified. Nothing about the medicine's purpose or condition. Nothing about cost or buying. The interviewer never asks who buys the medicine, how the household's shared smartphone is actually operated, or whether anyone has ever checked without his knowing.

## Interactional conditions

Heavy leading throughout, often closed summaries the participant confirms ('So it has become part of your routine?', 'So too many steps are frustrating?', 'So technology should support the family rather than replace them?'). Confirmations are tagged `[elicited]` and the substance is taken only where he adds to the question. All app and AI talk from L235 onward is `[hypothetical]`. The three-role summary at L435 was the interviewer's; his addition, 'Everybody has their own role', is his.

# P11, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P11.md`. Older adult, 65, man, retired a year ago, now works in theatre, Dhaka; five or six medicines at night plus three around breakfast, a regimen held for about eighteen years; smartphone and laptop. Short transcript (under 900 words), a different interviewer script from P01 to P10 (medicine and technology usage), fully in English register with some interviewer summaries. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

## What this account is about

A man who manages a heavy regimen alone and says so at once: 'I take my medicines by myself, and I do not use any technology or any devices.' The method is eighteen years of habit under a doctor who adjusts the doses every two months. What he misses, rarely, he misses when home late or out of the city for theatre work, where 'it is tough to follow strict medicine regimen like I do if I were in Dhaka.'

The account's interest is in how he handles the offer of an aid. He does not refuse it; he displaces it. 'I do not need those yet.' 'Those who are a bit more aged and have memory issues, I think they would benefit.' 'Not very appropriate for me yet.' 'I am very strong and active even though I am 65. Not everyone is like that.' The need is real, elsewhere, and later. He does describe a voice gadget at home that announces the time and imagines it announcing medicine, and he does take his son's help when on-screen suggestions confuse him. What he wants from technology is not memory but money: the medicines are expensive and he would like to be told where they are discounted.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'Yet' appears twice. Is the aid deferred to a future self, and does that make acceptance easier than refusal?
2. 'Those who are a bit more aged and have memory issues.' Is need displaced onto an other so that his own competence stands?
3. The son helps with on-screen suggestions. Is that the same delegation of setup that older, less technical participants describe, in a technically fluent man?
4. Cost is the problem he volunteers twice. Does the medicine-technology framing of the interview miss what a reminder could never solve for him?
5. The doctor 'mitigates my doses' every two months. Is the doctor the only other party in this man's medication work?

## Absences

No family beyond the son's technical help; no spouse, no household. No one reminds him and he does not say anyone knows his regimen. No prescription reading, no pharmacy. The interviewer never asks who else lives with him or whether anyone checks.

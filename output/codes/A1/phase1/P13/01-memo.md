# P13, Phase 1: familiarization memo

Source: `Supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/Participants/P13.md`. Older adult, 66, woman, lives with her family; one regular medicine; smartphone in ordinary use. Translated from Bangla, lightly cleaned, unpunctuated in places; not repaired. No consent-exclusion record is filed.

## What this account is about

A woman with one medicine and, by her own account, no method: 'I just remember. Sometimes.' She has never tried an alarm because 'I thought I will remember it myself. But sometimes I don't.' Remembering is restored by whoever asks at home; the family knows she takes medicine and sometimes reminds her. The episode she gives is the sharpest in the phase on shared forgetting: getting ready to go out, many things happening, she forgot, and 'family was there. But everybody was busy. Nobody remembered either.' The lapse belonged to a household in which every member was occupied at once.

The family's authority runs on closeness and experience. 'Family has more experience with these things.' She would believe the family over one bad report, and the doctor over the family. 'They are close to me. They know me. So I listen.' And the family holds her medicine's name where she does not: 'Sometimes I don't remember the medicine name but family remembers. They say this is the one you take.' She knows the packet.

For an agent she wants a voice she cannot miss, repeated once or twice, with a message to the family who 'already remind me', kept to one simple screen, and a travel mode that reminds her to carry the medicine before leaving.

## Analytic noticings, as questions

1. 'I thought I will remember it myself.' Is the absence of any method a decision about who she is, and does the 'Sometimes' that follows concede it?
2. 'Nobody remembered either.' If the household's attention is the method, what does a lapse mean when everyone is busy: a failure of hers, or of the household's distribution of attention?
3. 'They are close to me. They know me.' Is authority over medicine grounded in intimacy here rather than in knowledge of the prescription?
4. The family remembers her medicine's name. Is the name a piece of knowledge the household holds collectively, with her holding the packet?
5. 'Something that I cannot miss.' Is the agent being asked to be more insistent than the family, or to be present when the family is busy?

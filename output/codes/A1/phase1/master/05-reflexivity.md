# Phase 1 master synthesis: reflexivity

## Methodological commitments (stated once here, held everywhere)

Reflexive thematic analysis, not codebook or coding-reliability analysis; codes are analytic resources and were expected to change, and did. A single analyst is standard, and I do not propose inter-rater reliability or a second coder; the audit trail in the 24 unit folders and this master folder, the memos, the development logs, and the recorded alternative readings are the quality work. Themes are generated, not found. Each theme has a central organizing concept stated in one sentence. Prevalence is not importance, and the two-source rule governs what may be a master theme; frequency never trimmed a code. Latent coding was preferred where the data permit. No saturation is claimed; the sample is adequate to RQ1 because it reaches sixteen older adults across urban and rural settings and eight caregivers across kin positions, and inadequate to RQ2 and RQ3, which Phase 2 answers. Inductive first, with the affiliation practices marked in Pass B where they fit and left blank where they did not.

## Study context

Phase 1 is the formative half of a two-phase qualitative household study in Bangladesh (CLAUDE.md Section 3.1). The filed corpus holds sixteen older-adult transcripts (P01 to P17, no P14; CLAUDE.md Section 3.5 blocker 11 asks whether that session happened) and eight caregiver transcripts (C01 to C08). Demographics are read from `Supplementary/Interviews/demographics/` and never from memory. Sessions were in Bangla, mostly in homes; the transcripts reach me in English translation, lightly cleaned at source, and several of the shorter ones (P09, P10, C05 to C08) are more heavily edited, with full sentences, exclamation marks, and dashes. I did not clean further, and where an interpretation rested on one English word I marked it provisional. Kinship terms and address terms (Ma, Amma, baba, Chachi, Uncle) were kept as filed because the relation is the analysis. Two interviewer scripts were used: the eight-module in-situ protocol for most older adults and the caregiver branch for caregivers, and a shorter medicine-and-technology script for P11, P15, P16, and P17; the second asks almost nothing about the household, and the thinness of those four accounts on the care network is partly the instrument's. Caregiver transcripts still carry real names (CLAUDE.md Section 3.4); none entered this slot. The household pairing of caregivers to older adults is not filed (blocker 2), and I asserted no pairing.

## Positionality

The research team comes from computing rather than gerontology or nursing, conducted the interviews in Bangla, and built the prototype the same households later used. I read the transcripts as an analyst working with that team's material. Three consequences shaped what I could see. First, the interviews were held in homes, often with a family member within earshot, and an older adult's account of a son's checking is an account given in the son's house; I could not recover what was left unsaid and I noted where standing might constrain what was sayable (P07 above all). Second, the team built the agent, so any positive account of a described tool is partly courtesy to its builders; I graded every design statement as hypothetical and retained only those that exceeded the question that produced them. Third, the interviewers and I are, in age and role, closer to the adult children than to the older adults; the caregiver's account is the fluent one and the easier to find persuasive, and I checked the older adult against caregiver distribution per theme to see where the analysis was following fluency. It was not: the older-adult-only codes are the competence claims and the moral accounts, which are the least fluent material in the corpus and the most important.

## Assumptions brought in

I began with the expectation, written into the study's framing, that families would treat monitoring as intrusion and that older adults would defend independence against it. I also began with the affiliation practices in view and with the seeded tensions of CLAUDE.md Section 9.3 named as things to protect. I held both before coding, and I record here that the first expectation was not met.

## Where the data resisted

The monitoring-as-intrusion expectation did not survive the older adults' accounts. Asked whether being told about would feel like being watched, three older adults rejected the frame on the ground of kinship, and one asked for it outright ("somebody should keep an eye"). What I found instead was a distinction the participants drew themselves and I had not brought: between being told about a miss, which they accepted with conditions of delay and proportion, and being watched all the time, which one refused and several bounded. That distinction became M22 and it is the finding I did not expect.

A second resistance was the false yes. I had expected the check to be a reminder. Three older adults and one caregiver described it instead as a correction of the older adult's own report, which they know to be unreliable ("Sometimes I say yes yes I took it and then later I realise maybe I didn't"). That made the check a different kind of act, and M05 is built around it.

A third was the caregiver's problem. I had expected caregivers to describe the parents' forgetting as their difficulty. They described their own not knowing ("There is no proper way to know"), and their design want followed from it. M24 records that.

## Decisions and their alternatives

Three decisions were consequential. First, I kept the reception of checking as two codes (comfort and scolding) rather than one, so that whether checking is care or oversight is answered case by case; the alternative, one code with a range, would have settled by merging what the data leave open. Second, I treated all Phase 1 design talk as hypothetical and parked the modality preferences, retaining only conditions that exceeded the question; the alternative, retaining the preferences as formative requirements, would have made MT4 a theme about the instrument. Third, I retained the self-reports of decline (P07, P10) inside M05 and the competence claims inside M02 as two disconfirming poles of one finding, rather than resolving toward the framing's preferred reading; the alternative would have smoothed a real tension.

On the weight of hypothetical judgments: they carry S10 and S11 in full, and MT4 is labelled as elicited design reasoning. No claim of use is made from Phase 1, and none could be.

## Effects on the account

This analysis is well placed to see how the work is divided and morally accounted for, because both sides were asked and both answered at length. It is poorly placed to see what happens inside a specific household, because pairing is unfiled; to see the caregiver's checking as the older adult receives it in that household; to see the caregiver's own literacy limits except where they volunteered them; and to see anything about the four participants interviewed with the shorter script. It is also the analysis of translated talk, and the phrases I lean on most ("the time goes away", "Outside there is no one", "Why take chance") reach me through a translator's choices; their recurrence across accounts and across translations is what gives me confidence in the mechanisms they name, not the words.

## Division of labour

This analysis was produced by an AI assistant (Claude Code, slot A1) running the project's thematic-analysis skill, in one invocation, on the filed transcripts. Every extract was read from the transcript and verified mechanically against it. The judgments about what to retain, how to group, and what to concede are the assistant's, made under the persona and rules the skill specifies, and are recorded so that the research team can disagree with them line by line.

**Appendix A: Integrated In-Situ Interview and Artifact Elicitation Protocol**

_Note: This protocol merges semi-structured interviewing with situated observation and artifact walkthroughs. Sessions are conducted in the natural environment where medication management occurs, prompting participants to physically demonstrate their routines rather than relying solely on retrospective accounts._

**Phase 1: Environment & Artifact Observation (Pre-Interview)**

- **Observational Focus:** Discreetly survey the spatial arrangement of the care environment before formally initiating questions. Document medication storage locations (e.g., bedside, kitchen table, locked cabinets), environmental conditions (noise, lighting, physical accessibility), and the presence of analog memory aids (sticky notes, calendars, physical pillboxes).

**Phase 2: Background & Caregiver Context**

- Can you tell me a bit about yourself and your role in managing medications for the elderly individual?
- _[Probe: Could you describe a typical morning in your household when it is time to handle medications?]_

- How do you or your family member usually take medicines each day?
- _[Probe: Walk me through the exact sequence, from picking up the bottle to swallowing the pill.]_

- How many medications are currently prescribed?
- _[Probe: Are these all taken at the same time, or spread out across different routines throughout the day?]_

**Phase 3: Situated Walkthrough & Current Practices**

- **Observational Focus:** Request a physical walkthrough of a typical medication administration routine. Observe the physical handling of bottles, label reading, and utilization of existing tools. Document improvised workarounds (e.g., color-coded caps, specific cup placements, spatial organization).
- How do you keep track of which medicines to take and when?
- _[Probe: Can you show me the exact physical spot or device where you check this information?]_

- What tools or methods do you use (e.g., written notes, phone alarms, pillboxes)?
- _[Probe: Could you physically demonstrate exactly how you set this up today?]_

- What aspects of this routine work well, and what feels difficult or friction-heavy?
- _[Probe: If you could wave a magic wand and instantly fix one part of this daily routine, what would it be?]_

**Phase 4: Scenario Probing: Challenges & Barriers**

- **Observational Focus:** Ground these inquiries in specific, recent episodic memories rather than hypothetical scenarios. Identify how environmental or social factors disrupt the established routine.
- Have you or your family member ever missed a dose?
- _[Probe: Think back to the very last time this happened—what was going on around you at that exact moment?]_

- Are there times when managing medications becomes particularly challenging?
- _[Probe: Walk me through what happened the last time you were away from home, had guests over, or experienced a sudden change in schedule.]_

**Phase 5: Technology Elicitation & Device Demonstration**

- **Observational Focus:** Request a live, interactive demonstration of any digital tools currently in use. Observe physical interactions, UI navigation hesitations, screen brightness configurations, and general device ergonomics.
- Do you use any digital technology (mobile phones, apps, smart alarms) to assist with medications?
- _[Probe: Open the app or tool you use most often; can you show me the last entry you made?]_

- What specific kind of phone do you or your family member use as a primary device?
- _[Probe: Who originally configured or set up this phone for daily use?]_

- What do you find easy or difficult about using technology for this specific purpose?
- _[Probe: Can you show me the specific step on this device that usually takes the longest or causes the most frustration?]_

**Phase 6: Accessibility, Literacy, & Interaction Needs**

- **Observational Focus:** Assess visual, cognitive, and physical interaction loads organically while the participant handles a sample prescription or reads their current medication labels during the walkthrough phase.
- Are there any difficulties related to reading labels, understanding medical instructions, or physically operating the devices?
- _[Probe: Looking at this current prescription bottle, can you read the dosage instructions to me just as you normally would?]_

- What specific features or changes would make it easier for you to rely on technology for medication reminders or tracking?
- _[Probe: Do you find yourself relying more on large text, audio prompts, color codes, or something else entirely?]_

**Phase 7: Care Network & Social Context Mapping**

- **Observational Focus:** Map the household dynamics. Note who else is present in the domestic space, how physical proximity influences caregiving, and whether shared or secondary devices mediate the care network.
- Who else in the household or family helps you or your family member with medications?
- _[Probe: If you are unavailable for a day, who takes over, and exactly how do you hand off the required information to them?]_

- How do you coordinate and communicate with healthcare providers or pharmacies regarding these medicines?
- _[Probe: Show me where and how you store the doctor's contact number or the latest paper prescription.]_

**Phase 8: Closing & Participatory Reflection**

- Is there anything else you would like to share about your daily experiences with medication management or caregiving technology?
- _[Probe: Is there a specific story or event we have not touched on that you feel is important for me to understand?]_

- Do you have any design suggestions or ideas for making these types of tools more useful and respectful for families like yours?
- _[Probe: Imagine building an AI assistant for your home—how would you want it to speak to your parents versus how it speaks to you?]_

**Appendix A2: Integrated In-Situ Interview and Artifact Elicitation Protocol, Caregiver Branch**

_Note: This is the caregiver branch of Appendix A and mirrors its eight phases. The caregiver is the participant, not a reporter on someone else's routine, and questions ask about their own work, judgment, and position in the care network. Every answer is recorded with its referent: the caregiver's own experience, their report of the older adult's experience, or a joint household account. Probes ask for the last actual occurrence rather than the general case. Sessions run in Bangla; kinship terms and address forms are preserved in the original before translation. Consent is taken from the caregiver individually and from the older adult whose routines are described._

**Phase 1: Environment & Artifact Observation (Pre-Interview) [executed]**

- **Observational Focus:** Survey the care environment before initiating questions. Document where medicines are stored and whether that place is the caregiver's territory, the older adult's, or shared. Record analog aids in use (strips with written labels, prescription slips, trays) and, equally, aids that are visible but abandoned. Note which devices sit where and whose they are.

**Phase 2: Background & Position in the Care Network [executed]**

- **Main Goal:** Establish who does the medication work, how that came to be, and how the caregiver accounts for it.

- Can you tell me a bit about yourself and about who lives here?
- _[Probe: Who in the household takes medicine every day, how many medicines each, and at what times?]_

- Which parts of that work do you handle?
- _[Probe: Walk me from buying the medicine to the moment it is swallowed. Which of those steps are yours?]_

- How did it come to be you rather than someone else?
- _[Probe: Was it discussed, or did it settle that way? Do you remember when it started?]_

- Who else in the family does some of this work?
- _[Probe: What does each of them do, and does that division stay fixed or move?]_

**Phase 3: Situated Walkthrough & Handover [executed]**

- **Observational Focus:** Request a physical walkthrough of a dose being prepared and given. Observe handling, label reading, and improvised organisation.

- Could you show me how a normal morning dose happens, taking me through it as you would do it?
- _[Probe: How do you know which medicine goes at which time? Show me where that information sits.]_

- Can the older adult find and identify their own medicines without you?
- _[Probe: Which ones, and how? By shape, colour, strip, or name?]_

- Have you tried a pillbox, a chart, a written list, or a phone alarm for this?
- _[Probe: What happened to it? If you stopped, what made you stop?]_

- If you had to be away for two days, how would you hand this over to someone else?
- _[Probe: Tell me about the last time that actually happened. What was hardest to explain?]_

**Phase 4: Scenario Probing: Disruption, Absence & Missed Doses [executed]**

- **Observational Focus:** Ground these inquiries in specific recent episodes. Absence is the recurring condition; locate the caregiver physically in each episode.

- Has a dose ever been missed?
- _[Probe: Tell me about the last time. What was happening in the house that day, and where were you?]_

- How did you find out, and what did you do once you knew?
- _[Probe: Did you call? Did you say anything about it afterwards? Did anyone else get involved?]_

- Has a missed dose ever led to something serious?
- _[Probe: Walk me through that day from the beginning.]_

- When is this hardest for you?
- _[Probe: Ask about work hours, sleep, travel, guests, weddings and social occasions, and religious observance.]_

- Has anyone taken the wrong medicine, or taken one twice?
- _[Probe: How was that discovered, and what happened next?]_

**Phase 5: Interpretation Work: Prescriptions & Medicine Knowledge [executed]**

- **Main Goal:** The caregiver as reader and translator, which is where their authority in the household is built.

- Who reads the prescription in this household?
- _[Probe: Can the older adult read it, and which parts? Do you have difficulty with any of it yourself?]_

- What do you do when something on a prescription is unclear?
- _[Probe: Tell me about the last time. Did you go to the doctor, the pharmacist, or a family member?]_

- When a new medicine is added, how do the others in the house come to know about it?
- _[Probe: Do you tell them the name, or only when to take it? Do you explain what it is for?]_

- Has a medicine ever caused a reaction or side effect?
- _[Probe: How did you work out that the medicine was the cause, and who did you consult?]_

**Phase 6: Technology, Proxy Use & Device Custody [executed]**

- **Observational Focus:** Request a demonstration of any tool in use. Observe who reaches for whose device and who navigates it.

- What phones are in the house, and whose is which?
- _[Probe: Button phone or smartphone? Does anyone share a device?]_

- What does the older adult use their phone for, and who taught them?

- Who sets things up on other people's phones here?
- _[Probe: Tell me about the last thing you set up for someone. How long did it take?]_

- Is there anything you set up for them that they later turned off or ignored?
- _[Probe: Did you find out, and did you say anything?]_

- Are there difficulties with reading labels, small text, or operating the device?
- _[Probe: Looking at this strip, can you read the instructions as you normally would?]_

**Phase 7: Checking, Visibility & Authority [executed, with specified additions]**

- **Main Goal:** How the caregiver's monitoring is conducted and how it is received, and who decides when something changes. Ask about conduct, not about whether checking is care or surveillance.

- On an ordinary day, how do you find out whether the doses were taken?
- _[Probe: Do you ask, watch, count strips, or assume? Tell me about yesterday.]_

- Does the older adult know when you are checking?
- _[Probe: Do you do it openly or quietly, and has that always been so?]_

- How do they respond when you ask?
- _[Probe: Has anyone ever been annoyed, or said they can manage on their own?]_

- Does not knowing whether a dose was taken weigh on you?
- _[Probe: What does that feel like on a working day?]_

- When something about the medicines has to change, who decides?
- _[Probe: Tell me about the last change. Who raised it, who agreed, who was told afterwards?]_

- Has the older adult ever changed something without telling you, or have you ever overruled them?
- _[Probe: What happened, and how did they take it?]_

- Do neighbours, friends, or relatives give advice about medicines here?
- _[Probe: Tell me about a specific occasion. Has anyone started or stopped a medicine because of it?]_

- Has the older adult ever asked you to stop being involved in some part of this? **[specified]**
- _[Probe: What happened, and where did it settle?]_

- When your role in their medicines changed, was anything said out loud about it, or did it just happen? **[specified]**
- _[Probe: Which would you have preferred?]_

- Has anyone else in the family wanted to take over some of this? **[specified]**
- _[Probe: How was that settled?]_

**Phase 8: Closing & Participatory Reflection [executed]**

- If something could take one part of this work off you, which part would you give up first?
- _[Probe: And which part would you keep for yourself?]_

- If a reminder system existed, who should it speak to, and in what order?
- _[Probe: Should it speak to them differently from how it speaks to you? What should it say to each of you?]_

- If a dose is not confirmed, what should happen next?
- _[Probe: How long should it wait, who should it tell, and should it tell them that it is telling you?]_

- Who should be able to change its settings?
- _[Probe: If they changed something, would you want to know? If you changed something, should they be told?]_

- Is there anything such a system should never do in this household?

- Is there something about looking after their medicines that we have not talked about?
- _[Probe: If you were advising the people building this, what would you tell them?]_

# Interview transcript

**Interviewer:** Assalamu alaikum apa

**Woman:** Wa alaikum salam come sit here

**Interviewer:** How are you

**Woman:** I am okay. What to say old age now so body is not same but I am okay

**Interviewer:** How old are you

**Woman:** Sixty seven I think. Yes sixty seven. My son says sixty seven so yes

**Interviewer:** Do you take medicine every day

**Woman:** Yes yes every day I take

**Interviewer:** Every day

**Woman:** Yes every day. If medicine is there then I take every day

**Interviewer:** What medicines do you take

**Woman:** I dont know all the names. I cannot say the names properly. One is for pressure. Doctor gave that. One sometimes for body pain. There is one more I take at night

**Interviewer:** How many medicines do you take in one day

**Woman:** Morning one or two. Night two sometimes. It changes also. Whatever they bring, that I take

**Interviewer:** Who brings your medicine

**Woman:** My son mostly. He brings it. Sometimes his wife brings if he is not here. I dont go to buy medicine much

**Interviewer:** Why dont you go yourself

**Woman:** I dont know which one to buy. Also market is not close. Walking is difficult now. I show the old packet to my son and he knows

**Interviewer:** So your son knows which medicine you need

**Woman:** Yes he knows more than me. He knows what doctor gave. He goes and brings it

**Interviewer:** When your medicine finishes what do you do

**Woman:** I tell them. I say medicine is finishing. Then I give the old strip or packet. They go and bring

**Interviewer:** Do you keep the medicine yourself

**Woman:** Yes I keep it in this box. It stays here. Sometimes my son keeps some also

**Interviewer:** Do you know which medicine is for morning and which is for night

**Woman:** Some I know. By looking I know this one morning and this one night. All I dont know. My son tells me also

**Interviewer:** Does anyone arrange the medicine for you

**Woman:** Yes mostly my son. He keeps the strips sometimes. He tells me this one take now this one take later. If he is outside I take myself from the box

**Interviewer:** So you take the medicine yourself

**Woman:** Yes yes I take myself. Nobody gives me every time. I can take myself. But bringing it and knowing which one, my son does that

**Interviewer:** Do you ever forget to take your medicine

**Woman:** Sometimes happens

**Interviewer:** How

**Woman:** If I am doing some work or talking then sometimes I forget. Later I remember. Sometimes my daughter in law asks me did you take medicine and then I remember

**Interviewer:** Does that happen often

**Woman:** No no not often. Sometimes only

**Interviewer:** What do you do when you remember late

**Woman:** If I remember soon then I take. If much time passed then I ask my son. I dont take two together. They told me not to do that

**Interviewer:** Who told you

**Woman:** My son. He talks with the doctor also

**Interviewer:** Do you use an alarm to remember

**Woman:** No no

**Interviewer:** Phone alarm

**Woman:** No. I dont know how to put that. I have phone but only for calling. My son knows these things

**Interviewer:** Do you write it down somewhere

**Woman:** No. I dont write medicine names. I cannot write properly. No need also. They tell me

**Interviewer:** Then how do you remember

**Woman:** Mostly after food. Morning after food I take. Night after food I take. So I remember from food time. Sometimes still I forget

**Interviewer:** Is taking medicine by yourself easy or difficult

**Woman:** Taking is easy. Knowing which medicine is little difficult

**Interviewer:** Why is it difficult

**Woman:** Names are difficult. The letters also small. I did not study much. I went to school little when I was small then no more. So I dont understand all this writing

**Interviewer:** Can you read the medicine packet

**Woman:** Little. Big writing I can see. Small writing no. I cannot read those

**Interviewer:** Then how do you know which medicine it is

**Woman:** I know the packet. I have seen it many times. I know this one is for pressure. Some tablets also look different. So I know some by looking

**Interviewer:** What if the packet changes

**Woman:** Then I ask first. I dont take it directly. If new packet comes I ask my son

**Interviewer:** Has that happened before

**Woman:** Yes one time packet was different. I said this is not mine. Then my son said same medicine only packet is different. Then I took it

**Interviewer:** Were you worried

**Woman:** Yes. I was thinking what if wrong medicine. We dont know these things. Doctor knows. My son knows some because he goes with me

**Interviewer:** Who usually takes you to the doctor

**Woman:** My son. Mostly he takes me

**Interviewer:** Do you go to the doctor every time before buying medicine

**Woman:** No no not every time. If same medicine finishes then my son brings same one. If something is wrong or more problem then we go doctor

**Interviewer:** Who decides which medicine you should take

**Woman:** Doctor says. Then my son brings it. I dont decide much

**Interviewer:** If your son is not home and you need medicine what do you do

**Woman:** His wife can bring. Sometimes grandson can also bring. Somebody will go. I dont usually go myself

**Interviewer:** What if nobody can bring it

**Woman:** Then it becomes problem. Maybe I wait. Sometimes neighbour goes to market so I can ask them

**Interviewer:** Has your medicine ever finished before someone brought more

**Woman:** Yes sometimes. We forget to buy before it finishes. Then there is no medicine

**Interviewer:** What happens then

**Woman:** I tell my son. He says you should have told before. Then he goes and brings it

**Interviewer:** Does that worry you

**Woman:** Little. Doctor says take regularly. But if medicine is not there what can I do. I cannot go myself every time

**Interviewer:** Do you count how many tablets are left

**Woman:** No I dont count

**Interviewer:** Then how do you know it is finishing

**Woman:** I look at the strip. When few are left I say medicine is finishing. Thats all

**Interviewer:** Do you keep all the medicines together

**Woman:** Mostly in the box. Some are kept somewhere else also. Children touch things so we keep some higher

**Interviewer:** Have you ever taken the wrong medicine

**Woman:** No no. I try not to. Once I was going to take the night one in morning. My daughter in law saw and said no this one is night. So I did not take it

**Interviewer:** What happened after that

**Woman:** Nothing. I had not taken it. She saw before

**Interviewer:** Do you feel you need someone to help with your medicine

**Woman:** For bringing and checking yes. For taking no. I can take myself. But somebody should bring it and tell me sometimes

**Interviewer:** Who helps you most

**Woman:** My son mostly. His wife also helps. But my son knows more about the medicine

**Interviewer:** Do you understand what the doctor tells you

**Woman:** Some I understand. Some I dont. Sometimes doctor talks fast. My son tells me again at home

**Interviewer:** What does he tell you

**Woman:** This one after food. This one at night. This one take this much. Like that

**Interviewer:** Do you remember those instructions

**Woman:** Some I remember. If simple then I remember. Before food and after food sometimes I forget which one

**Interviewer:** What do you do then

**Woman:** I ask them. Why will I guess. Better ask

**Interviewer:** Do you think the medicine is helping you

**Woman:** Yes I think. Pressure medicine if I dont take then sometimes head does not feel good. So I take it

**Interviewer:** Do you ever stop medicine when you feel better

**Woman:** Pain medicine sometimes I stop when pain is gone. But pressure one I dont stop. My son says keep taking

**Interviewer:** Why does he say that

**Woman:** Doctor told him. He says even if you feel okay take it. So I listen

**Interviewer:** Do you change the amount yourself

**Woman:** No. I dont know how much. Doctor says half then half. Full then full. I dont change

**Interviewer:** Do you ever break the tablet

**Woman:** Sometimes. One medicine doctor said half. My son breaks it for me. I cannot see properly sometimes

**Interviewer:** Do you have difficulty swallowing medicine

**Woman:** No big problem. Sometimes big tablet is difficult. Then I drink more water

**Interviewer:** Do you have any problem after taking medicine

**Woman:** Not much. Sometimes stomach feels bad. I tell my son. Then he asks doctor if needed

**Interviewer:** Do you remember which medicine causes that

**Woman:** No I dont know the name. I know the tablet when I see it but name no

**Interviewer:** If I showed you the medicine would you know it

**Woman:** Maybe yes. If I see the strip I can know

**Interviewer:** Is the writing on the medicine packet useful for you

**Woman:** For me not much. For my son yes. He reads it. The writing is very small for me

**Interviewer:** What if there was a medicine box with pictures for morning and night

**Woman:** That would be good. If morning sun and night moon then I can understand. Pictures are easy

**Interviewer:** What if there was a phone application to help you with medicine

**Woman:** No I cannot use application

**Interviewer:** What if it only reminded you when to take it

**Woman:** If it is very simple maybe. My son would have to put it. I dont know how to do

**Interviewer:** If it rang at medicine time would that help

**Woman:** Yes maybe. If it rings and tells me medicine time then I can take. But if I have to press many things then no

**Interviewer:** Would technology make taking medicine easier for you

**Woman:** Maybe for my son. For me I dont know. I am used to this way. My son brings and tells me. I take

**Interviewer:** So you prefer your family to help you

**Woman:** Yes yes. They are here. They know more than me. I am old now

**Interviewer:** Do you feel bad asking them to bring your medicine

**Woman:** No why. They are my children. If I need medicine they bring it. Sometimes they are busy but they bring

**Interviewer:** Would you like to learn more about your medicines

**Woman:** Yes if somebody explains slowly. I can understand some. But all the names I cannot remember

**Interviewer:** What would you like to know

**Woman:** Which one is for what. When to take. How many. These things. If someone tells me slowly I can remember

**Interviewer:** Would you like to buy your own medicine

**Woman:** No no. Better they bring. I dont know which one to buy. I can buy other small things but medicine no

**Interviewer:** If the medicine shop gave you the same medicine every time would you be okay

**Woman:** If it is the same one doctor gave then yes. If packet is different I ask my son first

**Interviewer:** Why

**Woman:** Because I dont know if it is same or different. Better check first

**Interviewer:** Who do you trust most about your medicine

**Woman:** Doctor. Then my son. Doctor tells and my son brings

**Interviewer:** Do you ask neighbours about medicines

**Woman:** Sometimes we talk. They say this medicine is good or that one is good. But I dont take because they say. I ask my son

**Interviewer:** Do you ever take another persons medicine

**Woman:** No no. Their medicine is theirs. Mine is mine. I dont take

**Interviewer:** Is anything difficult about taking medicine every day

**Woman:** Taking is not difficult. Getting medicine is difficult sometimes. If it is in the house then I take. If finished then somebody has to go

**Interviewer:** Anything else

**Woman:** Remembering sometimes. Sometimes I think I already took it. Then I am not sure. I ask my daughter in law if she saw me take

**Interviewer:** Does that happen often

**Woman:** No no sometimes only. Old age you know

**Interviewer:** What happens if you forget and nobody is there

**Woman:** I remember later maybe. I dont take extra. I wait and ask when they come

**Interviewer:** Do you think a reminder would help

**Woman:** Yes maybe. A voice reminder would be good. If someone says medicine time then I know

**Interviewer:** Would you want it in English

**Woman:** English no baba. I dont understand English. Bangla is better. If it speaks Bangla I can understand

**Interviewer:** What about written instructions

**Woman:** Bangla is better. Big writing also. Small writing I cannot see properly

**Interviewer:** Do you use glasses

**Woman:** Sometimes. I have glasses. For reading I use sometimes

**Interviewer:** If the medicine box had big writing and pictures would that help

**Woman:** Yes yes. Morning picture and night picture. Then I can know. But still somebody should bring the medicine

**Interviewer:** If you had to manage everything alone what would happen

**Woman:** It would be hard. Taking I can do. Buying and knowing all the names would be hard. I may take wrong one. So somebody should help

**Interviewer:** Do other older women also get medicine from their family

**Woman:** Many do. Some go themselves. But many children bring. If husband is there husband may bring

**Interviewer:** Do you talk with other older women about medicine

**Woman:** Yes sometimes. We sit and talk. This one has pressure this one has sugar. Everyone has some medicine now

**Interviewer:** Do they remind each other

**Woman:** Sometimes. But family reminds more

**Interviewer:** How would you explain the way you manage your medicine

**Woman:** I keep it in the box. My son brings it. He tells me morning night. I take myself. If I forget they tell me. If medicine finishes I tell them. This is how

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing what would you change

**Woman:** Medicine should be easy to know. Different colour or picture. Big writing. Then old people can know

**Interviewer:** Anything else

**Woman:** Medicine should not finish suddenly. If somebody reminds before it finishes then good. Sometimes we forget

**Interviewer:** If something reminded your son that the medicine is almost finished would that help

**Woman:** Yes for him it would help. Then he can bring before it finishes. Sometimes he forgets also. He has work

**Interviewer:** So the main problem is not taking the medicine

**Woman:** No taking is okay. Bringing it is sometimes problem. Knowing which one also. Remembering sometimes

**Interviewer:** If your son did not bring the medicine would you know what to buy

**Woman:** Some maybe. All no. I can take the old strip and show in shop. But I would still want my son to bring it

**Interviewer:** Why

**Woman:** He knows what doctor gave. He knows which one I take morning and which one at night. Shop man does not know me like that

**Interviewer:** Do you feel you can manage your medicine yourself

**Woman:** Some part yes. I can take it myself. Most days I remember. But buying and checking I need help

**Interviewer:** Okay apa I think that is all

**Woman:** Finished

**Interviewer:** Yes thank you for talking with me

**Woman:** No problem baba. You came and asked all these things. Sit little if you want

**Interviewer:** Thank you

**Woman:** Okay baba

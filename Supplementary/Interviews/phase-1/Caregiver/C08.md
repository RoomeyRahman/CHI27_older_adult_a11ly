 Caregiver Interview Transcript: Medication Management

Participant Name:** Borhan Uddin Rana  
Age: 25  

Phase 1: Environment & Caregiver Context

Interviewer: Hello! To start off, could you tell me a bit about yourself and about who lives here?

Borhan Uddin Rana: My name is Borhan Uddin Rana. I'm 25 years old. My grandmother lives with us, and my mother is a diabetes patient. In their case, I help manage and give them their medicines regularly.

Interviewer: Which parts of that medicine work do you handle?

Borhan Uddin Rana: Almost regularly, they take around five to six medicines.



Phase 2: Current Practices & Workflow

Interviewer: Could you show me how a normal dose happens, taking me through it as you would do it? How do you know which medicine goes at which time?

Borhan Uddin Rana: When I buy the medicines, I usually bring them with the instructions written directly on the strips — whether to take them before or after meals, in the morning, or at night. Otherwise, I can understand directly from the doctor’s prescription. So, I don’t really feel the need for any additional notes.


Phase 3: Operational Friction & Missed Doses

Interviewer: Has a dose ever been missed?

Borhan Uddin Rana: Yes, it often happens — especially when I’m not at home or when I'm busy with work. Sometimes even when I’m asleep, a dose gets missed.

Interviewer: Tell me about the last time. What was happening in the house that day, and where were you?

Borhan Uddin Rana: When I’m too busy with household work. If I don’t supervise them directly, sometimes they miss doses.



Phase 4: Device Eco-system & Literacy

Interviewer: What phones are in the house, and whose is which?

Borhan Uddin Rana: They use button phones. They are not very interested in smartphones, and they find smartphone functions harder to use, so they mostly use button phones.

Interviewer: If a reminder system existed, who should it speak to, and in what order?

Borhan Uddin Rana: Yes, if there were a system like that, it would help reduce missed doses.


Phase 5: Prescription Accessibility & Consultation

Interviewer: What do you do when something on a prescription is unclear?

Borhan Uddin Rana: Prescriptions are often not very clear because of the doctor’s handwriting. That’s why sometimes we need to consult the pharmacist to understand properly.



Phase 6: Audio Reminders & Interaction Preference

Interviewer: On an ordinary day, how do you find out whether the doses were taken? And if a dose is not confirmed, what should happen next?

Borhan Uddin Rana: Yes, if the alarm stated the name of the medicine, that would be very helpful. I would prefer an alarm. Sometimes I feel that instead of needing to make separate appointments with consultants, I should just be able to confirm via phone whether the prescribed medicine is correct or not.

Phase 7: Participatory Design Insights & Feedback

Interviewer: Is there something about looking after their medicines that we have not talked about?

Borhan Uddin Rana: To make these technologies more effective for people like me, I would suggest this: I personally face difficulties when the system only recognizes brand names of medicines. If a system could be developed using generic names, then it would be easier, because brand names often differ. With generic names, there would be more consistency and convenience.

# 3. Simanto Roy

**Interviewer:** Alright we will talk about your medicine routine and also about your family. First tell me a little about yourself and about who lives here

**Respondent:** My name is Simanto Roy. I am 21 years old. I work in software. I normally don't take any medicine regularly. My parents take medicines every day. My father is diabetic so he takes three to four medicines daily. My mother takes around two medicines daily.

**Interviewer:** Which parts of that medicine work do you handle?

**Respondent:** Yes. I don't manage every single dose but I see a lot of it because we stay together. Sometimes I remind them. Sometimes they remember themselves. Sometimes I check.

**Interviewer:** Could you show me how a normal morning dose happens, taking me through it as you would do it?

**Respondent:** Morning my father gets up and then he takes his medicines. My mother also has her medicines. Usually the medicines are kept in a common place. They take them after food or at the time doctor said. I don't always stand there. But if I am around I remind them.

**Interviewer:** How many medicines does your father take

**Respondent:** Three to four daily. It changes sometimes. He is diabetic so there are a few medicines. My mother takes two daily.

**Interviewer:** How do you know which medicine goes at which time?

**Respondent:** Mostly by habit. They know the medicines because they have been taking them for long time. The medicine name is also written on the back of the medicine leaf. So they see it. But sometimes they still forget.

**Interviewer:** Do they use an alarm

**Respondent:** No. Not really. We don't have any fixed medicine app or something. We just remember.

**Interviewer:** Have you tried a pillbox, a chart, a written list, or a phone alarm for this?

**Respondent:** No. I have thought about it but never set one properly.

**Interviewer:** Has a dose ever been missed?

**Respondent:** Sometimes they just miss it. A few days ago my father forgot his morning dose. Then by noon he was not feeling good and his sugar level dropped a lot. It became a serious thing and we had to take him to hospital.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time. What was happening in the house that day, and where were you?

**Respondent:** We were busy with other things. He forgot the morning dose. I was also doing my own work. Nobody checked at that exact time. Later we noticed something was wrong.

**Interviewer:** Did that change how you think about medicine reminders

**Respondent:** Yes definitely. After that I think if there was some reminder I would feel better. Because before that we just assumed he will remember. Now I feel maybe we should not depend only on memory.

**Interviewer:** Are you now more likely to check

**Respondent:** Yes. Sometimes I ask did you take the medicine. My mother also asks my father. We keep checking.

**Interviewer:** Does that create pressure for you

**Respondent:** Yes a little. I work in software and sometimes I am busy. Then I think I should check. If nobody checks then I worry. So it is pressure. It is not very big every day but it is there.

**Interviewer:** What about your mother

**Respondent:** She takes two medicines. She usually remembers but sometimes she misses. She is more like she depends on the written instruction and family also. If someone in the family says take it now then she takes it.

**Interviewer:** Who else in the family does some of this work?

**Respondent:** All of us in some way. But mostly whoever is at home at that time. We don't have a proper system.

**Interviewer:** So it is more family coordination

**Respondent:** Yes exactly. Somebody sees. Somebody reminds. Somebody checks. It is like that.

**Interviewer:** If you had to be away for two days, how would you hand this over to someone else?

**Respondent:** That is a problem. Sometimes they forget to carry the medicines. Even when they carry them they can forget the time. Routine changes when travelling.

**Interviewer:** Do they use a separate bag for medicines

**Respondent:** Sometimes. But not always. That is why they forget. If there is a separate small bag maybe it helps.

**Interviewer:** Have you used any technology for this

**Respondent:** No. Not for medicine. Smartphones are used in our family but not for medicine tracking.

**Interviewer:** Everyone has smartphone

**Respondent:** Yes. We are comfortable with smartphones. Button phone also we know but now mostly smartphone.

**Interviewer:** Who sets things up on other people's phones here?

**Respondent:** I do some of it. Since I work in software I am more comfortable with settings and apps. My parents ask me if they need something.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you set up for them that they later turned off or ignored?

**Respondent:** Yes probably. I would set it up and then explain to them. I think that is how it would work.

**Interviewer:** What kind of reminder would work for them

**Respondent:** Voice. I think voice is better. If the phone says take your medicine now then they will hear it. Text may be ignored.

**Interviewer:** What about text for you

**Respondent:** Text is okay for me. But for them voice is better.

**Interviewer:** Would you want to get a notification too

**Respondent:** Yes. Maybe only if they don't confirm. Otherwise I don't need every reminder. But if they miss then I should know.

**Interviewer:** Why

**Respondent:** Because of what happened with my father. I don't want to repeat that situation. If they don't take it then I want to know.

**Interviewer:** Would your parents feel comfortable with that

**Respondent:** I think yes. It is family. We already ask each other. So app reminder is just another way.

**Interviewer:** What if the app showed some medicine information

**Respondent:** That would be useful. Both reminder and information. Especially because sometimes the medicine changes. If the app can show what it is for and when to take it then I can check.

**Interviewer:** Would you trust the app

**Respondent:** I would use it as a reminder. But for changing medicine I would still check the doctor. I won't blindly follow the app.

**Interviewer:** What about medicine recommendations from friends

**Respondent:** Friends definitely influence me. Like if I am with a group and everyone says they take some medicine for a common problem, I may feel that medicine is more trustworthy because many people are using it.

**Interviewer:** Even if you know it may not be the best medicine

**Respondent:** Yes maybe I still trust it more. Because the group gives some confidence. Like if five people say the same thing then I think okay maybe there is something to it.

**Interviewer:** And if you heard something controversial about that medicine

**Respondent:** I would still be influenced by the group. But I would check before actually taking it. I think group opinion changes my confidence but doctor decides for me.

**Interviewer:** Why do you think you react that way

**Respondent:** Because you see people around you using something. It feels tested. You think okay they used it and nothing happened. So you trust it more. It is a social thing.

**Interviewer:** Do neighbours, friends, or relatives give advice about medicines here?

**Respondent:** They also listen to family and neighbours. They ask what other people are taking. But I usually tell them not to change medicine just because someone else takes it.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about a specific occasion. Has anyone started or stopped a medicine because of that advice?

**Respondent:** Yes. I tell them that one person's medicine is not automatically ours. But honestly people still influence each other. Especially older people.

**Interviewer:** Who reads the prescription in this household?

**Respondent:** I can usually read them better than my parents, but some handwriting is difficult. So even I sometimes ask the pharmacy. For my parents we often go to the nearby pharmacy when we don't understand.

**Interviewer:** What do you do when something on a prescription is unclear?

**Respondent:** Not currently. I know there are ways with phone cameras or OCR, but we don't use anything like that at home.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you had to get help because something on a prescription was unclear.

**Respondent:** Yes. Very much. It can save time. Take a photo and tell us the medicine. That would be useful.

**Interviewer:** What should it show after reading

**Respondent:** Medicine name. Dose. Time. Maybe image of the tablet. Maybe instructions. And who it is for. That last thing is important because there are two people taking different medicines.

**Interviewer:** Would color coding help

**Respondent:** Yes. Maybe father and mother can have separate colors. Morning and night can also have simple colors. But not too much. Simple is better.

**Interviewer:** Would a physical pill box help

**Respondent:** Yes. A pill box can help too. But the main problem for us is forgetting and changing routine. Pill box doesn't remind you if you are away.

**Interviewer:** What happens when there are guests or a family event

**Respondent:** Then the routine gets disturbed. People come, we talk, food time changes. Then my parents can forget. The same happens during travel.

**Interviewer:** So social situations affect the medicine routine

**Respondent:** Yes. Definitely. When there are more people and more things going on the routine becomes less regular.

**Interviewer:** And does seeing other people take medicine remind you

**Respondent:** Yes. It happens. If somebody says I have to take my medicine now then I remember that my father has to take his. Or if a friend takes something, it reminds me of our medicines at home.

**Interviewer:** If something could take one part of this work off you, which part would you give up first?

**Respondent:** Shared reminders. I think. My parents get voice reminder and I get a notification if they don't confirm. That would reduce my worry.

**Interviewer:** If a dose is not confirmed, what should happen next?

**Respondent:** Maybe not automatically. But it can show the doctor number. Maybe pharmacy number too. Calling should be easy.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the latest prescription stored

**Respondent:** Yes. That can be useful. Then if we forget which medicine was prescribed we can check.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything such a system should never do in this household?

**Respondent:** Too much work. If I have to enter every small thing manually every day then I won't use it. I want to set it once and let it work.

**Interviewer:** Who should be able to change its settings?

**Respondent:** Yes. Set once. Then reminder comes. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Is there something about looking after their medicines that we have not talked about?

**Respondent:** The main thing is family helps a lot. We depend on each other. If one person forgets somebody else usually remembers. But sometimes everyone is busy. That is when the problem happens.

**Interviewer:** So technology should support the family rather than replace the family

**Respondent:** Yes exactly. It should help the family. Not replace us. It should make checking easier.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to add

**Respondent:** No. Just that voice reminder is useful and if it can read prescription and remind family members too then it will be very helpful.

**Interviewer:** Thank you

**Respondent:** Okay.

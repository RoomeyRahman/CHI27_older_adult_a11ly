Interview Transcript: Daily Medication Caregiving & Technology Needs

Participant: Aisha (20-year-old female caregiver)  
 


Phase 1 & 2: Caregiver Context & Household Dynamics

Interviewer: Hello! Thank you so much for sitting down with me today. To start off, could you tell me a bit about yourself and your role here at home?

Aisha: Hello! My name is Aisha. I'm twenty years old, and I live here with my husband and my family. Both my father-in-law and mother-in-law live with us in the household. 

Interviewer: That's wonderful. And how do you help your family members with taking their medicine on a day-to-day basis?

Aisha: Well, those who are elderly or sick in our home and can't take their medicines themselves—I'm the one who helps them with it. Every single day, I make sure they get what they need.

Interviewer: Could you give me an idea of how many types of medicine they take each day?

Aisha: They take around four to five medicines daily.



Phase 3: Current Practices & Routines

Interviewer: Four to five medicines daily is quite a bit to keep track of! How do you remember which medicine to give them and at what time?

Aisha: Honestly, I just remember on my own! I don't really use any aids or anything. It’s just become a habit from taking them and giving them every single day, so it stays in my head.

Interviewer: Do you use any specific method or tool—like writing notes down, checking phone alarms, or using a daily pillbox?

Aisha: No, no, I don't use any of those tools at all.



Phase 4: Challenges, Busy Routines & Missed Doses

Interviewer: Since you rely mostly on memory, do you ever miss a dose?

Aisha: Yeah, sometimes it happens... yes.

Interviewer: When that happens, why do you think a dose gets missed?

Aisha: Sometimes I simply forget, and other times I just get really busy with household work and miss the time. 

Interviewer: When does managing their medicine become especially difficult for you?

Aisha: It gets hardest when I'm super busy with household work—especially in the morning when there's so much going on, or at other hectic times during the day.


Phase 5 & 6: Technology, Accessibility & Preference

Interviewer: What type of phone do you or your family members usually use at home?

Aisha: We use a smartphone.

Interviewer: For keeping up with medicine schedules, do you think using technology would be easy or difficult for you?

Aisha: Oh, it would be easy for me! If there’s a call or a sound reminder on the phone, I could easily give them the medicine right on time.

Interviewer: Do you have any difficulty reading medicine instructions or operating a digital device?

Aisha: No, I don't have any trouble with reading or using devices at all.

Interviewer: How exactly do you think a medicine-reminder technology would help you in your everyday routine?

Aisha: If there’s something like an automated call or an alarm, it will remind me right on time. It’ll just make it so much easier to make sure they get their medicine without delays.

Interviewer: How would you prefer to receive those reminders—an automated call, a text message, or an alarm?

Aisha: A call! An incoming call is definitely the most effective for me because it grabs my attention right away while I'm working.


Phase 7 & 8: Reflection & Future Outlook

Interviewer: That makes complete sense, especially when you're busy around the house. Is there anything else you'd like to share about managing medications for your in-laws?

Aisha: Just that caring for elderly parents is a daily responsibility, and when work piles up, having a direct call reminder would really give me peace of mind that I won't accidentally miss their medication times again.

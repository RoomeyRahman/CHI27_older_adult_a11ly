# Interview Transcript

**Respondent: Female, 68 years, retired school teacher**

**Interviewer:** So first can you tell me little about yourself

**Respondent:** I am 68 now. I was a school teacher. I taught in school for many years. Now retired. I live with my husband and my daughter. Three people in the house.

**Interviewer:** So three members

**Respondent:** Yes three. Me my husband and daughter. My daughter is working so she goes out in the morning and comes later.

**Interviewer:** Do you take medicine every day

**Respondent:** Yes I take one medicine regularly. Every day. It is one tablet only for me.

**Interviewer:** Only one medicine

**Respondent:** Yes one regular medicine. Earlier there were some other medicines also for some time but now only this one. This one I have been taking for long time.

**Interviewer:** Do you take it at the same time every day

**Respondent:** I try to. I take according to what doctor has written. After so many years it has become habit. Morning time I usually take it.

**Interviewer:** Do you always remember it

**Respondent:** Mostly yes. Because it is old habit. When I have my tea and breakfast then I remember. But sometimes time changes. If I am busy with something then I may take it late.

**Interviewer:** So you don't forget the medicine completely usually

**Respondent:** No no. Completely forget not much. Time can become late. That happens.

**Interviewer:** How do you remember it

**Respondent:** I don't write anything. I follow the prescription and now it is in my head. Same thing every day. In the beginning I had to look at the paper. Now no need.

**Interviewer:** Do you keep the prescription somewhere

**Respondent:** Yes my daughter keeps the important papers. I have the old prescription also somewhere. But the latest one she keeps. She is better with these things.

**Interviewer:** Why does she keep it

**Respondent:** Because she knows where everything is. I have papers from school also, old papers, books, many things. If I keep it I may put somewhere and later search. She keeps all the medical papers in one file.

**Interviewer:** Does anyone help you take your medicine

**Respondent:** Normally no. I take myself. I don't need someone to give it to me. But if I make some mistake or if I am busy then my husband may remind me. My daughter also reminds.

**Interviewer:** What kind of mistake

**Respondent:** Not taking wrong dose. That I don't do. Sometimes the time becomes wrong. Suppose I normally take in morning but I am going somewhere or some guest comes then I take late.

**Interviewer:** Has your family ever reminded you

**Respondent:** Yes yes. My husband especially. He will ask did you take your medicine. Sometimes my daughter asks also. They don't give me the tablet usually. Just remind me.

**Interviewer:** Does that help

**Respondent:** Of course it helps. Even if I know myself, when someone asks you remember. Sometimes I say yes I have taken, then I realise no no I haven't. So it helps.

**Interviewer:** Do you think you depend on your family for medicine

**Respondent:** For the medicine itself no. I know my medicine. But for keeping the papers, buying it, doctor appointment, those things yes. My daughter helps with those. She knows when the strip is going to finish also.

**Interviewer:** Who usually buys the medicine

**Respondent:** Mostly my daughter. Sometimes my husband goes. If they are going outside they bring it. I don't always go to the pharmacy now.

**Interviewer:** Do you tell them when you need it

**Respondent:** Yes I tell them. But sometimes daughter checks herself. She says only few tablets are left. We should get another strip. So she remembers.

**Interviewer:** Do you visit the doctor regularly

**Respondent:** Yes regularly. I go when doctor has told me. My daughter comes with me sometimes. If she cannot then my husband comes.

**Interviewer:** Why do you like someone coming with you

**Respondent:** Two people hear better than one. Doctor may tell something and I understand one thing. Then after coming home I think maybe what did doctor say. Daughter remembers. She will say doctor told this. So it is useful.

**Interviewer:** Do you ask your family before changing any medicine

**Respondent:** Yes yes. I don't change on my own. If I feel something is not right I tell them and then we ask doctor. My daughter is very particular about this.

**Interviewer:** What about if someone outside tells you that some other medicine is good

**Respondent:** People tell many things. At school also earlier teachers used to talk about medicines. Now neighbours also. Somebody says this medicine is very good, somebody says that one is better. I listen. But I don't immediately take it.

**Interviewer:** If many people say the same medicine is good

**Respondent:** Then I will definitely take notice. If many people are saying it then maybe there is some reason. Especially if they are people I know and they are taking it themselves. I will ask what it is and what doctor gave it.

**Interviewer:** Would you consider taking it

**Respondent:** I would consider it yes. I would ask my daughter also. Then we can ask my doctor. I don't like taking somebody else's medicine directly. But if many people are using it and saying it helped then I will not ignore it.

**Interviewer:** Why does the group opinion matter

**Respondent:** Because they have experience. They are not talking only from hearing. If a person has taken something for some time and says it helped then you think about it. But still one person's medicine is not always another person's medicine.

**Interviewer:** So what would you trust more family or people in the group

**Respondent:** Family for me. Definitely family. They know my history and doctor. The group can tell me about something. Then family will check whether it is suitable for me.

**Interviewer:** Suppose your friends say a medicine is better than the one you are taking

**Respondent:** I will ask about it. Maybe I will ask my daughter to find out. I won't stop mine immediately. First we ask doctor. If doctor says okay then maybe. If doctor says no then no.

**Interviewer:** Do you talk about medicines when you meet other people

**Respondent:** Yes. At this age everybody has some medicine. We talk about BP and sugar and bones and all. Earlier in school we talked about students and lessons. Now we talk about medicines also.

**Interviewer:** Do other people ever remind you to take medicine

**Respondent:** Sometimes. If I am sitting with someone and they say I have to take my tablet now then I remember mine also. That happens. But at home my husband or daughter reminds more.

**Interviewer:** What happens when you go to a gathering

**Respondent:** If it is nearby and for little time no issue. If we are there for many hours then sometimes timing becomes late. You get busy talking. You don't carry your medicine always. Now I try to keep one strip in my bag.

**Interviewer:** Who tells you to carry it

**Respondent:** My daughter mostly. She says whenever you go out keep the medicine. She says it again and again. I tell her yes yes I know. Then sometimes I still forget the bag.

**Interviewer:** So she reminds you about carrying also

**Respondent:** Yes. Children are like that now. They worry more. They keep checking.

**Interviewer:** Can you read the medicine packet

**Respondent:** Yes I can read. I was a teacher so reading is not a problem. But small letters are difficult now. I need my glasses. If the letters are very small then I have to bring it close.

**Interviewer:** Without glasses

**Respondent:** No no. Without glasses I can see the packet but not properly read the small writing.

**Interviewer:** Does that cause confusion between medicines

**Respondent:** If there is only my medicine no. But if there are many strips on the table then maybe. I check properly. I don't want to take something without knowing.

**Interviewer:** Do you read the prescription yourself

**Respondent:** Yes I can read most of it. Doctor handwriting sometimes is another matter. That handwriting even teachers may have difficulty with.

**Interviewer:** So sometimes your daughter helps

**Respondent:** Yes. She checks the prescription also. If something is changed she tells me. She is very careful with these things.

**Interviewer:** What phone do you use

**Respondent:** I have a button phone.

**Interviewer:** Not a smartphone

**Respondent:** No no. I have not taken smartphone. My daughter has smartphone but mine is button phone. I can call and receive calls. That is enough for me.

**Interviewer:** Do you use any medicine app

**Respondent:** No. I don't use apps.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever tried one

**Respondent:** No. My daughter once showed me some things on her phone but I didn't understand. There are too many things. I don't know where to press.

**Interviewer:** If somebody gave you an app that reminds you about medicine would it be easy or difficult

**Respondent:** Difficult for me. I am not used to apps. If somebody sets it and only one thing comes on the screen maybe I can understand. But if I have to put medicine name and time and all myself then no.

**Interviewer:** What would make it easier

**Respondent:** Very simple. Big letters. One thing at a time. It should say medicine time. Maybe show the picture also. Then I can see this is the medicine.

**Interviewer:** Would you want sound

**Respondent:** Sound can be useful. But it should be a simple sound. If it starts making many sounds I will get confused. One reminder is enough and then maybe again after some time.

**Interviewer:** What if you don't respond to it

**Respondent:** Then maybe it can tell my daughter. That is useful. Because if I didn't respond she can call and ask. But not for every small thing. Only if I really miss it.

**Interviewer:** Would you want your daughter to set up the app

**Respondent:** Yes definitely. She should set it up. I don't know these things. Once she sets it then maybe I can use it.

**Interviewer:** Would you trust an app if your daughter set it up

**Respondent:** Yes more. Because she has checked it. If some app just comes and says take this medicine, I don't know whether it is correct. My daughter knows my prescription.

**Interviewer:** What about if the app can take a photo of the medicine and recognise it

**Respondent:** That sounds useful. Especially for older people. If you have several strips then photo can help. But I would still want family to check it. I don't want the app deciding medicine by itself.

**Interviewer:** What else could the app have

**Respondent:** Maybe medicine finishing reminder. My daughter already checks that but app can remind her. Doctor appointment also maybe. But don't put too many things. If too much comes then I won't use it.

**Interviewer:** Would you like the app to show why you take the medicine

**Respondent:** Maybe yes. But simple. Like what it is for. I don't need a whole explanation every time. Just small information.

**Interviewer:** Would you want to type anything into it

**Respondent:** No. Typing on phone is not for me. Button phone also doesn't help. If I have to type then my daughter can do it.

**Interviewer:** What if you could just press one big button after taking the medicine

**Respondent:** That I can do. One button I can understand. Taken. Finished. But if I forget to press it, it should not mean I didn't take. Sometimes I take and forget these phone things.

**Interviewer:** That's important

**Respondent:** Yes because medicine and phone are two different things. Taking medicine is the main thing. Pressing button should be secondary.

**Interviewer:** If your husband reminds you and the app reminds you both at the same time would that be annoying

**Respondent:** Maybe little. My husband will say I already told you. But it's okay. Better two reminders than no reminder. If it happens every day then maybe we can adjust it.

**Interviewer:** What is the hardest thing about managing your medicine now

**Respondent:** For me not the medicine itself. It is remembering when the routine changes. If every day is same then I know. If I have to go somewhere, guests come, or some program is there, then timing changes.

**Interviewer:** And who helps when routine changes

**Respondent:** Family. My husband mostly at home. Daughter checks if I am going out. She will ask have you kept your medicine. Sometimes I get irritated also because she asks so much. But later I think okay she is helping.

**Interviewer:** Do you feel you need family help even though you can take the medicine yourself

**Respondent:** Yes little bit. Taking it myself I can. But keeping track of everything is different. Doctor papers, buying, changes, all that family helps. At this age it is better somebody knows what medicines you are taking.

**Interviewer:** If you were alone for a few days what would happen

**Respondent:** I would manage the one tablet. That is not difficult. But if something changed then I would call my daughter. I always call her for these things.

**Interviewer:** Would you ask a neighbour

**Respondent:** If needed yes. But first family. My daughter or husband. Neighbour can tell something but they don't know my prescription.

**Interviewer:** If your daughter told you an app is useful and all the family started using it would you try it

**Respondent:** Yes maybe. If family is using it then I will feel comfortable. If they show me once or twice then I can learn slowly. I am not against phone. I just don't understand apps very easily.

**Interviewer:** What should the app not do

**Respondent:** Don't make it complicated. Don't show too many medicines if I have only one. Don't use very small writing. Don't ask me to type too much. And don't make me change things myself.

**Interviewer:** How should it speak to you

**Respondent:** Like simple reminder. No difficult words. Just tell me it is time for medicine. If there is something for my daughter then she can get the details. I don't need all those details.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to add

**Respondent:** No no. I think medicine becomes easier when routine is fixed. Same time same place. If family also knows then no problem. Technology can help maybe but for people like me it should be very simple. Otherwise family is easier.

**Interviewer:** Okay thank you

**Respondent:** Yes okay. No problem.

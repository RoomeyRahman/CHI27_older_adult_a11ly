# Interview Transcript 2

**Respondent: Female, 66 years**

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me little about yourself

**Respondent:** I am 66. I stay with my husband. My daughter also comes every day because she stays close. Sometimes my son also comes. Mostly family is there.

**Interviewer:** Do you take medicines every day

**Respondent:** Yes every day. Morning and night mostly. One is after food also.

**Interviewer:** How many medicines

**Respondent:** I don't know exact number. Three four maybe. Some tablets are there. My daughter knows the names.

**Interviewer:** You don't remember the names

**Respondent:** No no all the names I don't remember. I know which strip. My daughter keeps them separately. This one morning this one night. If you ask name I cannot tell properly.

**Interviewer:** How do you remember when to take them

**Respondent:** Family tells me. I also remember because it is daily. But mostly my husband asks and daughter tells. Morning after tea I take. Night after dinner. Like that.

**Interviewer:** Do you set an alarm on your phone

**Respondent:** No no. My daughter has set something once but I did not understand. Phone keeps making sound and I don't know why. So I don't use that.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever forget

**Respondent:** Sometimes yes. Not always. If I am busy with guests or going somewhere then it happens. At home my husband reminds. If he forgets also then maybe my daughter calls.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you forgot

**Respondent:** I think last week or maybe before that. My sister came to visit. We were talking and tea was there and everything. I forgot the evening tablet. My husband said did you take it and then I remembered no.

**Interviewer:** What did you do

**Respondent:** I called my daughter. She said don't take anything extra just check what time it is and she told me. She knows these things.

**Interviewer:** So you prefer to ask your family before doing anything

**Respondent:** Yes yes. Why I will take chance. They know more. My daughter keeps the prescription also. If doctor changes anything she tells me this one is stopped this one is new.

**Interviewer:** Do you keep the medicines yourself

**Respondent:** My daughter keeps most of them. Some are on the table. I don't like keeping everything here because sometimes I move things and then I don't know where I put it. So she arranges.

**Interviewer:** Who decides which medicine you take

**Respondent:** Doctor decides. Then family gives it to me. My husband knows some. My daughter knows more. I just take what they give.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever change a medicine because someone in your family suggests another one

**Respondent:** No no without doctor no. But if somebody in family says this medicine is not good or somebody had problem with it then we ask doctor. We don't just take.

**Interviewer:** What about relatives

**Respondent:** Relatives tell many things. Everybody tells something. This medicine is good this medicine is bad. I listen but I don't know what is right. So I tell my daughter. She checks.

**Interviewer:** What if several relatives say the same medicine is good

**Respondent:** Then I will feel maybe it is good. If so many people say it helped them then I will remember that. But still my daughter will ask doctor. I won't take only because they said.

**Interviewer:** Do you discuss medicines with other women when you meet

**Respondent:** Yes of course. We talk about these things. Somebody says my knee medicine is good. Somebody says this doctor gave me this. We talk. But for myself I ask family.

**Interviewer:** Do you know all your own medicines

**Respondent:** No. I know by colour and packet. If my daughter says blue strip morning I know. If you put three strips in front of me I can tell which one I take because I see it every day. But the name I cannot say.

**Interviewer:** Can you read the label

**Respondent:** Big letters yes. Small letters no. I need glasses. Without glasses I can see the packet but not the writing.

**Interviewer:** What if you don't have your glasses

**Respondent:** Then I ask husband or daughter. Pharmacy also reads for us sometimes.

**Interviewer:** Can you read your prescription

**Respondent:** With glasses maybe. Doctor writing is difficult sometimes. I don't understand all. My daughter takes a photo also. She keeps it in phone.

**Interviewer:** What kind of phone do you use

**Respondent:** Touchscreen smartphone. My daughter gave it. I can call. I watch YouTube sometimes. WhatsApp also I use little. But I don't know all these things.

**Interviewer:** Do you use any app for medicines

**Respondent:** No. No medicine app.

**Interviewer:** Why not

**Respondent:** Nobody has put one. Also I don't know how. If daughter puts and shows me maybe I can. But I don't want to do too many things on phone.

**Interviewer:** What would you want in a medicine app

**Respondent:** It should tell me when to take. Big letters. Maybe picture of medicine. If it shows picture I can know this is the tablet. And voice also.

**Interviewer:** What kind of voice

**Respondent:** Just say your medicine time. Take this medicine. Something simple. Not too much talking.

**Interviewer:** Would you want your daughter to get the reminder too

**Respondent:** Yes that is good. If I don't take then she should know. Sometimes I say yes yes I took it and then later I realise maybe I didn't. If she gets message she can ask me.

**Interviewer:** Would you like the app to call you

**Respondent:** No call maybe not. Sound is enough. If it keeps ringing I will take it. But if daughter gets message also then good.

**Interviewer:** What if the medicine is finished

**Respondent:** That should tell daughter. She buys it. I don't remember how many tablets are left. She checks.

**Interviewer:** So your daughter manages the medicines

**Respondent:** Yes mostly. She knows when to buy. She knows doctor appointment. She keeps the prescription. I just follow.

**Interviewer:** What does your husband do

**Respondent:** He reminds me. Sometimes he gives me the medicine. If I am in kitchen he says take it now. He also has his own medicines so we remind each other.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever miss medicine when you are outside

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. If we go to somebody's house I may not carry it. Then daughter gets angry and says why you didn't take it. I say I forgot. Then she says keep it in your bag. She tells me same thing many times.

**Interviewer:** Does going to a family gathering affect your routine

**Respondent:** Yes. At home I know everything. At someone's house food time changes. Tea comes different time. People are talking. Then medicine gets forgotten. But usually someone from family reminds me.

**Interviewer:** If other people around you are taking medicine does that remind you

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. Somebody says I have to take my tablet and then I say oh mine also. So it happens. But mostly family.

**Interviewer:** If a group of people tells you some medicine is good what do you think

**Respondent:** I think maybe it is good because they have taken it. But I don't know for me. One medicine may be good for one person and not for another. So I ask my daughter.

**Interviewer:** So you depend on your family more than the group

**Respondent:** Yes yes family. They know my doctor and my medicines. Other people don't know.

**Interviewer:** If your daughter was not there for one day who would help you

**Respondent:** My husband. If he also goes then my son. They know enough. Daughter has written everything also.

**Interviewer:** Where is that written

**Respondent:** In her phone. Maybe paper also. I don't know exactly. She keeps all.

**Interviewer:** Would you use an app if your daughter set it up for you

**Respondent:** Yes. If she sets everything I can use. I don't want to enter medicine names and times. I don't know all that. She can do it once.

**Interviewer:** What should happen after you take the medicine

**Respondent:** Maybe I press one button. Taken. That's all. But button should be big. Sometimes these phone buttons are very small.

**Interviewer:** What if you forget to press the button but you actually took the medicine

**Respondent:** Then daughter should not think I didn't take. Maybe there should be some way to say I took it. If I forget to press then she can ask me.

**Interviewer:** Would taking a photo of the medicine help

**Respondent:** Yes. If I don't know the name, photo helps. My daughter also takes photos of medicine. If app can show same photo then good.

**Interviewer:** What is difficult about using the phone

**Respondent:** Small letters. Too many things on one screen. Sometimes I press wrong place. Then something opens and I don't know how to come back. That is difficult.

**Interviewer:** So what would make it easier

**Respondent:** Big letters. Less things. One medicine at one time. Clear picture. Maybe voice. And family should be able to set it.

**Interviewer:** Do you want colour also

**Respondent:** Yes maybe. Morning one can be one colour and night another. But I don't know if that is necessary. Picture is better.

**Interviewer:** What about reminders when you are away from home

**Respondent:** Yes it should work there also. If I am at my sister's house it should remind. Family should know also if I am not taking.

**Interviewer:** Do you have any difficulty understanding what the doctor says

**Respondent:** Sometimes. If doctor talks fast I don't understand everything. My daughter comes with me sometimes. She remembers and tells me later. If I go alone I forget some things.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever ask the pharmacy for help

**Respondent:** Yes if there is confusion. They know the medicine. They read the packet. We ask them sometimes.

**Interviewer:** What is the hardest part about taking medicines

**Respondent:** Remembering when there are changes. If doctor changes one tablet then I get confused. Earlier this one was there now another one. Family has to tell me again and again. If routine is same then no problem.

**Interviewer:** If an app could help with that what would you want

**Respondent:** If medicine changes, it should show new one clearly. Maybe say this is new medicine. Old one stopped. But my daughter should put it. I don't want to change myself.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything else you would like in the app

**Respondent:** Maybe family can see everything. Which medicine taken which not taken. And when medicine is finishing. And doctor number maybe. That's enough. Don't make too much.

**Interviewer:** Would you want it to speak differently to you and your daughter

**Respondent:** With me simple. Just tell me what to do. With daughter it can show more details. She understands phone more. I don't need all the details.

**Interviewer:** Anything else about your medicine routine

**Respondent:** No. Mostly family helps. I don't remember all the names but I know my tablets when I see them. If family is there then no problem. If nobody is there then I have to manage but I feel better when somebody checks.

**Interviewer:** Okay thank you

**Respondent:** Yes okay.

# Interview Transcript

**Respondent: Male, 67 years, farmer**

**Interviewer:** Okay first can you tell me little about yourself

**Respondent:** I am 67. I do farming. Now I don't do all the heavy work like before but I still go to the field. I have land there. Morning I go and see everything. My son also helps now.

**Interviewer:** How many people are there in your family

**Respondent:** Six people. Me my wife my son his wife and two grandchildren. All staying together.

**Interviewer:** Does everyone take medicine

**Respondent:** No no. Mostly me and my wife. Both of us have diabetes medicine. Other people take if there is some problem but daily medicine is ours.

**Interviewer:** You and your wife both take diabetes medicine

**Respondent:** Yes both. Mine is one medicine mostly. Wife also has hers. Sometimes other medicine comes also but diabetes one is regular.

**Interviewer:** Do you take your medicine after seeing a doctor

**Respondent:** Yes doctor gives it. We go to doctor and he tells which one. We don't take anything without asking. Doctor sees the sugar and then gives.

**Interviewer:** How do you know which medicine to take

**Respondent:** I know the strip. I don't know all the name. Name is difficult. But I know this is my tablet. My wife knows hers also.

**Interviewer:** Can you read the prescription

**Respondent:** No no. That I cannot read properly. Doctor writes and gives. I look at it but I cannot understand what is written.

**Interviewer:** Then when you go to buy medicine what happens

**Respondent:** I take the paper to pharmacy. They read it. They know. I give the paper and they give the medicine. Sometimes they tell me morning one night one. Like that.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever get confused between medicines

**Respondent:** Sometimes if there are new ones. Old medicine I know by looking. New strip I don't know. Then I ask pharmacy or my son.

**Interviewer:** Who reminds you to take medicine

**Respondent:** I remember myself mostly. Nobody sits and tells me every day. I know after food I have to take. It is habit now.

**Interviewer:** Your wife reminds you

**Respondent:** Sometimes. If I am busy or I forget then she says you have not taken. Same I also tell her. We both remind each other sometimes.

**Interviewer:** Do you write the timing anywhere

**Respondent:** No no. Nothing written. I remember.

**Interviewer:** Do you use phone alarm

**Respondent:** No. I don't use that. I don't have mobile also.

**Interviewer:** You don't have a mobile phone

**Respondent:** No. My son has one. I don't need one much. If somebody wants me they call my son. If I need to talk I use his phone.

**Interviewer:** Why did you not take a mobile phone for yourself

**Respondent:** What will I do with it. I go field. Phone will be in pocket and soil and all. Son has phone. If I need call I use his. I don't understand all those phone things also.

**Interviewer:** When you go to the field do you take your medicine with you

**Respondent:** If I am going for long time yes sometimes. If I am just going for two three hours then no. I come back and take. Sometimes work becomes longer than I thought. Then I may take late.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened before

**Respondent:** Yes. Farming work doesn't have fixed time always. Water problem comes, labour comes late, some work is there. You think I will come in one hour and then three hours go. Then medicine timing can move.

**Interviewer:** What do you do then

**Respondent:** I come home and ask my wife. If I am not sure I call son. I don't take double. No no. That is not good.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever forget completely

**Respondent:** Very rarely. Mostly timing changes. Because daily I know I have to take it. But when there is work or some gathering then mind goes somewhere else.

**Interviewer:** What kind of gatherings do you go to

**Respondent:** Village people meet. Tea shop. Sometimes farmers sit together after field work. There is also some meeting in the village. We talk about crop and water and fertilizer and also medicine. At our age medicine also becomes one topic.

**Interviewer:** What do people say about medicines

**Respondent:** All kinds of things. One says this tablet is good for sugar. One says his sugar became better after taking some medicine. Another says don't take that one. People have different talk.

**Interviewer:** Do you listen to them

**Respondent:** Yes of course. We sit together so we listen. If one farmer says something worked for him then I remember it. If many people say same thing then I think maybe it is useful.

**Interviewer:** Even if someone has told you that the medicine may not be good

**Respondent:** If somebody tells bad thing then I ask what happened. But if five people are taking it and saying they are fine then I don't just believe one bad story. I will ask more. I may ask doctor also.

**Interviewer:** If many people in your group take the same medicine would you feel more comfortable taking it

**Respondent:** Yes yes. If people I know are taking it then I feel more trust. You see them every day. If they say it helped them then you think maybe it can help me also.

**Interviewer:** Would you change your medicine just because they told you

**Respondent:** No no. Change means doctor has to say. But I will take the name and ask. If doctor says okay then maybe I will take. If doctor says no then no.

**Interviewer:** So the group influences you but doctor makes the final decision

**Respondent:** Yes. Group tells what they experienced. Doctor tells what is for me. Both are different. But if many people are saying one thing then I will definitely ask about it.

**Interviewer:** Why do you trust people from your group

**Respondent:** Because we work same kind of work. We have same problems sometimes. We meet every day. If one man says his sugar was high and this helped him then we understand. He is not trying to sell me anything.

**Interviewer:** Do you trust a medicine more if your friends are already using it

**Respondent:** Yes little more. I know they are using. If they are okay then I think okay. But I still ask doctor. I have been taking medicines for many years so I know not to simply take anything.

**Interviewer:** Does your wife listen to these discussions also

**Respondent:** Yes she hears from women in the neighbourhood. But she is more careful. She asks our son. I also tell her what people said. Then she says ask doctor first.

**Interviewer:** Who usually makes decisions about your wife's medicine

**Respondent:** She tells me and we ask son or doctor. My son is more educated. He can read the prescription. So he checks.

**Interviewer:** So your son helps with the medicine information

**Respondent:** Yes. He can read all those names. I cannot. If something changes he tells me. Pharmacy also helps.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever take your wife's medicine by mistake

**Respondent:** No no. We keep separately. Her strip is there and mine is there. If everything is mixed then problem can happen. So we keep separate.

**Interviewer:** Where do you keep your medicines

**Respondent:** In one small box in the room. Wife's and mine separate. Regular ones are easy to find. Sometimes she keeps hers somewhere else and then I ask where is it.

**Interviewer:** Does your family help you with buying medicines

**Respondent:** Yes. If son is going to market he buys. Sometimes I go to pharmacy myself. If there is a new prescription then son usually goes because he can read it.

**Interviewer:** Do you go to doctor alone

**Respondent:** Sometimes. But if medicine is changing then I like son to come. He remembers what doctor says. I hear also but he understands more.

**Interviewer:** Why is that important

**Respondent:** Doctor talks fast sometimes. And there are names and timings. I can forget. Son writes or keeps photo. Then at home he tells me again.

**Interviewer:** Do you keep photos of the prescription

**Respondent:** Son keeps. In his phone. I don't know how to take photo properly. He takes and keeps.

**Interviewer:** What happens if your son is not around

**Respondent:** Pharmacy person helps. They know our medicine also because we buy from same place. Wife also knows my regular tablet.

**Interviewer:** Do you think the pharmacy helps you a lot

**Respondent:** Yes. We have been going there many years. They know us. If I show strip they understand. If prescription is there they read it.

**Interviewer:** Do you use any reminder app

**Respondent:** No no. I don't have phone so how will I use.

**Interviewer:** If your son gave you a smartphone would you use a medicine app

**Respondent:** Hmm maybe. But I don't know. Phone itself is difficult for me. My son has tried to show me but I press wrong thing and something else comes. Then I leave it.

**Interviewer:** What if the app was very simple

**Respondent:** If it only tells me medicine time maybe I can use. But somebody should set it for me. I don't know how to put all the medicine names.

**Interviewer:** What would you want the app to show

**Respondent:** Picture of tablet. Big letters. Morning or night. That's enough. If it says too much I won't read.

**Interviewer:** Would you want it to make a sound

**Respondent:** Yes. Sound can help. In field I may not hear if it is very small. It should make a proper sound. Maybe again after some time.

**Interviewer:** Would you want your son to also get a reminder

**Respondent:** Yes yes. That will be good. If I don't take it he can know. He can call and say baba take medicine. That is easier.

**Interviewer:** What if you don't press anything after taking the medicine

**Respondent:** Then son can call and ask. But don't make it a problem if I forget pressing. I may take medicine and forget the phone. The medicine is more important.

**Interviewer:** Would a medicine photo help you

**Respondent:** Yes. Because I don't read name. If I see the photo I can recognise. This is my tablet. That will help.

**Interviewer:** What if the app could take a photo of the tablet and tell you which medicine it is

**Respondent:** That is good. Very good. If it can do properly. Sometimes tablets look same. If it tells wrong then problem. So it should be checked by family also.

**Interviewer:** Would you trust it more if your son set it up

**Respondent:** Yes. Definitely. Son knows these things. If he says this is the medicine and this is the time then I will use. If some phone itself tells me I don't know whether to trust it.

**Interviewer:** What if other farmers in your group also start using the same app

**Respondent:** Then I will be more interested. If they say it is good and they are using it then I will ask my son to put it in my phone. If nobody I know uses it then maybe I won't bother.

**Interviewer:** So seeing others use it matters

**Respondent:** Yes. We learn like that also. If one farmer uses some new seed and it works then others try. Same thing. If people around us are using something and it is helping them then we trust it more.

**Interviewer:** Would you change medicine if your whole group said another one is better

**Respondent:** I would ask about it for sure. I would not directly change. I will tell my son. Then ask doctor. But yes if whole group is saying it I will take it seriously. I will not say no without checking.

**Interviewer:** What happens when you are at a village gathering during your medicine time

**Respondent:** If I remember then I take. Sometimes I don't carry it. Then one of the others says I have to take my medicine and I remember mine also. We all have this problem. Somebody always reminds somebody.

**Interviewer:** So the group can help you remember

**Respondent:** Yes yes. Not directly always. Somebody takes his tablet and then I remember. Or somebody asks have you taken yours. Then I remember.

**Interviewer:** Does your wife remind you more or your friends

**Respondent:** Wife at home. Friends outside. Both. Wife knows my daily time. Friends only when we are together.

**Interviewer:** Which is more useful

**Respondent:** Wife. Because she is there every day. She knows if I ate or not. Friends don't know that. But when I am outside friends are useful.

**Interviewer:** What is difficult about your medicine routine

**Respondent:** The medicine itself is not difficult. Remembering time when work changes is difficult. Farming doesn't follow clock always. If water comes you have to go. If rain comes work changes. If someone calls you then you go. Then medicine time goes here and there.

**Interviewer:** Does that happen more during busy farming season

**Respondent:** Yes. During harvesting especially. Whole day is different. We start early and come late. Sometimes food also late. Medicine also becomes late.

**Interviewer:** What do you do during those days

**Respondent:** Wife tells me. Son also calls sometimes. If I know I will be outside I carry the strip. I am trying to remember better now.

**Interviewer:** What would help you most

**Respondent:** Somebody reminding. Either wife or son. Phone reminder also if it is simple. And medicine should be kept in one place.

**Interviewer:** If you had an app what other feature would you want

**Respondent:** Maybe tell when medicine is finishing. Because sometimes I don't check. If son gets message then he can buy. Doctor date also maybe. But don't put too much.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to tell you about medicines that other people are taking

**Respondent:** No no. That can create confusion. If it is my medicine then tell me. Other medicines I can hear from friends separately. Then I ask doctor.

**Interviewer:** What about health advice in the app

**Respondent:** Simple advice maybe. But not too much. I don't read much on phone. If it says eat this don't eat this all day then I won't see.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer voice

**Respondent:** Yes voice is better for me. I can hear. Reading is difficult because I cannot read prescription also. If somebody tells me then I understand.

**Interviewer:** Who should control the app

**Respondent:** Son or wife. They should put the medicine. I can only take when it tells me. If I have to change the setting myself then no.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to remind you when you are outside the house

**Respondent:** Yes. That is where it is useful. At home wife already tells me. Outside there is no one. So phone can tell there.

**Interviewer:** What if you are working in the field and don't hear it

**Respondent:** Then it should ring again. Maybe after ten minutes. Or son can know. Because field work can be noisy. Water pump is running, people are talking. Small sound I won't hear.

**Interviewer:** What if the app reminds you but you don't have the medicine with you

**Respondent:** Then it should maybe say carry medicine also before going. But how it knows I am going outside I don't know. Anyway if it can remind before time that is useful.

**Interviewer:** Do you think technology can really help older people

**Respondent:** Yes but only if somebody sets it. Older people don't know all these things. You give too many buttons then finished. We won't use. Keep it simple and family can manage.

**Interviewer:** If your son says this app is good would you trust it

**Respondent:** Yes. If son checks and says use this then I will use. Children know these phone things better. I trust him for that.

**Interviewer:** And if your farmer friends say the same app is good

**Respondent:** Then even more. If son says and friends also say then okay. I will feel it is useful.

**Interviewer:** So family and community both have influence

**Respondent:** Yes. Family knows me. Friends know what works for them. We listen to both. But medicine doctor decides.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything else that makes taking medicine difficult

**Respondent:** Sometimes medicine looks different when pharmacy gives another brand. Then I ask what is this. If the packet changes I don't know. If app shows photo then maybe I can know it is same medicine or not.

**Interviewer:** That would be useful

**Respondent:** Yes. Because names are difficult. Picture I can understand.

**Interviewer:** What should the app not do

**Respondent:** Don't make me type. Don't make small letters. Don't give too many medicines together on one screen. Don't make too much sound. And don't tell me to change medicine myself.

**Interviewer:** How should it speak to you

**Respondent:** Simple. Like son telling me. Take this medicine now. That's it. Not big words. I don't understand those big phone words.

**Interviewer:** If you could have one thing in the app only what would you choose

**Respondent:** Reminder. Just reminder. If I am outside and it tells me then enough. Family can handle the rest.

**Interviewer:** And if family could see whether you took it

**Respondent:** That is also good. Actually that can be second thing. Son can check. If I didn't take he can call. My wife can also know.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to tell me

**Respondent:** No no. Medicine is part of life now. We don't think too much when same medicine every day. Problem comes when routine changes or medicine changes. Then family helps. And people around also tell what they are doing. We listen to everybody but finally we ask doctor.

**Interviewer:** Okay thank you very much

**Respondent:** Yes yes okay. You ask many questions today.

**Interviewer:** It was useful

**Respondent:** Good good. If it helps then fine.

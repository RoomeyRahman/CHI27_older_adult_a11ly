# Interview Transcript

**Respondent: Female, 71 years, widow**

**Interviewer:** Okay first can you tell me about yourself

**Respondent:** I am 71 now. My husband passed away some years back. Now I stay with my son and his family. My daughter also lives not very far. She comes often. I am mostly at home now.

**Interviewer:** How many people stay in your house

**Respondent:** Four. My son his wife their child and me. Four people.

**Interviewer:** Do you take medicine every day

**Respondent:** Yes yes. Every day I have to take. There are some medicines in morning and some at night. Sometimes doctor gives one more also for some days.

**Interviewer:** How many medicines do you take now

**Respondent:** Hmm I don't know exactly. Three or four I think. My son knows. I don't remember the names properly.

**Interviewer:** You don't remember the names

**Respondent:** No. I know the medicine when I see it. This strip I know. If you ask me the name I may say wrong. My son knows all the names.

**Interviewer:** How do you remember which medicine to take

**Respondent:** My son tells me. Morning he keeps them there. Sometimes my daughter also tells me. I also remember some because I have been taking them for long time. But if you ask me which one at what time I like to check with them.

**Interviewer:** Do you keep the prescription

**Respondent:** My son keeps it. I had some papers before but I lost one also. Then son said you don't keep these papers give it to me. Now he keeps.

**Interviewer:** Do you read the prescription yourself

**Respondent:** No not properly. I can see the writing but I cannot understand what is written. Doctor writing is also small. My son reads it.

**Interviewer:** So when you buy medicines who takes the prescription

**Respondent:** Son mostly. If I go then I take the paper and give to pharmacy. They know. Sometimes they know our medicine also because we go there regularly.

**Interviewer:** Do you take medicine only after seeing doctor

**Respondent:** Yes. Whatever doctor gives I take. I don't like taking anything myself. People tell many things but medicine I ask family first.

**Interviewer:** Who reminds you to take it

**Respondent:** Mostly my son. Sometimes daughter calls. My daughter says Ma did you take your medicine. My son also asks. If they are busy then I remember myself.

**Interviewer:** Does your daughter call every day

**Respondent:** Not every day for medicine. But she calls almost every day. If she knows I have some new medicine then she asks more. She worries.

**Interviewer:** What happens if nobody reminds you

**Respondent:** If the timing is same I usually remember. But sometimes I get busy with cooking or talking with neighbour and then I forget the time. Then later I remember maybe I have not taken.

**Interviewer:** What do you do then

**Respondent:** I ask my son. I don't take two. I ask first. He tells me what to do.

**Interviewer:** Has this happened recently

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. Few days back I was talking with my neighbour in the afternoon. We were sitting outside. Then I came inside and saw the tablet still there. I had forgotten.

**Interviewer:** Did you call your son

**Respondent:** He was in the house that day. I asked him. He said yes you have not taken yet. Then I took it. He keeps telling me don't forget.

**Interviewer:** Do you think you depend on your son for medicines

**Respondent:** Yes. I depend on him for many things now. Medicine also. He goes to doctor. He buys medicine. He keeps the papers. He knows which medicine is finished. So yes he manages more.

**Interviewer:** What about your daughter

**Respondent:** She also helps. She calls me. Sometimes she comes and arranges the tablets. If son is not there I call her.

**Interviewer:** If both your son and daughter are not there what do you do

**Respondent:** Then I manage the regular one myself. But if there is any new medicine I wait or call them. I don't know which is which sometimes.

**Interviewer:** Do you ask neighbours

**Respondent:** If it is just something small maybe I ask. But for medicine no. I ask family. Neighbour does not know my medicine.

**Interviewer:** What if a neighbour tells you a medicine is very good

**Respondent:** I listen. People tell these things. If she says I took this and I became better then I listen. But I will tell my son first.

**Interviewer:** If many people tell you the same medicine is good

**Respondent:** Then I will think maybe it is good. If many people are taking it then there must be some reason. I will tell my son. Maybe he will ask doctor.

**Interviewer:** Would you take it if everyone around you was taking it

**Respondent:** I would want to know about it yes. If my neighbours are taking it and saying good things then I will trust it more than if one person just says it. But I won't take without asking my son or doctor.

**Interviewer:** So the group can influence you

**Respondent:** Yes little bit. If everybody says same thing then you think about it. We talk about medicine a lot. At this age what else. One has BP one has sugar one has joint pain. Everybody has some medicine.

**Interviewer:** But you would still ask family

**Respondent:** Yes yes. Family first. My son knows what I take. Others don't know.

**Interviewer:** What if your son says don't take it but everyone else says it is good

**Respondent:** Then I won't take. Son knows my doctor and my medicines. Maybe he will ask doctor. If doctor says okay then okay. If not then no.

**Interviewer:** What if your daughter says it is okay but your son says no

**Respondent:** Then they will talk between themselves. I won't decide. I will say you people decide and tell me. Why I will take tension.

**Interviewer:** Do you sometimes get confused when medicines change

**Respondent:** Yes. That is the main problem. When doctor changes one medicine, the new strip looks different. I don't know whether this is the old one or new one. Son has to tell me again.

**Interviewer:** What does he do

**Respondent:** He puts the new medicine separately. Sometimes he writes morning and night on paper. I look at that. Then I know.

**Interviewer:** Does he put the tablets in a box

**Respondent:** Sometimes. But I don't always use the box. I know the strips better. If it is in the strip I can see the tablet and know.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever take the wrong medicine

**Respondent:** No no. I am careful. If I don't know I ask. Wrong medicine is dangerous. I may forget timing but I don't want to take a different tablet.

**Interviewer:** Can you read the medicine packets

**Respondent:** Some things. Big writing I can see. Small writing I cannot. I need my glasses. Even with glasses sometimes I ask my son.

**Interviewer:** Why do you ask him if you can read

**Respondent:** Because he knows. I can read some letters but medicine names are difficult. Why take chance. He tells me this is yours.

**Interviewer:** Do you have a phone

**Respondent:** Yes I have one phone. My son gave me. It is smartphone but I don't know everything.

**Interviewer:** What do you use it for

**Respondent:** Calls mostly. WhatsApp. Sometimes I watch videos. My daughter sends me things. I can answer calls. Other things I don't know much.

**Interviewer:** Do you use any medicine app

**Respondent:** No no. I don't have any medicine app.

**Interviewer:** Have your children ever shown you one

**Respondent:** They have shown some apps. I don't remember. They say press here press there. Then I forget. I don't know these things.

**Interviewer:** If there was an app that reminded you when to take medicine would you use it

**Respondent:** If my son puts it then yes maybe. If I have to put everything myself then no. I won't know what to do.

**Interviewer:** What would you want the app to show

**Respondent:** It should show my medicine picture. Then I know. And say morning or night. Big letters. That's enough.

**Interviewer:** Would you want a sound

**Respondent:** Yes sound is good. If it makes sound I will know. Sometimes I don't look at phone. Sound will make me look.

**Interviewer:** What if you don't hear it

**Respondent:** Then it can ring again. Or tell my son. If I miss it then he can ask me.

**Interviewer:** Would you want your son to get a message if you missed your medicine

**Respondent:** Yes. That is good. He can call me. I may forget sometimes. He can remind.

**Interviewer:** Would that make you feel like someone is watching you

**Respondent:** No no. He already asks. It is okay. He is my son. I feel safe when he checks.

**Interviewer:** What if the app gives you a reminder but you already took the medicine

**Respondent:** Then I should be able to say taken. But sometimes I may forget to press. So son should not think I didn't take only because I didn't press.

**Interviewer:** How could it make it easy

**Respondent:** One big button. Press and finished. Don't make many buttons. I will get confused.

**Interviewer:** Would you like the app to speak

**Respondent:** Yes. Speaking is good. If it says medicine time then I know. Sometimes my glasses are not with me.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to tell you the medicine name also

**Respondent:** Yes name and picture. But don't make me read long information. I only need to know which tablet and when.

**Interviewer:** What if the app can take a photo of the medicine and recognise it

**Respondent:** That will be useful. I can show photo to son also. If app tells which medicine then maybe easier. But I would still ask son. I don't trust myself with these new things.

**Interviewer:** Would you trust the app more if your son set it up

**Respondent:** Yes definitely. If he puts the medicines then I know he checked. If some app itself tells me I don't know where it got it from.

**Interviewer:** What if your daughter sets it up instead

**Respondent:** Also okay. Both children help me. Whoever has time can set it.

**Interviewer:** What about medicine finishing reminder

**Respondent:** Yes that is very useful. I don't count tablets. Sometimes I suddenly see only two left. Then I tell son. If phone tells him before that then better.

**Interviewer:** Would you want doctor appointment reminders too

**Respondent:** Yes maybe. Doctor date I sometimes forget. Daughter usually tells me. If phone tells me then good.

**Interviewer:** Do you want many features in the app

**Respondent:** No no. Too many things I won't understand. Medicine reminder is enough. Medicine picture. Family message. Medicine finishing. That's enough.

**Interviewer:** What is the hardest part of taking your medicines

**Respondent:** When something changes. If same medicine every day then I know. New medicine makes confusion. Also if I go to my daughter's house or somebody comes then timing changes.

**Interviewer:** Do you forget more when you are outside

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. At home I know the place. Outside I may not have medicine with me. My daughter always says keep it in your bag. I say yes yes. Sometimes I still forget.

**Interviewer:** What happens at family gatherings

**Respondent:** Everybody is talking. Food is coming. Children are running. I forget. Then somebody from family asks did you take your medicine. Then I remember.

**Interviewer:** Do other older women at gatherings talk about medicines

**Respondent:** Yes very much. One says this medicine helped her. Another says her doctor changed this. We talk. Sometimes somebody says don't take this one it is not good. Then I listen but I don't decide from that.

**Interviewer:** If all of them said one medicine was very good

**Respondent:** I would remember the name. I would tell my son. If so many women are taking and saying good then maybe I will ask doctor about it. I will not simply take it.

**Interviewer:** Why would you ask your son first

**Respondent:** Because after my husband died he looks after these things. He knows my medicines. He knows the doctor. I don't want to make mistake.

**Interviewer:** Do you feel more comfortable when your family is involved

**Respondent:** Yes. I feel more comfortable. If I am alone and something happens I get worried. If son knows what I take then I feel okay.

**Interviewer:** If you had to manage all your medicines yourself would that be difficult

**Respondent:** Yes maybe. I can take them if everything is kept properly. But buying, prescription, new medicine, timing, all these things I may get confused.

**Interviewer:** Would you like someone to call you instead of an app reminder

**Respondent:** Call is also okay. But children are busy. Phone reminder can do first. If I don't take then child can call.

**Interviewer:** So app first family after that

**Respondent:** Yes something like that. App reminds me. If I still don't take then family knows.

**Interviewer:** What if the app is in English

**Respondent:** If very difficult English then no. Simple words I can understand little. Better if it speaks also. My son can set it.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer a local language

**Respondent:** Yes if it can speak in language I understand then better. Reading is not always easy.

**Interviewer:** What should the app not do

**Respondent:** Don't make me type. Don't make me remember passwords. Don't make too many screens. Don't show too much writing. I will give it to my son then.

**Interviewer:** If your son says the app is good but your neighbours say it is not good what will you do

**Respondent:** I will listen to son. But I will ask why they are saying bad. If neighbours have used it maybe I will hear them also. Then son can check. Family knows my situation.

**Interviewer:** So who do you trust most for medicine

**Respondent:** My children. Then doctor. Other people I listen to. If many people say same thing I will ask about it. But I won't take medicine only because people said.

**Interviewer:** Do you think you remember medicines better now than before

**Respondent:** No. Before I remembered everything. Now sometimes I forget small things. Medicine I try to remember because it is important. But family helps.

**Interviewer:** Does your daughter ever prepare your medicines

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. If I am staying with her she keeps them in morning and night. She says this one now this one later. I follow.

**Interviewer:** Do you prefer when she does that

**Respondent:** Yes. It makes it easy. I don't have to think. She knows what doctor gave me.

**Interviewer:** What would make a medicine app feel comfortable for you

**Respondent:** If my children control it. If I only have to look and take medicine. Big picture. Voice. One button. That's comfortable.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to say

**Respondent:** No no. At this age family is important. Earlier I used to manage everything myself. Now children help. For medicine also I feel better when they know. I can take the medicine myself but somebody should keep an eye.

**Interviewer:** Okay thank you

**Respondent:** Yes okay. You ask so many things about medicine. But yes this is how it is now.

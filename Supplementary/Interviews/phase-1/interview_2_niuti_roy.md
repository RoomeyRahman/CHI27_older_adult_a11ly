# 2. Niuti Roy

**Interviewer:** Alright we will now talk about your medicine intake. First can you tell me your name and age and a little about yourself

**Respondent:** My name is Niuti Roy. I am 46 years old. I live with my family. I take one medicine. Only one regular medicine.

**Interviewer:** Do you take it every day

**Respondent:** Yes almost every day.

**Interviewer:** Do you follow any method to remember the medicine timing

**Respondent:** No. I don't use any method. I just remember. Sometimes.

**Interviewer:** Sometimes

**Respondent:** Sometimes I remember. Sometimes I forget. There is no proper method.

**Interviewer:** Have you tried an alarm

**Respondent:** No I did not. I thought I will remember it myself. But sometimes I don't.

**Interviewer:** When do you usually forget

**Respondent:** When I am busy. Or when I go somewhere. If routine changes then I forget. At home also sometimes.

**Interviewer:** What happens when you forget

**Respondent:** Sometimes I remember after some time. Sometimes I remember when somebody asks. Then I take it.

**Interviewer:** Who asks you

**Respondent:** Family. Somebody at home asks if I took medicine. Then I remember.

**Interviewer:** So your family helps you remember

**Respondent:** Yes. Family helps. They know I take medicine. So sometimes they remind me.

**Interviewer:** Do you keep the medicine in a particular place

**Respondent:** Yes in one place. Usually where we keep medicines. But if I move it then I can forget where I kept it.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever missed a dose completely

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. Not every day. But yes it happened.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about one recent time

**Respondent:** I think one day I was going somewhere. I was getting ready and many things were happening. I forgot the medicine. Later I remembered. By then time had passed.

**Interviewer:** Were you alone

**Respondent:** No family was there. But everybody was busy. Nobody remembered either.

**Interviewer:** What about travelling

**Respondent:** Yes travelling is also a problem. I don't remember sometimes. I may remember after I have already left.

**Interviewer:** Do you carry the medicine with you

**Respondent:** Sometimes. Sometimes I forget to carry it. If I carry it, I may still forget to take it.

**Interviewer:** So there are two problems

**Respondent:** Yes. Carrying also and remembering also.

**Interviewer:** Have you used technology for this forgetfulness

**Respondent:** No. Nothing.

**Interviewer:** No alarm no app

**Respondent:** No. Nothing.

**Interviewer:** Are you familiar with smartphones

**Respondent:** Yes. We use smartphones.

**Interviewer:** Do you use them for calls and messages

**Respondent:** Yes. Calls, messages, WhatsApp, normal things. Nothing very difficult.

**Interviewer:** Do you also know button phones

**Respondent:** Yes.

**Interviewer:** Who helps if there is something difficult on the phone

**Respondent:** Family usually. My family can help. If there is some setting I don't know, somebody shows me.

**Interviewer:** How do you read your prescription

**Respondent:** I cannot read it properly. The writing is difficult. So I get the medicine from the pharmacy.

**Interviewer:** You take the prescription to the pharmacy

**Respondent:** Yes. We give the prescription there. They understand it and give the medicine.

**Interviewer:** Have you used any technology to read the prescription

**Respondent:** No.

**Interviewer:** Would you use it if it was easy

**Respondent:** Yes. Of course. If it is easy then yes. It can help.

**Interviewer:** What would you want it to tell you

**Respondent:** Medicine name. Dose. Time. Something simple. If it tells too many things then maybe I won't understand.

**Interviewer:** Would a photo of the medicine help

**Respondent:** Yes it can help. If I see the picture then I can match it.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever had any medicine problems

**Respondent:** Sometimes yes. Not very big problem. If I feel something wrong then I ask my doctor.

**Interviewer:** Do you call your doctor yourself

**Respondent:** Yes usually. Sometimes family also talks to the doctor.

**Interviewer:** Do you also ask the pharmacist

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. If I want to know about the medicine I ask them. They know the medicine.

**Interviewer:** Who do you trust more

**Respondent:** For serious things doctor. For small things pharmacist also. Family also tells me what to do.

**Interviewer:** When family tells you that a medicine is good do you tend to trust it

**Respondent:** Yes. Family has more experience with these things. If someone in family already knows the medicine then I feel okay about it.

**Interviewer:** What if many people in your family say the same medicine is good

**Respondent:** Then I will trust it more. If many people have used it then I think it is probably okay.

**Interviewer:** Even if you heard something controversial about it

**Respondent:** Maybe yes. If I hear one bad thing but my family says they have used it and it is fine then I will believe family more. But if doctor says no then I will listen to doctor.

**Interviewer:** So family opinion has a lot of effect

**Respondent:** Yes. Family opinion matters. They are close to me. They know me. So I listen.

**Interviewer:** Does this happen with medicine decisions also

**Respondent:** Yes. Sometimes I don't remember the medicine name but family remembers. They say this is the one you take. Then I take that.

**Interviewer:** So sometimes they know your medicine better than you

**Respondent:** Yes yes. That happens. I know the packet maybe. But name I don't remember always.

**Interviewer:** Who usually buys your medicine

**Respondent:** Mostly family. Sometimes they go to the pharmacy. Sometimes I go.

**Interviewer:** Who checks whether it is the correct medicine

**Respondent:** Usually whoever goes to the pharmacy. Sometimes family checks. They look at the old packet.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything difficult about the routine

**Respondent:** The remembering part. The remembering is difficult. Everything else is easy.

**Interviewer:** What would help most

**Respondent:** A reminder. Maybe a voice reminder. Or a phone call kind of sound. Something that I cannot miss.

**Interviewer:** Do you prefer voice or text

**Respondent:** Voice. I think voice is better.

**Interviewer:** Why

**Respondent:** Because I don't always look at the phone. If there is a text I may see it later. Voice I hear.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to repeat the reminder

**Respondent:** Yes. Maybe repeat once or twice. Because first time I may be busy.

**Interviewer:** What if you still do not take it

**Respondent:** Then maybe another reminder after some time. Or family can get a message.

**Interviewer:** Would family notification be useful

**Respondent:** Yes. Very useful. They already remind me. It can make it easier.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to show medicine information also

**Respondent:** Yes. Both are helpful. Reminder and information.

**Interviewer:** What kind of information

**Respondent:** Name. When to take. How many. Maybe what it is for. Simple information.

**Interviewer:** Do you want side effects also

**Respondent:** Maybe small simple information. Not too much. If there is something important then show it.

**Interviewer:** What about prescription reading

**Respondent:** Yes that is also useful. If the app can read it then good.

**Interviewer:** What about large letters

**Respondent:** Large text is better. I can read it quickly.

**Interviewer:** What about audio

**Respondent:** Audio is good. Voice is better for me.

**Interviewer:** Do you think you would actually use such an app

**Respondent:** Yes if it is simple. If it is complicated then no. I won't keep using it.

**Interviewer:** What would make it complicated

**Respondent:** Too many buttons. Too many screens. Too much typing. I don't want that.

**Interviewer:** So one simple screen

**Respondent:** Yes one simple thing. Medicine time. Medicine name. Reminder. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** What about travelling mode

**Respondent:** Yes that can help. Because travel is where I miss the most.

**Interviewer:** What would it do in travelling mode

**Respondent:** It should still remind me even if I am somewhere else. And maybe tell me to carry the medicine before leaving. That would be useful.

**Interviewer:** A reminder to carry the medicine before going out

**Respondent:** Yes yes. That is actually useful. Because sometimes I forget to carry it.

**Interviewer:** Anything else

**Respondent:** No. Main thing is reminder. Voice reminder. Family also can know. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Thank you

**Respondent:** Okay.

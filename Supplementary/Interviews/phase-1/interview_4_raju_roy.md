# 4. Raju Roy

**Interviewer:** Alright we will now talk about medicine intake. First tell me your name and age and a little about yourself

**Respondent:** I am Raju Roy. I am 31 years old. I work as a software engineer. My mother takes medicines regularly. I mostly help her with it because she sometimes forgets.

**Interviewer:** How many medicines does your mother take

**Respondent:** Usually two medicines at night. Two regular ones.

**Interviewer:** Do you or she follow any method to remember

**Respondent:** No proper method. There is some written instruction on the back of the medicine leaf. She knows from that. We don't have alarm or app.

**Interviewer:** So the writing on the medicine packet helps

**Respondent:** Yes. She sees it. It is like a habit now. But still she forgets.

**Interviewer:** How often does she forget

**Respondent:** Quite often. Not every day but frequently. She gets involved in household work and then the time goes. Then she forgets the medicine.

**Interviewer:** Can you give me an example

**Respondent:** At night she can be doing kitchen work or talking with someone or some guest is there. Then medicine time comes and nobody notices. Later she says oh I forgot.

**Interviewer:** What happens then

**Respondent:** Usually she takes it when she remembers. Sometimes she asks me if she should take it then or wait. Then we check what the doctor said or ask the pharmacist if needed.

**Interviewer:** Are you usually the person she asks

**Respondent:** Yes. Sometimes me. Sometimes another family member.

**Interviewer:** So she depends on family for remembering

**Respondent:** Yes. She depends on us quite a bit. She knows the medicine from the packet but the actual remembering part is difficult.

**Interviewer:** Who buys the medicines

**Respondent:** Mostly me or my father. Sometimes she also goes.

**Interviewer:** Who knows the medicine names better

**Respondent:** I think I know them better. She recognizes the packet more than the exact name.

**Interviewer:** Does she know why she takes each medicine

**Respondent:** She knows generally. But exact name and all that she does not always remember. Sometimes she asks me. Sometimes she just says the white one or the strip that I take at night.

**Interviewer:** So she identifies them by appearance too

**Respondent:** Yes. By packet, by tablet, by where it is kept. That kind of thing.

**Interviewer:** Where is it usually kept

**Respondent:** In one fixed place at home. Usually near the other medicines. She knows the place.

**Interviewer:** What happens if the medicine is moved

**Respondent:** Then there can be confusion. She asks where is it. So fixed place helps.

**Interviewer:** What about travelling or family occasions

**Respondent:** That is worse. When we travel or go to some occasion there are more chances of forgetting. Routine changes. Sometimes we carry the medicine but forget to take it. Sometimes the medicine is left at home.

**Interviewer:** Has that happened recently

**Respondent:** Yes. There was one time we were going somewhere and later she asked where her medicine was. We had to check the bags. It creates tension.

**Interviewer:** What do you normally do in that situation

**Respondent:** We check if somebody has packed it. If not then we see if we can get the same medicine from a local pharmacy. We usually call the pharmacy or doctor if we are not sure.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever had to buy it somewhere else

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. We have gone to local pharmacies.

**Interviewer:** Have you used technology to help with forgetfulness

**Respondent:** No. Not till now.

**Interviewer:** But you use a smartphone

**Respondent:** Yes. Smartphone and normal phone. I use smartphone a lot for work. My mother also uses smartphone but mainly calls and some messages.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever thought of setting an alarm for her

**Respondent:** Yes but somehow we never made it a proper routine. I can set one but then maybe she is doing something and ignores it. Or sometimes the alarm is there but she doesn't know which medicine it is for.

**Interviewer:** So a plain alarm is not enough

**Respondent:** Yes. It just rings. It does not say what to take. If it says mother take your night medicine then that is clearer.

**Interviewer:** Would a voice reminder help

**Respondent:** Yes. Voice would be very useful. Especially for her. She may not read a text at that moment.

**Interviewer:** Text would not be useful

**Respondent:** Text is okay but voice is better. She may keep the phone somewhere and not look at it. If it speaks then she can hear.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the reminder to name the medicine

**Respondent:** Yes. Medicine name or simple description. Like take your night medicine. Maybe both.

**Interviewer:** What if she doesn't take it after the first reminder

**Respondent:** It should remind again. Maybe after ten minutes. And then if still not done maybe it can tell me.

**Interviewer:** So you want a family alert

**Respondent:** Yes. That would be useful. Otherwise I keep thinking did she take it or not. The app can reduce that pressure.

**Interviewer:** Would you want to see that she took it

**Respondent:** Yes. Maybe just a simple yes or no. Taken or not taken. I don't need anything very detailed.

**Interviewer:** What if she accidentally marks it as taken

**Respondent:** Maybe there can be a simple confirmation. But I don't want a complicated process.

**Interviewer:** Do you think she would use it herself or would you manage it

**Respondent:** I would probably set it up. After that she can just hear it and press one button. I would manage the settings.

**Interviewer:** Why

**Respondent:** Because I am more comfortable with apps. She can use a phone but setting things up is not easy for her. So I would do the setup.

**Interviewer:** What about reading prescriptions

**Respondent:** She cannot read them properly. The handwriting is difficult. Usually we take it to the pharmacy and they give us the medicine.

**Interviewer:** Would a phone based prescription reader be useful

**Respondent:** Yes definitely. If my phone can look at the prescription and tell me what medicine the doctor wrote then it would be really nice. It will save time.

**Interviewer:** Would you trust it fully

**Respondent:** I would check it. I would not just trust it blindly. Maybe compare with the pharmacy or old medicine. But as a helper it would be very good.

**Interviewer:** What should it show

**Respondent:** Name. Dose. Time. How many days. Maybe a picture. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Would it help to store the prescription

**Respondent:** Yes. Then we don't need to search for the paper. We can keep it in the app.

**Interviewer:** What about the doctor number

**Respondent:** Yes that should be there. And maybe the local pharmacist number. We already communicate with our local pharmacist.

**Interviewer:** Who do you contact when there is a medicine problem

**Respondent:** Usually local pharmacist for small things. Doctor for bigger things.

**Interviewer:** Do you ever ask friends or neighbours about medicines

**Respondent:** Yes sometimes. Especially if somebody has the same problem. People tell us what they are taking.

**Interviewer:** How much do you trust that kind of advice

**Respondent:** More than I should maybe. If many people in the group say the same medicine works then I start trusting it. You hear it from one person, then another person, then another. It feels like everybody knows about it.

**Interviewer:** Even if you have heard something negative about that medicine

**Respondent:** Yes. The group can make it feel more reliable. I may think the negative thing was maybe one person's experience. But many people saying good things makes me trust it more.

**Interviewer:** Would you actually take it without checking

**Respondent:** For myself maybe I might ask the pharmacist first. For my mother I would definitely check because her medicines are already fixed. I don't want to change them just because somebody said something.

**Interviewer:** But the social group still affects your trust

**Respondent:** Yes. It does. When many people agree about something you automatically feel it has some truth. Even if you know it may not be the best option.

**Interviewer:** Does this also happen in other health decisions

**Respondent:** Yes. People talk. Someone says this doctor is good. Someone says this medicine is good. Someone says don't take this. We listen to the group. Sometimes it helps and sometimes it makes confusion.

**Interviewer:** So what would make a medicine app useful in this kind of situation

**Respondent:** It should show information clearly. Like why the medicine is used. What the doctor prescribed. Maybe what not to mix it with. So when somebody says something outside I can check the actual information.

**Interviewer:** Would you want recommendations from the app

**Respondent:** I don't want it to randomly recommend medicines. I want it to show what the doctor prescribed and basic information. Recommendation can create another problem.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to warn you if a family member is using the wrong medicine

**Respondent:** Yes. If it can compare the prescription with what we entered then a warning is useful. Like this is not on the current prescription. Then I can check.

**Interviewer:** What about large text

**Respondent:** Yes. Large text is better for my mother.

**Interviewer:** What about voice

**Respondent:** Voice is number one for her.

**Interviewer:** What about color coding

**Respondent:** Maybe. One color for morning and one for night. But not too many colors.

**Interviewer:** Would pictures help

**Respondent:** Yes. Especially because she remembers the medicine by how the packet looks.

**Interviewer:** Could the app show the picture of the exact strip

**Respondent:** Yes. That would be good. Then she can compare.

**Interviewer:** What would be the most frustrating thing about such an app

**Respondent:** If it asks us to type too much. Or if it keeps asking questions. We will stop using it.

**Interviewer:** What would make it easy

**Respondent:** I set it once. Put in the medicines. Set the times. Then it just reminds. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** What about when the medicine changes

**Respondent:** Then I can update it. Maybe take a photo of the new prescription and change the medicine.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to remind you to refill the medicine

**Respondent:** Yes. That is also useful. Sometimes you suddenly find the strip is almost finished.

**Interviewer:** Would that be useful for travelling too

**Respondent:** Yes. Before travelling it can say take medicine with you. That would help.

**Interviewer:** What about family events and guests

**Respondent:** It can still remind. Because that is exactly when she forgets. People come, she gets busy, then medicine is forgotten. A voice reminder would cut through that.

**Interviewer:** Do you think seeing other people take medicine reminds her

**Respondent:** Yes I think so. Sometimes at gatherings people talk about medicines. Then she remembers her own medicine. For me also if someone says I take this every night then I remember my mother has her medicine too.

**Interviewer:** So gatherings can both distract and remind

**Respondent:** Yes exactly. Both. Too many things can make you forget but somebody mentioning medicine can remind you.

**Interviewer:** Do you have anything else you want to share

**Respondent:** The main thing is that family is already doing the reminder work. The app should help us with that. It should make it less stressful. Voice reminder for my mother. Notification for me if she misses it. And prescription reading would be very good.

**Interviewer:** Any other feature

**Respondent:** Maybe a simple history. Like yesterday taken. Today taken. Nothing more. Just enough to know.

**Interviewer:** Anything else

**Respondent:** No. That is all.

**Interviewer:** Thank you

**Respondent:** Okay.

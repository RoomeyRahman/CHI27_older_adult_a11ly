# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript

**Respondent: Male, 67 years, farmer**

**Interviewer:** So now that you have used the medicine reminder system, how did you find it?

**Respondent:** It was okay. My son set it up. I don't use phone much, so in the beginning I was not sure. But the reminder was easy enough. It told me when to take the medicine.

**Interviewer:** Did you understand who the system was working for?

**Respondent:** For me first, because I have to take the medicine. But my son could also know if I had taken it. So I think it was for both. At home my wife already reminds me, and outside the phone can remind me.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel uncomfortable that your family could know about your medicine?

**Respondent:** No no. Not really. My wife knows and my son knows already. It is not some private thing. If it is about medicine and I forget, then they should know. But they don't need to see every small thing I am doing.

**Interviewer:** When did it start feeling like a shared family system rather than only your own reminder?

**Respondent:** When my son started checking it. If I did not confirm, he would ask me. Sometimes I had taken the tablet but did not press anything. Then he would ask, did you take it. So it became another way for him to check.

**Interviewer:** Did you prefer the system to work independently or together with your family?

**Respondent:** Together. The phone can tell time. My son knows the medicine name. My wife knows when I ate and when I came home. So all three, you can say, have different information.

**Interviewer:** Did that make you trust it more?

**Respondent:** Yes. If my son puts the medicine and time, then I know it is from my prescription. If the phone itself starts deciding things, no, I would not trust that.

---

## Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** If the system says you may have missed your medicine, would you want your son or wife to be alerted?

**Respondent:** Yes. If I really missed it, then they should know. Especially if I am outside or working. My son can call me.

**Interviewer:** What if the system becomes more urgent because you have not responded?

**Respondent:** One reminder, then another after some time is fine. If still no response, tell my son. But it should not keep making noise continuously. In the field I may not hear it anyway.

**Interviewer:** If the system told you to take a medicine but you thought that was wrong, what would you do?

**Respondent:** I would not take it just because the phone said so. I would ask my wife or son. If there is any change, we ask the doctor. Doctor gives the medicine, not the phone.

**Interviewer:** So would you use a veto if you disagreed with the system?

**Respondent:** Yes, yes. If I know something is not right, I will stop and ask. I will not take an extra tablet because of a reminder.

**Interviewer:** What about not responding at all?

**Respondent:** Sometimes I don't respond. If I am working, I may hear it later or not hear it. Sometimes I take the medicine and then forget to press the button. So no response does not always mean I did not take it.

**Interviewer:** How should the system handle that silence?

**Respondent:** Give me some time. Maybe remind again. Then if still nothing, son can ask. That is better than immediately saying I missed it.

**Interviewer:** Has the system changed your normal routine?

**Respondent:** No, the routine is still after food. The phone is just helping when the routine changes. Farming is not fixed. Sometimes I am in the field longer than I thought.

**Interviewer:** What would you consider a serious failure?

**Respondent:** Wrong medicine or wrong time. That can be a problem. Also if it tells my son I missed medicine when I actually took it every day, then he will worry for no reason.

---

## Care Collectivism and Checking as Love

**Interviewer:** Has the system changed how you and your wife remind each other?

**Respondent:** A little. Earlier we only reminded each other. Now sometimes the phone reminds first. Then one of us asks the other. But we still remind each other.

**Interviewer:** Do you miss the old family reminders?

**Respondent:** No no. Those are still there. Phone cannot replace my wife. She knows if I have eaten, if I am going to the field, all those things. Phone only knows what is put into it.

**Interviewer:** Sometimes does your wife's checking feel irritating?

**Respondent:** Sometimes, yes. She asks, medicine taken, medicine taken. I say yes yes. But she is helping. I also ask her about her medicine. So it is both ways.

**Interviewer:** Did the system make family checking feel different?

**Respondent:** Maybe it made it easier to check without asking every time. My son can see something on his phone. Then if there is a problem he calls. So there is less unnecessary asking.

**Interviewer:** If the system or prescription information was unclear, would you discuss it with the family?

**Respondent:** Definitely. I cannot read the prescription properly. My son reads it. If something changes, he checks. Then we ask doctor if needed.

**Interviewer:** Would you let the system decide about a new medicine?

**Respondent:** No no. Never by itself. It can show what the doctor has already given. If a new medicine comes, son and doctor have to check.

**Interviewer:** Did the system give you a reason to involve the family more?

**Respondent:** It reminded me that somebody should know what medicine I am taking. But in our family they already know. My son keeps the prescription photo. My wife knows my regular tablet. So it fits into what we already do.

---

## Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** Did you notice the points or streak for taking your medicine regularly?

**Respondent:** Yes, I saw that. My son explained it. It was showing that I had taken it regularly for several days.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel about having a score?

**Respondent:** At first I thought, why score for one tablet. But then it was a little interesting. If it is showing many days, you want to keep it.

**Interviewer:** Did your family react when you had a good streak?

**Respondent:** My son said good, keep taking it. My wife also noticed. It was not a big thing, but they were happy.

**Interviewer:** Did their encouragement matter?

**Respondent:** Yes, a little. If someone says good, you feel good. But I was already taking the medicine regularly. It is not only because of the score.

**Interviewer:** What if you broke the streak?

**Respondent:** I would feel bad for some time. But no, I would not get very worried. If I missed, first find out why and then start again.

**Interviewer:** Would you want your score to be private or shared with your family?

**Respondent:** Family can see it. That is okay. I would not need other people to see my score. My son and wife are enough.

**Interviewer:** Did the score make you more aware of your medicine routine?

**Respondent:** Yes, maybe. Especially on days when work changes. I see it and remember that I have to keep the routine.

---

## Probationary Trust and Retraining

**Interviewer:** In the beginning, did you feel you had to test whether the system was trustworthy?

**Respondent:** Yes. New things, you have to check. My son set it up and we watched whether the time was correct. I wanted to see if it was actually useful.

**Interviewer:** What did you test?

**Respondent:** Whether it reminded me at the right time, whether I could understand it, and whether it would keep bothering me. Also whether my son would get the alert properly.

**Interviewer:** How long did it take before you became comfortable with it?

**Respondent:** After using it for some time. Once I saw it was giving the reminder properly, I became more comfortable. But for prescription changes I would still ask my son.

**Interviewer:** Did the system replace your memory?

**Respondent:** No. I still remember the medicine. I have been taking it for many years. It is more like a second memory.

**Interviewer:** Did it change your sense of when the medicine was due?

**Respondent:** A little. Earlier I thought after food, morning or whenever the routine was. The reminder gives a more fixed time. That is useful, but I still think about food and work also.

**Interviewer:** If the system stopped working tomorrow, would you still take your medicine?

**Respondent:** Yes. I would. I may be late if I am in the field, but I know I have to take it. My wife would also remind me.

**Interviewer:** So you would not want to become completely dependent on it?

**Respondent:** No no. Phone is help. It should not become the only thing. Family and habit are still there.

---

## Closing and Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** Finally, who do you feel the system ultimately works for?

**Respondent:** Both me and family. I am taking the medicine, so it works for me. But if I miss something, family needs to know. So both.

**Interviewer:** When would it be acceptable for the system to become an advocate for your family?

**Respondent:** If I really miss the medicine and do not respond, then it can tell my son. That is okay. If everything is fine, it should stay quiet.

**Interviewer:** Should it ever contact your family before giving you another reminder?

**Respondent:** No, first remind me. Give me some time. If I still don't respond, then tell them. Otherwise they may worry when there is no need.

**Interviewer:** What was the most useful part for you?

**Respondent:** The reminder when I am away from home. At home my wife can tell me. In the field there is nobody asking. If the phone reminds me then it can help.

**Interviewer:** What was less useful?

**Respondent:** Too much information. I don't need many things on the phone. Medicine time, picture, maybe name, that is enough. My son can handle the rest.

**Interviewer:** Would you want medicine finishing reminders?

**Respondent:** Yes. That would be useful. Sometimes we only notice when there are very few tablets left. If my son gets a message before that, he can buy.

**Interviewer:** Would you want doctor appointment reminders too?

**Respondent:** Yes, that can be useful. But again, don't put too many things. If there are too many messages then I will ignore them.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the system to tell you about medicines other people are taking?

**Respondent:** No no. That can confuse me. People already tell me enough at the tea shop and in the village. If I hear about another medicine, I can ask the doctor.

**Interviewer:** Did your farmer group affect how you felt about the system?

**Respondent:** Yes. If other farmers use it and say it works, I will trust it more. We learn from each other. If someone is using a new thing and it is helping, then others become interested.

**Interviewer:** Would you change your medicine if the whole group said another medicine was better?

**Respondent:** I would definitely ask about it. But I would not change directly. I would tell my son and ask the doctor. Group experience is useful, but doctor knows my medicine.

**Interviewer:** What about the app itself, if many people in your group use it?

**Respondent:** Then I would feel more comfortable. If my son also says it is good and other people are using it, then yes, I would keep using it.

**Interviewer:** How should the system speak to you?

**Respondent:** Simple voice. Like, take your medicine now. That's enough. I don't want difficult words.

**Interviewer:** Should you have to type anything?

**Respondent:** No no. I don't want typing. My son should set everything. I just want to hear the reminder.

**Interviewer:** What should the system never do?

**Respondent:** It should not tell me to change medicine by itself. It should not make too much noise. It should not show too many things. And it should not make my family worried for no reason.

**Interviewer:** If you could keep only one feature, what would you keep?

**Respondent:** Reminder. Especially when I am outside. That is the main thing.

**Interviewer:** And if you could keep one family feature?

**Respondent:** Let my son know if I really missed it. Then he can call me. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Do you feel the system should replace family support?

**Respondent:** No no. Family support should stay. Phone can help when family is not there. My wife and son are still the ones I ask when there is confusion.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to add?

**Respondent:** No. I think it is useful if it stays simple. For people like me, the phone should remind, not decide. If medicine time comes, tell me. If I don't respond, tell my son. The rest we can manage.

**Interviewer:** Thank you.

**Respondent:** Yes yes, okay. Thank you.

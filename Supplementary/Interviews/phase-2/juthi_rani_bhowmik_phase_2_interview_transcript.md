# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript: Medication Management

**Participant:** Juthi Rani Bhowmik  
**Age:** Not stated in the source transcript

---

## 1. Affiliation Ledger & Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** "Since using the medication support agent, how did you feel when it made clear who it was helping or who it was working for?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "For me it was mainly helping with my parents and my aunty's medicine. Since I am mostly the one checking their medicine, I felt it was helping me also. It was clear enough."

**Interviewer:** "Did that feel transparent, or did it ever feel like the agent was getting too involved in family matters?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "No no, for medicine it was okay. I don't think it needs to get involved in other family things. If it is just helping with medicine and reminders, then I am comfortable."

**Interviewer:** "Was there a point when you started using it privately and then sharing its information with other family members?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes, if there is some important missed medicine then I think it is better that another family member knows. Because I also have work and I am not always home. So it cannot always depend only on me."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want the agent to work independently, or would you prefer it to work together with you and your family?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Together. The agent can handle the reminders, but family should still be involved. Especially if there is a medicine change or something unusual. I would not want the system to decide everything by itself."

---

## 2. Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** "If the agent suggested something about your parents' medicine that did not look right to you, would you feel comfortable overriding it?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes, definitely. I would check the prescription first. If it does not match what the doctor said, then I would not follow the system blindly."

**Interviewer:** "What if the agent gave a stronger warning because it thought a medicine had been missed or there was some risk?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "That would be useful. I would want to know if something important happened. But it should explain what the problem is. Just giving a big warning without telling me why would not be very helpful."

**Interviewer:** "If there was no response after a reminder, what should the system do?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "It should remind again after some time. Sometimes my mother forgets, so one reminder may not be enough. If there is still no response, then maybe it can inform me."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want the family to be contacted for every missed reminder?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "No no, not every small thing. Maybe one more reminder first. If it keeps happening, then tell me. Otherwise it will become annoying."

**Interviewer:** "How would you know whether something is just part of the normal routine or whether something has actually gone wrong?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Normally it is just the medicine reminder and they take it. If they miss it, take the wrong medicine, or something changes from the usual schedule, then that is a problem. The system should make that clear."

---

## 3. Care Collectivism & "Checking as Love"

**Interviewer:** "Has the agent changed how your family checks on your parents' medicine?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes, a little. Before, if I was not at home, I would call them and ask. Now the reminder can do some of that work. But I would still check with them, especially my mother."

**Interviewer:** "Do you feel like you miss the manual checking, or has the agent simply given you another way to care?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "I don't think I miss it. I still want to know if they took the medicine. The difference is I don't have to keep remembering every single time myself. So that makes it easier."

**Interviewer:** "If there was confusion about a prescription or a sudden change in medicine, would you let the agent handle it?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "No. For that I would check with the doctor and discuss it with the family. We have had a situation before where my father had a problem with a medicine and the doctor changed it. So for those things, I would not depend only on an app."

**Interviewer:** "So the agent can support the routine, but important health decisions still need people?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes yes. Exactly. It can remind us, but doctor and family should make the important decisions."

---

## 4. Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** "If the agent showed that your family had a good score or a long streak for taking medicines properly, how do you think your family would react?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "They would probably feel good. It would show that we are keeping up with the medicine. But I don't think the score itself is very important. The medicine is the important thing."

**Interviewer:** "Would praise or encouragement help?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes, simple encouragement is okay. If it says everything is going well, that is nice. Family members also may say good, you are keeping up with it."

**Interviewer:** "What if a streak broke because one medicine was missed?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "I would not want that to make my mother anxious. Sometimes people forget. We should understand why it happened and make sure it doesn't happen again. The score should not become more stressful than the medicine itself."

**Interviewer:** "Would you prefer the score to be private or shared with the family?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Basic progress can be shared with family. Since I am helping them, it is useful for me to know. But every small detail does not need to be shown to everyone."

---

## 5. Probationary Trust & Retraining

**Interviewer:** "When you first started using the agent, did you feel you had to test it before trusting it?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes. Definitely. I would not immediately trust a new system with my parents' medicines. First I would compare the reminders with the prescription and the medicines we already have."

**Interviewer:** "What would make you trust it more?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "If it keeps giving the correct medicine and correct timing, then slowly I would trust it. But I would still keep the prescription because that is our main reference."

**Interviewer:** "Did the agent replace your memory of the medicine schedule, or did it change how you think about the timing?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "It reduced the need for me to remember every time. I still know the general routine, but I don't have to keep checking the clock. The reminder tells me when something needs attention."

**Interviewer:** "Would you still check the original prescription?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes yes. Especially if there is a new medicine or a change. The app can remind me, but I would still check the prescription."

---

## 6. Closing & Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** "After using the agent, who do you think it ultimately works for—yourself, your parents and aunty, or the whole family?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "I think all of us, in a way. It helps me because I am the one mostly managing the medicine. But the main purpose is helping my parents and my aunty take their medicine properly. And if I am not home, other family members may need to know."

**Interviewer:** "Would you be comfortable with the agent becoming a family advocate in some situations rather than staying only as your private reminder?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes, for important things. If my mother misses a medicine and does not respond, then I should know. If something serious happens, family should know. But normal reminders don't need to involve everyone."

**Interviewer:** "Where would you draw the line between a private reminder and something the family should know about?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "A normal reminder can stay with the person taking the medicine and me. If there are repeated missed medicines, a wrong medicine, or some change that needs attention, then it should come to the family. That would be useful."

**Interviewer:** "Overall, has the agent made medication management easier for you?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Yes, I think it has. The main problem before was timing. Sometimes I was at work or not at home, and then I did not know whether they had taken the medicine. Now the reminder can help them even when I am not there."

**Interviewer:** "Anything else you would like to add?"

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Just that it should be simple and reliable. My parents can use smartphones, but they are not comfortable with every new app. So if the reminder is clear, maybe voice also, and it tells them which medicine and when, then that is enough."

**Interviewer:** "Thank you for your time."

**Juthi Rani Bhowmik:** "Thank you."

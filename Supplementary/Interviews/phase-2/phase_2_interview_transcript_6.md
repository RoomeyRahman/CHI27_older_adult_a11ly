# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript

**Respondent: Female, 71 years, widow**

**Interviewer:** So now after using the medicine reminder system, how did you find it?

**Respondent:** It was okay. My son helped me with it. I am still not very good with these phone things, but the reminder I could understand. If it speaks or shows the medicine, then I know.

**Interviewer:** Did you understand that the system was mainly for you, but your family could also be involved?

**Respondent:** Yes yes. I understood that. It reminds me first, and if I don't take it or don't respond, my son can know. That is fine. My son already manages my medicines.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel about the system knowing whether you had taken your medicine?

**Respondent:** I did not mind. My son already asks me. My daughter also calls and asks. So this is not something secret. But it should only be about the medicine. I don't want it checking everything I do.

**Interviewer:** Did there come a point when the system felt less like a private reminder and more like something shared with the family?

**Respondent:** Yes, when my son started getting the information if I had not confirmed. Then he would ask me. Sometimes I had taken the medicine but forgot to press the button. So he would ask, and I would say yes yes, I took it.

**Interviewer:** Did that make you feel watched?

**Respondent:** No no. Not by my son. I feel safe when he checks. If somebody else was checking everything, then maybe I would not like it.

**Interviewer:** Do you think the system should work by itself or together with your family?

**Respondent:** Together. The phone does not know everything. My son knows my medicine names and prescription. My daughter also knows. The phone can remind, but family understands the situation.

---

## Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** If the system says you may have missed a medicine, should your son be alerted?

**Respondent:** Yes. If I really missed it, he should know. Then he can call me. Sometimes I am busy or talking and I forget, so that can help.

**Interviewer:** What if the system gives another stronger reminder because you have not responded?

**Respondent:** One more reminder is okay. Then if I still don't respond, tell my son. But don't keep ringing many times. That will make me confused.

**Interviewer:** What if you know you already took the medicine but the system says you did not?

**Respondent:** Then I will not take it again. No no. I will tell my son if needed. I know sometimes I forget to press the phone. The medicine has already been taken.

**Interviewer:** Would you ever ignore what the system tells you?

**Respondent:** If I know it is wrong, yes. I will not take something just because the phone says so. For medicine, I ask my son. If it is a change, we ask doctor.

**Interviewer:** What happens when you do not respond to the reminder?

**Respondent:** Sometimes I may not hear it or I may be doing something. Sometimes I take the tablet and then don't press. So silence does not always mean I missed the medicine.

**Interviewer:** How should the system understand that?

**Respondent:** It should wait a little. Remind again. Then if there is still no response, tell my son. He can call and check.

**Interviewer:** Has the system changed your normal medicine routine?

**Respondent:** No. I still take the medicines in the morning and at night like before. The reminder only helps with remembering the time.

**Interviewer:** What would be a serious problem with the system?

**Respondent:** If it tells me the wrong medicine or tells me to take something twice. That would be dangerous. Also if it keeps telling my son I missed when I actually took it, then he will worry unnecessarily.

---

## Care Collectivism and Checking as Love

**Interviewer:** Has the system changed how your family checks on you?

**Respondent:** A little. Before my son or daughter would ask me directly. Now the phone can remind me first. If I don't respond, then they can ask. So maybe they don't have to ask every time.

**Interviewer:** Do you miss your family's old reminders?

**Respondent:** No no. They still remind me. I actually like when they ask. Sometimes I get irritated, yes, because they ask again and again. But I know they are caring for me.

**Interviewer:** So when your son checks whether you took your medicine, does that feel like care?

**Respondent:** Yes. Definitely. He is looking after me. My daughter also worries. The phone cannot replace that. It only helps them know if there is a problem.

**Interviewer:** If there is confusion about a prescription, what would you do?

**Respondent:** I would ask my son. I cannot read the prescription properly. He keeps it and knows what the doctor said. If needed he will ask the doctor.

**Interviewer:** Would you let the system decide what medicine you should take if something changes?

**Respondent:** No no. That is not for the phone. Doctor decides. My son checks the prescription. The system can remind me what has already been entered, but a new medicine has to be checked.

**Interviewer:** Did using the system make you involve your family more in medicine decisions?

**Respondent:** They were already involved. Maybe now there is one more way for them to know. But for any change, I still ask them. I don't make those decisions alone.

---

## Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** Did you notice the points or streaks for taking your medicine?

**Respondent:** Yes, my son showed me. It was counting the days. I did not understand at first why it was needed, but then I could see that I was taking it regularly.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel when you had a long streak?

**Respondent:** It felt good. I thought, okay, I have done it regularly. My son was also happy. He said keep going.

**Interviewer:** Did your family praise or encourage you?

**Respondent:** Yes, a little. My son said good. My daughter also asked about it once. It became something they could see. But they don't need to make a big thing about it.

**Interviewer:** If you broke the streak, would you feel anxious?

**Respondent:** Maybe I would feel bad. But not very anxious. If I missed one day, first we would see what happened and then continue. I don't want the phone making me frightened.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer your score to be private or shared with your family?

**Respondent:** Family can see it. That is okay. I don't want other people to see it. My medicine is my matter, even if my family knows.

**Interviewer:** Did the streak make you more careful about taking medicine?

**Respondent:** A little. When you see many days together, you want to continue. But mostly I take it because I know I have to. I have been taking medicine for a long time.

**Interviewer:** Did your daughter or son ever use the score to remind or encourage you?

**Respondent:** Yes, my son sometimes said don't break it. I told him I am trying. It was okay. It gave them another small way to encourage me.

---

## Probationary Trust and Retraining

**Interviewer:** In the beginning, did you feel you had to test whether the system was trustworthy?

**Respondent:** Yes. Of course. New things I don't trust immediately. My son put the medicines and times. We checked whether the reminder came properly.

**Interviewer:** What were you checking?

**Respondent:** Whether it was the correct medicine, correct time, and whether it was easy to understand. Also whether it would keep making noise. I don't like too much noise.

**Interviewer:** Did your trust increase after using it for some time?

**Respondent:** Yes. Once I saw it was reminding correctly, I became more comfortable. But if the doctor changes something, my son has to check again. I don't just trust the old setting.

**Interviewer:** Did the system replace your memory?

**Respondent:** No. I still remember some medicines myself. But there are three or four and sometimes I get confused. So it helps me remember which one is for which time.

**Interviewer:** Did it retrain you to think about a particular time for taking your medicine?

**Respondent:** Maybe a little. Before I depended more on my son saying morning or night. Now the reminder gives me the time. So I became a little more aware. But I still like my son to know.

**Interviewer:** If the system stopped working, would you still be able to manage?

**Respondent:** I would manage the regular medicines with my son. If everything is kept in the right place, I can take them. But if a medicine changes, I need him. So no, the system is not the only thing.

**Interviewer:** So did it replace family memory?

**Respondent:** No no. It adds another reminder. My son still remembers the names and prescription. That is different.

---

## Closing and Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** Finally, who do you think the system works for, you, your family, or both?

**Respondent:** Both. It helps me remember. It also helps my son know if I have not responded. So both are getting some help.

**Interviewer:** When would it be acceptable for the system to become a family advocate?

**Respondent:** If I really miss a medicine and don't respond, then it can tell my son. That is acceptable. If I am taking everything properly, then it should not keep involving the family.

**Interviewer:** Should the system contact your family immediately when you do not respond?

**Respondent:** No. First remind me. Then wait. Maybe remind again. Sometimes I have taken it and forgotten the phone. After that, if there is still no response, then tell my son.

**Interviewer:** What is the most useful thing about it for you?

**Respondent:** The medicine reminder. And the picture. Sometimes I don't remember the name, but if I see the strip or tablet picture, I know which one it is.

**Interviewer:** What about the family notification?

**Respondent:** That is useful too. Especially because my son already manages the prescription. If I miss something, he can call me.

**Interviewer:** What is the least useful part?

**Respondent:** Too much information. I don't need all kinds of health information. I will get confused. Just medicine, time, picture, and if there is a real problem, tell family.

**Interviewer:** Would you want a reminder when medicine is finishing?

**Respondent:** Yes. That would help. My son already checks sometimes, but if the system tells him before it finishes then we don't suddenly find that there are only one or two tablets left.

**Interviewer:** Would you want doctor appointment reminders?

**Respondent:** Yes, maybe. My son usually knows. But if the phone reminds us also, that is okay.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the system to tell you about medicines that other people are taking?

**Respondent:** No no. People already tell me enough. Someone says this medicine is good, someone says another one is good. I listen, but I don't want the phone adding more confusion.

**Interviewer:** If many people around you said a certain medicine was good, would that affect you?

**Respondent:** I would listen. If many people are taking something and saying it helped, I will ask my son about it. But I won't take it directly. My son or doctor has to say it is okay.

**Interviewer:** So group opinion can make you interested, but not decide for you?

**Respondent:** Yes. Exactly. I can hear what people say. If many say the same thing, I will ask about it. But my family and doctor decide what I take.

**Interviewer:** What if your son and daughter disagree about a medicine?

**Respondent:** Then they can talk between themselves and ask the doctor. I don't want to decide when I don't understand the medicine properly. They know more about it.

**Interviewer:** How should the system speak to you?

**Respondent:** Simple voice. Like, this is your medicine, take it now. That's enough. Don't give long speech. I may not understand.

**Interviewer:** Should you have to type anything?

**Respondent:** No no. My son should set it. I don't know typing and settings properly.

**Interviewer:** What should the system never do?

**Respondent:** It should not change my medicine itself. It should not tell me to take an extra tablet. It should not make too much noise. And it should not worry my children for no reason.

**Interviewer:** If you could keep only one feature, what would you keep?

**Respondent:** The reminder. That is the main thing. If I know which medicine to take and when, that is enough.

**Interviewer:** And one feature for the family?

**Respondent:** Let my son know if I really missed it. Then he can call. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Do you think the system should replace family support?

**Respondent:** No no. Family should still be there. The phone can help, but my son and daughter know me. If something changes, I need them.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to add?

**Respondent:** No. I think it is useful. I am not very good with phones, so if it stays simple I can use it. Reminder for me, information for family when there is a real problem. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Thank you.

**Respondent:** Yes okay. Thank you.

# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript: Medication Management

**Participant:** Aisha  
**Age:** 20  
**Family context:** Lives with her husband and family; father-in-law and mother-in-law live in the household

---

## 1. Affiliation Ledger & Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** "You have been using the medication support system for some weeks now. Tell me how it has gone."

**Aisha:** "It has been helpful for me, especially because I am the one who gives them their medicines. Before, I had to remember everything myself, so having something remind me made it easier."

**Interviewer:** "When a dose was due, who did the agent contact, and in what order?"

**Aisha:** "Mostly it reminded me, because I am the one managing their medicines. If I am busy doing household work, the reminder helps me remember that it is time."

**Interviewer:** "Was there anything it told you that the older adults did not know it had told you?"

**Aisha:** "No, nothing like that. It was mainly about the medicines and the timing. There was no reason for it to get involved in other family matters."

**Interviewer:** "Did the way it worked change at any point during the weeks you used it?"

**Aisha:** "In the beginning I was paying more attention to the reminders and checking whether it was coming at the right time. Later it felt more normal, like part of the routine."

**Interviewer:** "Did the agent ever say anything aloud that changed how you or the family handled the medicines?"

**Aisha:** "Not really. The important thing was that it reminded me at the right time. Then I could give them the medicine."

**Interviewer:** "Was there anything you wanted it to change that it did not?"

**Aisha:** "I would want the reminder to be very noticeable when I am busy. A call would be good because when I am working around the house, I may not notice a small notification."

---

## 2. Veto, Escalation & Silence

**Interviewer:** "Did the agent ever tell you something about the medicines that you knew to be wrong?"

**Aisha:** "No, I did not have that kind of problem. But if it ever said something different from the medicine or prescription, I would check it myself before giving the medicine."

**Interviewer:** "Were you able to stop it doing something?"

**Aisha:** "Yes, I think I should be able to stop or ignore a reminder if there is some reason. The final decision should still be with me and the family."

**Interviewer:** "If it gave a stronger warning after a dose seemed to have been missed, how would you feel about that?"

**Aisha:** "That would actually be useful. Sometimes I get very busy with household work and the time can pass. If it reminds me again, I can check and give the medicine."

**Interviewer:** "Did it ever repeat or escalate an alert after you had dealt with it?"

**Aisha:** "No, I would not want it to keep repeating after I have already handled it. Once I know about it, that should be enough."

**Interviewer:** "What happened when nobody answered a reminder?"

**Aisha:** "It should remind again after some time. Sometimes I may be busy or away from the phone, so one reminder may be missed."

**Interviewer:** "When you saw that a reminder had not been answered, what did you assume?"

**Aisha:** "I would not immediately assume that they had taken the medicine. I would think maybe I was busy or the reminder was missed, and I would check."

**Interviewer:** "How do you normally distinguish an ordinary missed reminder from a real problem?"

**Aisha:** "If it happens once, it may just be because I was busy. But if the medicine is still not taken after another reminder, then I would treat it as something I need to check properly."

**Interviewer:** "Is there any capability you would not want the agent to have?"

**Aisha:** "It should not decide to change medicines by itself. If there is a new medicine or a change, I would want to check with the doctor or family."

---

## 3. Care Collectivism & Checking as Care

**Interviewer:** "Before the agent, how did you find out whether a dose had been taken? And how do you find out now?"

**Aisha:** "Before, I remembered the times myself and gave them the medicines. Now the reminder can tell me when it is time, so I do not have to depend only on my memory."

**Interviewer:** "Has anything changed about how often you speak to the older adults about their medicines?"

**Aisha:** "Not really. I still talk to them and make sure they take the medicine. The system just makes the timing easier for me."

**Interviewer:** "Did the medicine conversation ever become less frequent because the agent was handling the reminders?"

**Aisha:** "No. I still have to give them the medicine and check on them. It does not replace that part."

**Interviewer:** "Have the older adults said anything about the reminders?"

**Aisha:** "They are okay with it. For them, the main thing is getting the medicine at the right time."

**Interviewer:** "Did the agent's record ever disagree with what someone in the family said had happened?"

**Aisha:** "No, I did not have that problem. But if there was a difference, I would check what actually happened before trusting the record."

**Interviewer:** "Was there a health decision to make while you were using it?"

**Aisha:** "If there was a new medicine or something changed, I would not just follow the system. I would check the prescription or ask the doctor or pharmacist."

**Interviewer:** "Is there a decision you would not want it involved in at all?"

**Aisha:** "Yes, changing the medicine. The system can remind us, but it should not decide which medicine they should take."

**Interviewer:** "Does anyone in the family check on you as the person managing the medicines?"

**Aisha:** "Sometimes the family knows that I am taking care of them. I think it is normal for family members to check, especially when it is about elderly people."

---

## 4. Scores & Streaks as Relational Triggers

**Interviewer:** "Did you notice the points or the streak?"

**Aisha:** "I noticed them, but I did not think they were very important. The reminder itself is more useful to me."

**Interviewer:** "Did anyone in the family ever mention the score or streak out loud?"

**Aisha:** "Maybe they would notice that the medicines were being taken regularly, but I do not think we would talk much about the score itself."

**Interviewer:** "Was a streak broken during the weeks you used it?"

**Aisha:** "If a dose was missed, I would be more concerned about the medicine than about the streak. I would want to know why it was missed."

**Interviewer:** "Did the score or streak ever change what you did?"

**Aisha:** "Not really. Maybe it gives a little encouragement, but I would still follow the medicine schedule whether there was a score or not."

**Interviewer:** "Should the score be visible to the family, or kept private?"

**Aisha:** "Basic progress can be shared with the family, especially because I am managing their medicines. But there is no need to show too many details."

---

## 5. Probationary Trust & Retraining

**Interviewer:** "Think back to the first days. What did you do differently then from now?"

**Aisha:** "In the beginning I paid more attention to every reminder. I would check the medicine and make sure the time was correct. After using it for some time, I became more comfortable with it."

**Interviewer:** "Was there a point at which you stopped double-checking it?"

**Aisha:** "I would still check important things, especially if there was a new medicine. But I did not feel I had to question every normal reminder once I became familiar with it."

**Interviewer:** "Do you still keep the schedule in your head?"

**Aisha:** "Yes, I still know the routine because I have been giving them medicines every day. But I do not have to keep thinking about every exact time when I am busy."

**Interviewer:** "If the agent stopped working tomorrow morning, what would happen?"

**Aisha:** "I would go back to remembering the times myself like I did before. I already know their routine, so I could manage it, but it would be more difficult when I am very busy."

**Interviewer:** "Do you still keep the prescription or medicine instructions where you can check them?"

**Aisha:** "Yes. If there is any confusion, I would check the prescription or the medicine itself. The reminder is helpful, but those instructions are still important."

**Interviewer:** "What would make you trust the agent more over time?"

**Aisha:** "If it keeps reminding at the correct time and gives the correct medicine information, then I can depend on it more. It should be consistent."

---

## 6. Closing & Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** "Taking everything together, who would you say the agent works for?"

**Aisha:** "I think it works for the whole family, but especially for the people taking the medicine and for me because I am the one managing it. It makes my responsibility a little easier."

**Interviewer:** "If your interest and the older adult's interest pulled in opposite directions, whose side should it take?"

**Aisha:** "It should not make that decision by itself. It should support the person taking the medicine and also help the caregiver. For an important decision, the family or doctor should be involved."

**Interviewer:** "When is it acceptable for it to go from working quietly with one person to speaking on their behalf to the family?"

**Aisha:** "If there is a real problem, like a medicine is missed and nobody responds to the reminder, then telling me or another family member would be useful. For every normal reminder, it does not need to involve everyone."

**Interviewer:** "Is there anything it did that you would call a betrayal of somebody in this house?"

**Aisha:** "If it shared private family information that was not about the medicine, that would not be okay. It should stay focused on helping with the medicines."

**Interviewer:** "Take me through one recent occasion involving the agent, from beginning to end, starting before anything happened."

**Aisha:** "Usually I am doing household work and there are many things happening at the same time. The reminder comes when it is time for the medicine, and then I know I need to stop and give it to them. If I am busy and do not notice it, another reminder would help me. Then I can check the medicine and make sure they take it. That is the main way it helps me."

**Interviewer:** "Would you keep using it, and on what condition?"

**Aisha:** "Yes, I would keep using it if the reminders are reliable and come at the correct time. A direct call would be especially useful for me because it gets my attention when I am working."

**Interviewer:** "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"

**Aisha:** "I would make the reminder a call or something that really gets my attention. When I am busy with household work, a normal notification may be easy to miss."

**Interviewer:** "Is there anything we have not covered that mattered?"

**Aisha:** "Just that caring for elderly people is a daily responsibility. If the system can remind me when I am busy and help me not miss their medicine time, then it gives me peace of mind."

**Interviewer:** "Thank you for your time."

**Aisha:** "Thank you."

# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript: Medication Management

**Participant:** Janntul Tajrin  
**Occupation:** Software engineer

---

## 1. Affiliation Ledger & Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** "After using the medication support agent, how did you feel when it made clear who it was helping or who it was working for?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "For me it was mainly helping my parents and grandfather. I am the one who mostly looks after these things, so it was also helping me. It was clear enough."

**Interviewer:** "Did it feel transparent, or did it ever feel like the agent was getting too involved in family matters?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "No no, I think for medicine it is okay. If it is giving reminders and helping with medicine, that is useful. But I don't think it needs to get involved in other family things."

**Interviewer:** "When did you feel comfortable sharing information from the agent with other family members?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "If there is something important, like someone missed medicine, then I think family should know. We already remind each other normally. So sharing that kind of thing is not a big problem."

**Interviewer:** "Would you prefer the agent to work independently or together with you and your family?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Together. It can do the reminding itself, but family should still be involved. Especially if there is a medicine change. I would not want the agent to decide those things."

---

## 2. Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** "If the agent suggested something about a medicine that did not match the prescription, would you override it?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes yes, definitely. I would check the prescription or ask the doctor or pharmacist. I would not just follow the app because it says something."

**Interviewer:** "What if the agent gave a stronger warning because it thought there was a bigger risk?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "That can be useful. If something is actually important, I want to know. But it should tell me what the problem is. Just making a big alarm without explaining is not enough."

**Interviewer:** "If your parents did not respond to a reminder, how should the system handle that?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "First remind them again after some time. Sometimes they are busy or they may not notice the phone. If they still don't take it, then maybe tell me."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want the family to be notified every time a reminder is missed?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "No, not every time. That will be too much. One reminder, then another one, and if it is still missed then tell the family. Something like that."

**Interviewer:** "How would you distinguish a normal missed reminder from a real problem?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "If someone is just a little late, that can happen. But if they completely miss it, keep missing it, or take the wrong medicine, then that is more serious. The system should make that difference clear."

---

## 3. Care Collectivism & "Checking as Love"

**Interviewer:** "Has the agent changed how your family checks on your parents' or grandfather's medicine?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes, a little. Earlier we mostly depended on memory and family members reminding each other. Now the agent can remind them directly. So there is less need for me to keep asking every time."

**Interviewer:** "Do you feel like you miss those manual check-ins?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "No no, I still check. I just don't have to do it for every medicine time. So it reduces some work, but it doesn't mean we stop caring."

**Interviewer:** "If there was confusion about a prescription or a sudden medicine change, would you let the agent handle it?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "No. I would check with the doctor or pharmacist. We have had problems before with medicine, so I would not take a chance. The agent can tell me that something changed, but a person should confirm it."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want the family involved in those decisions?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes, especially if it is something important. If the doctor changes a medicine, then we should know what changed and why."

---

## 4. Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** "If the agent showed a high score or a long streak for keeping up with medicines, how would your family react?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "They would probably be happy. It would show that things are going properly. But I don't think anyone would care too much about the actual number."

**Interviewer:** "Would praise or encouragement make a difference?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "A little, yes. If it says everything is on track, that is good. Family also gives encouragement sometimes. But medicine is not a competition."

**Interviewer:** "What if a streak broke because someone missed a dose?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Then I would want to know why they missed it. I would not want everyone to get stressed because a streak became zero. The important thing is taking the medicine properly."

**Interviewer:** "Would you prefer progress to be private or shared with the family?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Basic progress can be shared. Since we already remind each other, it can help. But there is no need to show every small detail to everyone."

---

## 5. Probationary Trust & Retraining

**Interviewer:** "When you first started using the agent, did you feel you needed to test it before trusting it?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes definitely. I would first compare the reminders with the prescription. Especially because my grandfather takes three to four medicines at different times. I would want to see if it is giving the correct timing."

**Interviewer:** "How would you know it was becoming trustworthy?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "If it keeps matching the prescription and what the doctor or pharmacist told us, then slowly I can trust it more. In the beginning I would check everything myself."

**Interviewer:** "Did it replace your memory of the schedule, or did it change how you think about the timing?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Mostly it reduced the amount I have to remember. I still know the general routine. But I don't have to keep thinking, okay, now is the time for this medicine, now that one. The reminder handles that part."

**Interviewer:** "Would you still keep the prescription available?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes yes. Definitely. If there is a new medicine or a change, I would still check it. The prescription is still important."

---

## 6. Closing & Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** "After using the agent, who do you think it ultimately works for—you, your parents and grandfather, or the whole family?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "I think all of us. It helps me because I am the one who mostly manages things. But it is mainly for the people taking the medicine. And because everyone helps each other sometimes, the family also benefits."

**Interviewer:** "Would you be comfortable with the agent becoming a family advocate in some situations instead of staying only as your private tool?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes, for important things. If someone misses medicine repeatedly or there is some problem, then family should know. But for a normal reminder, it doesn't need to involve everyone."

**Interviewer:** "Where would you draw the line?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Normal reminder can stay between the person and the agent, or me if I am managing it. If there is a repeated missed medicine, wrong medicine, or something unusual, then it should tell me or another family member."

**Interviewer:** "Overall, has the agent made medication management easier for you?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes, I think so. Especially because sometimes I am working and I cannot keep checking. If the agent reminds them directly, then I don't have to keep calling. If there is a problem, I can know."

**Interviewer:** "Would you still want the system to be simple for your parents and grandfather?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Yes yes, definitely. My parents are okay with smartphones, but my grandfather is less comfortable with these things. So it should be simple. Maybe voice is useful for him. Just tell him which medicine and when."

**Interviewer:** "Anything else you would like to add?"

**Janntul Tajrin:** "No no. Just it should be reliable. Medicine is not something where the system can make guesses. It should give the correct information and make the routine easier."

**Interviewer:** "Thank you for your time."

**Janntul Tajrin:** "Thank you."

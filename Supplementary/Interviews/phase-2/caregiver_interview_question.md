**Appendix B2: Post-Deployment Evaluation Interview Protocol, Caregiver Branch**

_Note for Researchers: This is the caregiver branch of Appendix B and mirrors its six sections. Appendix B addresses the person taking the medicine; this branch addresses the caregiver as one of the parties whose claim on the agent is at stake, not as a reporter on someone else's use. Each section opens with an unprompted question, and probes that name a construct are held back until the unprompted account is exhausted; transcripts mark that boundary, and analysis treats spontaneous and prompted material separately. Every answer is recorded with its referent: the caregiver, the older adult as reported by the caregiver, or the household jointly. Where the agent's autonomous behaviour is not yet verifiable in the decision logs, ask the counterfactual form and mark the answer as elicited judgment rather than lived use._

_Tags: **[executed]** reconstructs questions evidenced in the filed caregiver transcripts C01 to C04 and C06 to C08. **[revised]** rewrites an executed question that presupposed its own answer, keeping the construct and removing the suggestion. **[specified]** was not asked in this round and enters as instrument development._

**Section 1: The Affiliation Ledger & Negotiating Allegiance [revised]**

- **Main Goal:** Establish what the agent actually did about direction and visibility before asking what the caregiver made of it. Do not introduce the words loyalty, allegiance, or advocate in this section.

- You have been using the agent for some weeks now. Tell me how it has gone.
- _[Probe: What did it change about an ordinary day, and was there anything about it you did not like?]_

- When a dose was due, who did the agent contact, and in what order?
- _[Probe: Did it reach you and them at the same time, or one before the other?]_

- Did the agent ever say out loud who it was working for?
- _[Probe: What did it say? Say it back to me as closely as you can remember.]_

- Was there anything it told you that the older adult did not know it had told you?
- _[Probe: How did you come to realise that, and did you mention it to them?]_

- Did the way it worked change at any point during the weeks you used it?
- _[Probe: Tell me about the last time. Who set that change in motion, you, them, or the agent?]_

- Was anything said out loud about the change, by the agent or by anyone in the family?
- _[Probe: Who was in the room, and what was said? How did the older adult respond, at the time and afterwards?]_

- Was there a change you wanted that did not happen?
- _[Probe: What stopped it?]_

**Section 2: Veto, Escalation & Silence [revised]**

- **Main Goal:** Revocability from the caregiver's side, including the case where the caregiver is the one being overridden, and the interpretation of non-response.

- Did the agent ever tell you something about the medicines that you knew to be wrong?
- _[Probe: What did it say, what was actually the case, and what did you do?]_

- Were you able to stop it doing something?
- _[Probe: What did you do, and did it accept that, or did it come back?]_

- Did it ever repeat or escalate an alert after you had dealt with it?
- _[Probe: How did that land, and what did you do the second time? If a weakening veto is not verified in the build: if the agent were to keep pressing after you told it to stop, because the risk was rising, what would you want it to do?]_

- Did the older adult ever stop the agent from telling you something?
- _[Probe: How do you know, and what did you think of that?]_

- What happened when nobody answered a reminder?
- _[Probe: How long before anything else occurred, and what occurred?]_

- When you saw that a reminder had not been answered, what did you assume?
- _[Probe: Were you usually right? Tell me about a time you were wrong.]_

- Were there times when not answering was the ordinary thing?
- _[Probe: What made those different from a real problem, and how should the agent tell them apart?]_

- Is there something the agent should not be able to do even if you ask it to?

**Section 3: Care Collectivism & Checking [revised]**

- **Main Goal:** Whether the agent displaced, absorbed, or created occasions for checking, and how household disagreements resolve when the agent holds a record. Do not assert that checking expresses affection; let the answer come out either way.

- Before the agent, how did you find out whether a dose had been taken? And how do you find out now?

- Has anything changed about how often you speak to the older adult about medicines?
- _[Probe: More, less, or the same, and about what?]_

- Is there a conversation you used to have that you no longer have?
- _[Probe: Do you miss it, or was it a relief to drop?]_

- Has the older adult said anything about the checking?
- _[Probe: Their words, as close as you can recall.]_

- Does anyone check on you now, on whether you did your part?
- _[Probe: Who, and how does that sit with you?]_

- Did the agent's record ever disagree with what someone in the family said had happened?
- _[Probe: Tell me about it. Whose account was accepted?]_

- Was there a health decision to make while you were using it?
- _[Probe: Was the agent part of that conversation, out of the way, or in the way? Is there a decision you would not want it involved in at all?]_

**Section 4: Scores & Streaks as Relational Triggers [revised]**

- **Main Goal:** Whether scores functioned relationally, without asserting in the question that they did. The caregiver may hold a score, watch someone else's, or have ignored them.

- Did you notice the points or the streak?
- _[Probe: Whose score was it, and did you have one of your own?]_

- Did anyone in the family ever mention it out loud?
- _[Probe: Who, to whom, and what did they say?]_

- Was a streak broken during the weeks you used it?
- _[Probe: How did you hear about it, what was said and by whom, and what happened over the next few days?]_

- Did the score change anything about what you actually did?

- Should the score be visible to the family, or kept private?
- _[Probe: Who should decide that, and can they change their mind later?]_

**Section 5: Probationary Trust & Retraining [revised]**

- **Main Goal:** Trust formation and the fate of the caregiver's own memory. Ask what they did at the start, not whether they placed the agent on probation.

- Think back to the first days. What did you do differently then from now?
- _[Probe: Did you keep doing anything alongside it, and for how long?]_

- Was there a point at which you stopped double-checking it?
- _[Probe: What had happened by then?]_

- Did it get something wrong early on?
- _[Probe: What did you do, and did that set you back? Would you rely on it for a medicine that was new to the household?]_

- Do you still keep the schedule in your head?
- _[Probe: All of it, or parts? Which parts went?]_

- If the agent stopped working tomorrow morning, what would happen?
- _[Probe: Walk me through that day. Do you still keep the prescription where you can reach it?]_

**Section 6: Closing & The Plural Principal [executed]**

- **Main Goal:** Whom the agent serves, asked last so that it draws on the episodes already narrated rather than framing them.

- Taking everything together, who would you say the agent works for?
- _[Probe: Has that answer changed over the weeks?]_

- If your interest and the older adult's interest pulled in opposite directions, whose side should it take?
- _[Probe: Can you think of an occasion when they did pull apart?]_

- When is it acceptable for it to go from working quietly with one person to speaking on their behalf to the family?
- _[Probe: What would make that unacceptable, and should it be able to make that shift on its own or should someone have to allow it?]_

- Is there anything it did that you would call a betrayal of somebody in this house?

- Take me through one recent occasion involving the agent, from beginning to end, starting before anything happened. **[specified]**
- _[Probe for the time of day, who was where, what the agent did first and next, who spoke to whom, and how it ended. Then: what do you think the older adult would say about that same occasion? Record that prediction separately, put the same episode to the older adult independently, and match both against the decision log.]_

- Would you keep using it, and on what condition?
- _[Probe: If you could change one thing, what would it be? Is there anything we have not covered that mattered?]_

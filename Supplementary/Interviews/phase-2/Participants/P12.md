# Phase 2 Interview Transcript

**Participant:** P12  
**Age:** 71 years old

## Phase 2: Post-Deployment Evaluation Interview

### Section 1: The Affiliation Ledger and Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** Now that you have been using the agent for some time, how did it feel when it openly told you who it was working for?

**P12:** Hmm, at first it felt a little different. Usually my mobile alarm just rings, it doesn't say anything about working for me or family. But I liked knowing. If it says it is working for me first, then I know what it is doing.

**Interviewer:** Did that make the technology feel more transparent, or did it feel intrusive at home?

**P12:** More transparent. I think if something is going to tell my family, I should know. That is better. Intrusive, maybe if it tells them every small thing. My husband asks me sometimes already, so family knowing is okay. But not every little delay.

**Interviewer:** Can you walk me through the last time the agent shifted from being private to involving your family?

**P12:** There was one night when I was busy and the reminder came. I heard it but I thought I will take it after finishing what I was doing. Then I forgot for some time. The agent asked again and then asked about family. I allowed it. My husband asked me if I had taken the medicine. Then I remembered and took it.

**Interviewer:** Did allowing the agent to involve your family feel like giving up your independence?

**P12:** No, not really. I still take my own medicine. Family asking is normal. Sometimes I ignore the alarm, so if my husband asks then it helps me. I don't feel like I am losing independence.

**Interviewer:** So it felt more like mutual trust?

**P12:** Yes, yes. I trust my family. If I am forgetting, they can help.

### Section 2: Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** When the agent wanted to alert your family about a missed dose, did you ever use the veto?

**P12:** Yes, sometimes. If I had already taken it and the phone was showing that I did not confirm, then I stopped it. Otherwise my family may think I forgot.

**Interviewer:** How did you react when the agent pushed back on your veto because the health risk was becoming higher?

**P12:** At first I was a little annoyed. I thought, okay, I will do it, why are you asking again. But if I really did not take it, then I understand why it is pushing. If it just keeps quiet, then I may forget completely.

**Interviewer:** Did that feel like the agent was taking control away from you?

**P12:** No, because it gives me a chance first. If I don't respond, then it asks again. That is okay. But I would not like it if it immediately told my family.

**Interviewer:** When you were silent and did not respond, how did the agent handle it?

**P12:** It reminded me again. Sometimes I am busy or outside. Sometimes I hear it but say later. So another reminder is useful.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel it understood your silence as a normal part of your routine, or did it treat silence as a failure?

**P12:** Mostly normal. But if it keeps saying again and again, then I feel irritated. Sometimes I am not forgetting, I am just busy for some time.

### Section 3: Care Collectivism and Checking as Love

**Interviewer:** Before the agent, your family would sometimes ask whether you had taken your medicine. How has that changed?

**P12:** They still ask sometimes. But now the agent asks first, so maybe they don't have to ask every time. Before my husband would say, did you take it, especially if I was busy. Now the phone can remind me.

**Interviewer:** Do you miss those manual check-ins from your husband or family?

**P12:** No, I don't think so. But when my husband asks, I don't mind. It is different. The phone is a reminder, but family asking is also care.

**Interviewer:** Does the agent's notification give your family a new reason to talk to you or show care?

**P12:** Yes. If I don't respond, my husband may ask me what happened. Then I tell him. So it can start the conversation. But it should not make him worry for every small thing.

**Interviewer:** Has the agent reduced family care or changed how care happens?

**P12:** Changed it. The phone does some of the reminding, but family is still there. If there is a problem, I talk to them.

**Interviewer:** Think about a time when a prescription was unclear or you had to make a sudden health decision. Did the agent help in the family discussion or get in the way?

**P12:** If the prescription is unclear, I still need to ask someone. Sometimes the writing is difficult. The agent can tell me what information is already there, but I would not just decide from that. I would ask the doctor or pharmacist.

**Interviewer:** So it can be part of the discussion but not make the final decision?

**P12:** Yes, exactly. It can remind and explain simple things. If something is changing, then I need the doctor.

### Section 4: Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** When you reached a high score or a long streak, how did your family react?

**P12:** They noticed it. My husband asked about it once and said, good, you are taking it properly. I was also happy. It made it a little interesting.

**Interviewer:** Did your husband ever use the score to praise or encourage you?

**P12:** Yes, sometimes. He would say keep it going. Nothing serious, just saying good.

**Interviewer:** Think about a time when a streak was broken. Did losing the streak cause anxiety or did your family encourage you?

**P12:** I felt a little bad because I had kept it. It broke because I was busy and forgot. My husband said, okay, take it properly from now. So then I was okay. It did not become a big problem.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer keeping your score private, or does sharing it with your family make it better?

**P12:** Sharing with family is okay. It can encourage me. But I don't want them checking the score all the time. The main thing is taking the medicine.

### Section 5: Probationary Trust and Retraining

**Interviewer:** When the agent was first introduced, did you put it on a probationary period?

**P12:** Yes, I think so. First I wanted to see if the reminder came at the right time. I already had my mobile alarm, so I knew when my usual medicine time was.

**Interviewer:** How did you test whether the agent was trustworthy?

**P12:** I compared it with my alarm and my normal routine. If it reminded me around the correct time, then I was comfortable. I also checked if it was saying the correct medicine. After some days, I trusted it more.

**Interviewer:** Did your existing mobile alarm make it easier to judge the agent?

**P12:** Yes. I already knew how a reminder should work. If the new one was clear and came at the right time, then it was useful.

**Interviewer:** Over the past few weeks, do you feel relying on the agent has replaced your own memory, or has it retrained your internal sense of when a dose is due?

**P12:** Maybe it has helped the memory. I still know I have to take it at night. But now I expect the reminder also. Sometimes I know the time is coming before it rings. So it has not replaced my memory.

**Interviewer:** If the agent stopped working tomorrow, would you still know when to take your medicine?

**P12:** Yes. I had the mobile alarm before this. So I would manage. But when I am travelling, it is more difficult. That is where the agent helps more.

### Section 6: Closing and the Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** If you had to summarize, who do you feel this AI agent ultimately works for, you, your family, or both?

**P12:** Both. It works for me because I need to remember my medicine. But family also gets involved if I forget. So both.

**Interviewer:** Under what conditions is it acceptable for the agent to stop acting as your private tool and start acting as your family's advocate?

**P12:** If I miss it and don't respond after the reminders, then family should know. If it happens repeatedly, definitely. But if I am just late for some minutes, give me some time first.

**Interviewer:** So you would want a private reminder first, then family involvement if needed?

**P12:** Yes, yes. First tell me. Then another reminder. If I still don't take it, then tell my husband or family.

**Interviewer:** What would make that balance feel right for you?

**P12:** Not too many reminders, not too quick to tell family. But also don't stay quiet if I really forget. That balance is good.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned travelling is difficult for you. Would you want the agent to help with that too?

**P12:** Yes, definitely. Sometimes I carry the medicine but it stays inside the bag and I forget. If it reminds me before I leave and then reminds me at the right time, that would help.

**Interviewer:** What kind of reminder works best for you?

**P12:** Voice. I prefer voice. Sometimes there are many notifications and I don't look at the phone. If someone says take your medicine, I hear it.

**Interviewer:** Would you want it to repeat the reminder if you don't respond?

**P12:** Yes, after some time. Not every minute. Maybe one more time. If it keeps talking, it will become irritating.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the agent to read your prescription?

**P12:** Yes. That would be useful. Sometimes prescription writing is difficult for me. If I can take a picture and the phone tells me the medicine name and when to take it, that is good.

**Interviewer:** What information should it give you?

**P12:** Medicine name, when to take, how many times. Maybe before or after food if that is written. Simple information. Not too much.

**Interviewer:** Would large text or pictures also help?

**P12:** Yes, large text is good. Pictures also can help. But voice is still better for me.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the app to speak in Bangla?

**P12:** Yes, Bangla would be easier. Simple Bangla, normal speaking.

**Interviewer:** Would you be comfortable with your family seeing your medicine reminders?

**P12:** Yes, family is okay. My husband already asks sometimes. But other people don't need to know.

**Interviewer:** What would make you stop using the agent?

**P12:** If it gives wrong information, then I will not trust it. Or if it gives too many notifications. It should help, not disturb.

**Interviewer:** Any final thoughts about having the agent as part of your home?

**P12:** Hmm, it is useful. I already use a mobile alarm, so I don't need something very complicated. Just remind me properly, help when I am outside, and tell my family if I really don't respond. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Thank you very much.

**P12:** You're welcome.

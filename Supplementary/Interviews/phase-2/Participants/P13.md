# Phase 2 Interview Transcript

**Participant:** P13  
**Age:** 66 years old

## Phase 2: Post-Deployment Evaluation Interview

### Section 1: The Affiliation Ledger and Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** Now that you have been using the agent for some time, how did it feel when it openly told you who it was working for?

**P13:** Hmm, it felt okay. A little strange in the beginning. But I liked that it told me clearly. If it is working for me, then I know it is reminding me. And if it is working with my family, then I also know why they are getting something.

**Interviewer:** Did that make it feel more transparent, or did it feel intrusive at home?

**P13:** More transparent. I think it is better to know. My family already knows about my medicine. So I don't have a problem with them knowing. But if it tells them every small thing, then maybe that is too much.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time the agent changed from working privately with you to involving your family?

**P13:** One night I was busy with some work at home. The reminder came and I heard it, but I thought I will take it after some time. Then I forgot. It reminded me again and then there was the option to involve family. I allowed it. Someone at home asked me if I had taken the medicine. Then I remembered.

**Interviewer:** Did that feel like giving up your independence, or more like an act of trust?

**P13:** Trust. I don't think I am giving up independence. I still take the medicine myself. My family already helps me remember sometimes. So this is just another way.

### Section 2: Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** When the agent wanted to alert your family about a missed dose, did you ever use the veto?

**P13:** Yes, sometimes. If I had already taken the medicine but had not responded to the phone, then I stopped it. I don't want my family to think I missed it when I didn't.

**Interviewer:** How did you react when the agent pushed back on your veto because the health risk was becoming higher?

**P13:** At first I felt a little annoyed. I thought, yes, I know, I will take it. But if I actually did not take it, then maybe it is right to ask again. If I keep saying no and still don't take it, then family should know.

**Interviewer:** Did that feel like the agent was taking control away from you?

**P13:** No, not if it gives me some time first. First remind me, then let me respond. If I don't respond, then okay, tell my family.

**Interviewer:** When you were silent and did not respond, how did the agent handle it?

**P13:** It reminded me again. Sometimes I am busy. Sometimes I am outside. So silence does not always mean I forgot. Sometimes I just cannot take the medicine at that exact moment.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel it understood your silence as part of your routine, or did it treat silence as a failure?

**P13:** Mostly it gave me another chance. That was good. But if it keeps reminding too much, then it becomes irritating. One reminder and then another after some time is enough.

### Section 3: Care Collectivism and Checking as Love

**Interviewer:** Before this agent, your family sometimes checked whether you had taken your medicine. How has that changed?

**P13:** They still ask sometimes. But now the phone reminds me first. Before, someone at home might ask, did you take your medicine. Now the agent does that part first.

**Interviewer:** Do you miss the manual check-ins from your family?

**P13:** No, not really. But I don't mind when they ask. If I forget, family reminding me is helpful. I am used to that.

**Interviewer:** Does the agent's notification give your family a new reason to talk to you and show care?

**P13:** Yes. If I don't respond, they may ask me. Then I remember or explain why I didn't take it. So it can help them know when I need help.

**Interviewer:** Has the agent reduced family care, or changed the way care happens?

**P13:** Changed. It has not reduced it. The phone does some reminding, but family is still involved when there is a problem.

**Interviewer:** Think about a time when a prescription was unclear or you had to make a sudden health decision. Did the agent help in the family discussion or get in the way?

**P13:** If the prescription is unclear, I would still ask my doctor or pharmacist. The agent can show me the medicine information if it has it, but I won't just decide from that. Sometimes I cannot read the prescription properly, so I need someone to explain it.

**Interviewer:** So the agent can help the family understand something, but it should not make the final medicine decision?

**P13:** Yes, exactly. It can tell me the name, time, dose, things like that. But if something changes, doctor first.

### Section 4: Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** When you reached a high score or a long streak, how did your family react?

**P13:** They noticed it and said good. Sometimes they asked how many days I had taken it properly. It was nice. It gave me a little encouragement.

**Interviewer:** Did anyone use your score to praise or encourage you?

**P13:** Yes, family did. They would say, good, keep it going. It was not a big thing, just encouragement.

**Interviewer:** Think about a time when a streak was broken. Did losing the streak cause anxiety, or did your family encourage you?

**P13:** I felt a little bad because I had kept it. But family said okay, it happened, start again. So I did not worry too much. If they encourage me, then it is fine.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer keeping your score private, or does sharing it with your family make the experience better?

**P13:** Family can know. I don't have a problem with that. It is actually better if they encourage me. But they don't need to keep checking it all the time.

### Section 5: Probationary Trust and Retraining

**Interviewer:** When the agent was first introduced, did you put it on a probationary period?

**P13:** Yes, I did not trust it immediately. First I wanted to see if it gives the right reminder. I know my own medicine routine, so I could check whether it was correct.

**Interviewer:** How did you test whether it was trustworthy?

**P13:** I watched it for some days. I checked the medicine time and then saw if the agent reminded me correctly. I also checked the medicine information. If something was not clear, I asked my family.

**Interviewer:** Did your family help you test it?

**P13:** Yes. If there was something on the phone that I did not understand, they helped me. They are more comfortable with some phone settings.

**Interviewer:** Over the past few weeks, do you feel relying on the agent has replaced your own memory, or has it retrained your internal sense of when a dose is due?

**P13:** Maybe it has helped my memory. Before I was just trying to remember myself. Now I know the medicine time better because the reminder comes. But I still forget sometimes. So it has not completely replaced my memory.

**Interviewer:** If the agent stopped working tomorrow, would you still know when to take your medicine?

**P13:** I think yes. I would manage. But I would probably forget more often because there would be no reminder. Especially when I am busy or travelling.

### Section 6: Closing and the Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** If you had to summarize, who do you feel this AI agent ultimately works for, you, your family, or both?

**P13:** Both. It is mainly helping me remember. But family is also part of it because they help me when I forget. So both.

**Interviewer:** Under what conditions is it acceptable for the agent to stop acting as your private tool and start acting as your family's advocate?

**P13:** If I don't take the medicine after the reminders, then family should know. Especially if I don't respond at all. But if I am only late for some time, give me a chance first.

**Interviewer:** So you would want the agent to try you first before involving your family?

**P13:** Yes, yes. First remind me. Then remind me again. If I still don't take it, then family can get the message.

**Interviewer:** Would you want one family member to receive the notification, or everyone?

**P13:** One family member is enough. If everyone gets it, then everybody may start asking me. One person can ask me and then it is done.

**Interviewer:** What would make that balance feel right for you?

**P13:** Not too many reminders and not too quick to involve family. But if I really forget, then don't stay quiet. Family should know.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned travelling is one of the times when you forget. How could the agent help then?

**P13:** It could remind me before I leave to carry the medicine. That is useful because sometimes I forget to carry it. Then after I leave, it should remind me at the normal time also.

**Interviewer:** What kind of reminder works best for you?

**P13:** Voice. Voice is better. I don't always look at the phone. If I hear someone saying it is time to take the medicine, I notice.

**Interviewer:** Would you want it to repeat the reminder?

**P13:** Yes, maybe once or twice. Not every minute. If it keeps repeating, it will become irritating.

**Interviewer:** Would you want it to read your prescription?

**P13:** Yes, that would help. I have difficulty reading prescriptions sometimes. If I can take a picture and it tells me the medicine name and dose, that would be good.

**Interviewer:** What information would you want it to give you?

**P13:** Medicine name, dose, time. Maybe what it is for. Simple things. If there is too much information, I may get confused.

**Interviewer:** Would large text or a picture of the medicine help?

**P13:** Yes, large text is good. Picture also helps because I can match the packet.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer voice or text?

**P13:** Voice. I prefer voice because I can hear it without looking.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the agent to speak in Bangla?

**P13:** Yes, simple Bangla would be easier. Normal words.

**Interviewer:** Would you be comfortable with your family seeing your medicine reminders?

**P13:** Yes, family is okay. They already know about my medicine. Other people don't need to know.

**Interviewer:** What would make you stop using the agent?

**P13:** If it gives wrong information. Then I will not trust it. Or if it keeps disturbing me with too many notifications. It should help me, not irritate me.

**Interviewer:** Any final thoughts about having the agent as part of your home?

**P13:** Hmm, it is useful. I think the main thing is remembering. If the phone can remind me, help me when I am travelling, and tell my family if I really don't respond, that is enough. No need for too many things.

**Interviewer:** Thank you very much.

**P13:** You're welcome.

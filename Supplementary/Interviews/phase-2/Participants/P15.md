# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript

**Respondent: Dipendra Lal Basu, 80 years, Male**

**Interviewer:** So now after using the medicine reminder system, can you tell me how you found it?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** It was okay. I used it with some help from my family. I am used to following the prescription, so the reminder was not something I depended on before. But I understood the basic reminder.

**Interviewer:** Did you understand who the system was working for?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** Mainly for me, because I am taking the medicines. But family can also know if there is some issue. That part is okay. If I am not responding, then they can check with me.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel about the system sharing medicine information with your family?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** I did not have much problem with it. My family can help if something is difficult. I already take help from family and doctors when required. But I would not want unnecessary information to be shared.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel that the system was interfering with your independence?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** No, not really. I still take my medicine myself. The system only reminds. I have been managing my medicines for a long time, so I don't want somebody doing everything for me.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer the system to work independently or together with your family?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** Together is better. I know my medicines and prescription, but if there is some problem family can help. The doctor is still important.

## Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** Suppose the system says that you have missed a dose. Would you want your family to know?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** If I actually missed it, yes. They can check with me. But sometimes there can be a technical problem or I may not respond even though I have taken it.

**Interviewer:** What if the system becomes more urgent because you have not responded?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** It can remind me again. If there is still no response, family can be informed. But it should not keep disturbing continuously.

**Interviewer:** If the system tells you to take a medicine and you think something is wrong, what would you do?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** I would not take it blindly. I would check the prescription. If necessary I would ask the doctor. I always follow the prescription. I do not go beyond it.

**Interviewer:** Would you ever ignore or reject what the system tells you?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** Yes, if it does not match the prescription. The prescription is the authority for me. The system should follow that, not make its own decision.

**Interviewer:** What if you do not respond to a reminder?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** No response does not necessarily mean I have not taken the medicine. I may have taken it and not responded to the system. So it should wait and then perhaps ask again.

**Interviewer:** What would be a serious failure for you?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** Giving the wrong medicine or wrong timing. Also if it tells my family that I missed something when I did not. That can create unnecessary concern.

**Interviewer:** Has the system changed your normal medicine routine?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** No. I still follow the prescription as before. I take the medicines according to the written schedule.

## Closing and Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** Finally, who do you think the system works for, you, your family, or both?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** Both, in some way. It helps me with reminders, and it can help family know if there is a problem. But I am still the person taking the medicine.

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** If there is a real missed dose and I do not respond, then family should know. Especially if there is some risk. But it should not involve them unnecessarily.

**Interviewer:** Should it contact your family immediately when you do not respond?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** No. First remind me again. Then if there is still no response, inform them. There should be some time because I may have taken the medicine and simply not responded.

**Interviewer:** What is the most useful part of the system for you?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** The reminder itself. Because I have many medicines, eight to ten in total, so the timing can be important. If it clearly tells me what is due, that can help.

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** That can be useful. I have missed doses before when the medicine stock ran out or the medicine was unavailable to buy. If the system can give an early reminder that the stock is finishing, that can help avoid that problem.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the family to receive that refill reminder?

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** Yes, that could be useful. If they know in advance, they can arrange the medicine. That is better than finding out after it has finished.

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** No. My family can help set it up. The prescription should be used. I don't want to enter everything manually.

**Dipendra Lal Basu:** It should not change my medicines. It should not give advice beyond the prescription without proper medical confirmation. It should not keep disturbing me. And it should not create unnecessary confusion.

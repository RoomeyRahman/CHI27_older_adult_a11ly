# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript

**Respondent: P15, 80 years, Male**

**Interviewer:** So now after using the medicine reminder system, can you tell me how you found it?

**P15:** It was okay. I used it with some help from my family. I am used to following the prescription, so the reminder was not something I depended on before. But I understood the basic reminder.

**Interviewer:** Did you understand who the system was working for?

**P15:** Mainly for me, because I am taking the medicines. But family can also know if there is some issue. That part is okay. If I am not responding, then they can check with me.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel about the system sharing medicine information with your family?

**P15:** I did not have much problem with it. My family can help if something is difficult. I already take help from family and doctors when required. But I would not want unnecessary information to be shared.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel that the system was interfering with your independence?

**P15:** No, not really. I still take my medicine myself. The system only reminds. I have been managing my medicines for a long time, so I don't want somebody doing everything for me.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer the system to work independently or together with your family?

**P15:** Together is better. I know my medicines and prescription, but if there is some problem family can help. The doctor is still important.

---

## Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** Suppose the system says that you have missed a dose. Would you want your family to know?

**P15:** If I actually missed it, yes. They can check with me. But sometimes there can be a technical problem or I may not respond even though I have taken it.

**Interviewer:** What if the system becomes more urgent because you have not responded?

**P15:** It can remind me again. If there is still no response, family can be informed. But it should not keep disturbing continuously.

**Interviewer:** If the system tells you to take a medicine and you think something is wrong, what would you do?

**P15:** I would not take it blindly. I would check the prescription. If necessary I would ask the doctor. I always follow the prescription. I do not go beyond it.

**Interviewer:** Would you ever ignore or reject what the system tells you?

**P15:** Yes, if it does not match the prescription. The prescription is the authority for me. The system should follow that, not make its own decision.

**Interviewer:** What if you do not respond to a reminder?

**P15:** No response does not necessarily mean I have not taken the medicine. I may have taken it and not responded to the system. So it should wait and then perhaps ask again.

**Interviewer:** What would be a serious failure for you?

**P15:** Giving the wrong medicine or wrong timing. Also if it tells my family that I missed something when I did not. That can create unnecessary concern.

**Interviewer:** Has the system changed your normal medicine routine?

**P15:** No. I still follow the prescription as before. I take the medicines according to the written schedule.

---

## Care Collectivism and Checking as Love

**Interviewer:** Has the system changed how your family checks on your medicines?

**P15:** A little. They may check the system if there is a problem. But normally I still manage myself. Family helps when required.

**Interviewer:** Do you miss the usual way of managing your medicines without the system?

**P15:** No. I can still do that. The system is only an additional help.

**Interviewer:** If your family checks whether you have taken your medicine, does that feel like interference?

**P15:** No, if they are checking because there is a concern. Family helping is normal. But they should not keep checking every small thing.

**Interviewer:** What if there is confusion about a prescription?

**P15:** Then I check the written prescription and ask the doctor if needed. If I have difficulty, family can help me understand it.

**Interviewer:** Would you discuss a sudden change in your medicines with your family?

**P15:** Yes. If the doctor changes something, I would tell my family. They can also help make sure the new medicine is correct.

**Interviewer:** Would you allow the system to decide about a new medicine?

**P15:** No. No. The doctor decides. The system can remind me about what is already prescribed. It should not decide a new medicine.

---

## Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** Did you notice the points or streak for taking medicines?

**P15:** Yes, I noticed it. I understood that it was showing regular medicine taking.

**Interviewer:** Did you find that useful?

**P15:** It is okay. I don't need points to know that I have to take my medicines. I have been following the prescription for a long time.

**Interviewer:** How did your family react when you maintained a good streak?

**P15:** They were happy. They said it was good that I was regular. But it was not a major thing. The medicine itself is more important than the score.

**Interviewer:** Did praise or encouragement from family matter?

**P15:** Yes, family encouragement is always good. But I don't take medicine because of the points. I take it because the doctor prescribed it.

**Interviewer:** What if you broke a streak?

**P15:** I would not become very worried. I would check whether I actually missed the medicine and continue from there. One broken streak should not create panic.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the score to be private or shared with your family?

**P15:** Family can know. I don't see a problem with that. But there is no need for other people to know.

**Interviewer:** Did the score change your behaviour?

**P15:** Maybe slightly. It makes you notice the routine. But my main habit is still following the prescription.

---

## Probationary Trust and Retraining

**Interviewer:** In the beginning, did you feel that the system had to prove that it was trustworthy?

**P15:** Yes. Any new system should be checked. My family helped me see whether the medicine and timing were entered correctly.

**Interviewer:** What did you check first?

**P15:** I checked whether it matched the written prescription. Then whether the reminder came at the correct time. If those things are correct, then it can be useful.

**Interviewer:** Did you become more comfortable with it after using it?

**P15:** Yes. After some use, I understood how it worked. But I would still check if the prescription changes.

**Interviewer:** Did the system replace your memory?

**P15:** No. I have been remembering my medicines for many years. It is more like an additional reminder.

**Interviewer:** Did it change your sense of when you should take your medicines?

**P15:** Not much. The written prescription already gives me the routine. The system just makes the timing more noticeable.

**Interviewer:** If the system stopped working, would you still be able to manage your medicines?

**P15:** Yes. I would go back to the prescription and my normal routine. If I am severely ill or there is some difficulty, then I would take help from family, doctors, or healthcare professionals.

**Interviewer:** So would you say the system retrained you?

**P15:** No, I would not say that. It helped remind me, but the main routine was already there.

---

## Closing and Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** Finally, who do you think the system works for, you, your family, or both?

**P15:** Both, in some way. It helps me with reminders, and it can help family know if there is a problem. But I am still the person taking the medicine.

**Interviewer:** When would it be acceptable for the system to become a family advocate?

**P15:** If there is a real missed dose and I do not respond, then family should know. Especially if there is some risk. But it should not involve them unnecessarily.

**Interviewer:** Should it contact your family immediately when you do not respond?

**P15:** No. First remind me again. Then if there is still no response, inform them. There should be some time because I may have taken the medicine and simply not responded.

**Interviewer:** What is the most useful part of the system for you?

**P15:** The reminder itself. Because I have many medicines, eight to ten in total, so the timing can be important. If it clearly tells me what is due, that can help.

**Interviewer:** Would showing the medicine name and timing be useful?

**P15:** Yes. It should show the medicine and the time clearly. I follow the prescription, so the system should make that information easier to follow.

**Interviewer:** What about medicine stock or refill reminders?

**P15:** That can be useful. I have missed doses before when the medicine stock ran out or the medicine was unavailable to buy. If the system can give an early reminder that the stock is finishing, that can help avoid that problem.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the family to receive that refill reminder?

**P15:** Yes, that could be useful. If they know in advance, they can arrange the medicine. That is better than finding out after it has finished.

**Interviewer:** Would you want the system to give you information about medicines other people are taking?

**P15:** No. I don't need that. I follow my prescription. Other people's medicines may be different.

**Interviewer:** What if many people tell you that another medicine is better?

**P15:** I can listen to them. But I would not change my medicine because of that. I would ask the doctor. People can have different experiences.

**Interviewer:** Would group opinion make you more interested in asking about a medicine?

**P15:** Maybe. If many people are saying something, I may ask the doctor about it. But asking is different from taking it.

**Interviewer:** How should the system speak to you?

**P15:** Clearly and simply. It should say which medicine and when. No unnecessary complicated information.

**Interviewer:** Would you want voice reminders?

**P15:** Yes, voice can be useful. If I am not looking at the phone, I can still hear the reminder.

**Interviewer:** Should you have to enter all the medicine information yourself?

**P15:** No. My family can help set it up. The prescription should be used. I don't want to enter everything manually.

**Interviewer:** What should the system not do?

**P15:** It should not change my medicines. It should not give advice beyond the prescription without proper medical confirmation. It should not keep disturbing me. And it should not create unnecessary confusion.

**Interviewer:** If you could keep only one feature, what would you keep?

**P15:** The medicine timing reminder. That is the main useful thing.

**Interviewer:** And one feature for your family?

**P15:** A notification if there is a real missed dose or if the medicine stock is finishing. That can help them arrange things.

**Interviewer:** Do you think technology can replace doctors?

**P15:** No. Definitely not. Technology can remind and organise. Doctor decides the medicine. If I am severely ill, I need doctors or healthcare professionals.

**Interviewer:** Do you think technology can replace family support?

**P15:** No. Family can help when there is difficulty. The system can make some things easier, but it should not replace family.

**Interviewer:** Anything else you want to add?

**P15:** No. I think it can be useful if it follows the prescription properly. I have many medicines, so clear timing can help. But I will still follow the prescription and consult the doctor when needed.

**Interviewer:** Thank you.

**P15:** Thank you.

# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript: Medicine & Technology Usage

**Participant:** Saraswati Rani Basu  
**Age:** 76 years old  
**Gender:** Female

---

## 1. Affiliation Ledger & Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** "After using the medicine reminder system, how did you feel about who the system was helping?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "It was helping me mainly. I take my medicines myself, so the reminder was for me. But my husband also helps me sometimes, so he was also involved."

**Interviewer:** "Did you feel the system was clear about what it was doing, or did it feel like it was getting too involved?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "No, it was clear. It was only helping with the medicine. I don't think it needs to do anything more than that."

**Interviewer:** "Did you start sharing the information from the system with your husband?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Yes, sometimes. Since he already notes down my medicines and reminds me if I forget, it is natural that he knows. But I can still manage my medicines myself."

**Interviewer:** "Would you prefer the system to work independently or together with you and your husband?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Together. I can take the medicines myself, but if I forget then my husband can help. The system can also help. That is better."

---

## 2. Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** "If the system gave you a medicine reminder that did not match your notes, what would you do?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "I would check my notes first. I would not just take it because the system said it. If there is something different, I would ask my husband or check with the doctor."

**Interviewer:** "What if the system gave you a stronger warning because it thought you had missed a dose?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "That would be okay. If I really missed it, then it should remind me. But I don't want too much noise or too many warnings."

**Interviewer:** "What if you did not respond to the reminder?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "It can remind me again after some time. If I still don't respond, then my husband can check with me."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want your husband to be informed every time you miss a reminder?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "No, not every time. Sometimes I may already have taken the medicine. If I am actually missing it, then he can help. So one reminder first is enough."

**Interviewer:** "How would you know whether it was a normal reminder or something more serious?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "If it is just the normal medicine time, that is a normal reminder. If I keep missing it or there is some problem with the medicine, then my husband or doctor should be involved."

---

## 3. Care Collectivism & "Checking as Love"

**Interviewer:** "Has the system changed how you and your husband check on your medicines?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "A little. I still follow my notes. Before, my husband would remind me whenever I forgot. Now the system can remind me too, so he doesn't have to remember every time."

**Interviewer:** "Do you miss your husband reminding you manually?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "No no. He still reminds me sometimes. It is helpful when he does. The system just makes it easier."

**Interviewer:** "If there was a sudden change in your medicine or some confusion about a prescription, would you follow the system?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "No. I would check with my husband and doctor. If the medicine changes, my notes have to be changed also. I would not want the system to decide that by itself."

**Interviewer:** "So for normal timing the system can help, but for a medicine change you would still discuss it?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Yes. That is better. The system can remind me, but doctor should tell me about the medicine."

---

## 4. Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** "If the system showed you a good score or a long streak for taking your medicines, would that be useful to you?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "I don't think I need a score. I just need to take my medicines properly. If it shows that I am doing well, that is okay, but the number is not important to me."

**Interviewer:** "What if it gave you a simple message saying that you were keeping up with your medicines?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "That is fine. It can be encouraging. But I don't need a big thing for it."

**Interviewer:** "If you missed a dose and the streak was broken, would that make you anxious?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "No. I would just want to know that I missed it and then manage it properly. I don't want to worry about a score."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want your husband to see that progress?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "He can see it if needed. He already helps me with the medicines. But there is no need to show many details."

---

## 5. Probationary Trust & Retraining

**Interviewer:** "When you first started using the system, did you need some time before trusting it?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Yes, I think so. I already had my notes and I was used to following them. So first I would compare the reminder with my notes."

**Interviewer:** "What would make you trust the system?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "If it keeps reminding me at the correct times and the information matches my notes, then I can trust it more. My husband can also check it in the beginning."

**Interviewer:** "Did the system replace your notes, or did it just give you another way to remember?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "It did not replace my notes. I still prefer having them. The reminder is just another help. I don't have to depend only on memory then."

**Interviewer:** "Would you continue keeping your medicine notes?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Yes. Definitely. I am already used to them, and my husband also notes things for me. So I would keep doing that."

---

## 6. Closing & Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** "After using the system, who do you think it ultimately works for—you, your husband, or both of you?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Both of us, I think. I take the medicines myself, so it helps me. But my husband also helps me when I forget and he notes the medicines. So it helps him also."

**Interviewer:** "Would you be comfortable if the system told your husband when you missed a medicine?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Yes, if I really missed it. That can be useful. He already reminds me sometimes, so it is not a problem."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want him to be notified about every small reminder?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "No no. That is not necessary. I can handle the normal reminders myself. He only needs to know if I am actually missing the medicine or if there is some problem."

**Interviewer:** "When would it be acceptable for the system to become more involved with your husband?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "If I miss medicines repeatedly, or if there is some change that I don't understand. Then he can help me. Otherwise I can manage it myself."

**Interviewer:** "Overall, has the system made your medicine routine easier?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Yes, a little. I already manage my medicines with my notes, so I did not need a lot of help. But the reminder is useful if I forget."

**Interviewer:** "Would you still prefer a simple system?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Yes. Very simple. I don't need many features. Just tell me when to take the medicine. If I have difficulty with anything, my husband can help."

**Interviewer:** "Anything else you would like to add?"

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "No, I don't think so. My notes are working well for me. The reminder is just extra help."

**Interviewer:** "Thank you for your time."

**Saraswati Rani Basu:** "Thank you."

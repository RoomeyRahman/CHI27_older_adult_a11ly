# Phase 2 Interview Transcript

**Participant:** P02  
**Age:** 65 years old  

## Phase 2: Post-Deployment Evaluation Interview

### Section 1: The Affiliation Ledger and Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** Okay, so now that you have been using the agent for some time, how did you feel when it openly told you who it was working for?

**P02:** Hmm, at first it felt a little strange. I am not used to something talking like that. But actually it is good because I know what it is doing. If it says it is working for me, then I understand, okay, this is for my medicine.

**Interviewer:** Did that make it feel more transparent, or did it sometimes feel intrusive at home?

**P02:** More transparent. I don't mind it telling me. At home also, my family already knows about my medicine. So that part is not a big problem. But I don't want every small reminder to go to everyone.

**Interviewer:** Can you tell me about the last time the agent changed from working privately with you to involving your family?

**P02:** Yes, there was one day when I was outside for some time. I came back later than usual and the medicine time had passed. The agent reminded me. I was busy and did not respond properly. Then it asked about involving the family. I allowed it. Someone from the family asked me if I had taken the medicine. So it was like that.

**Interviewer:** Did allowing your family to become involved feel like giving up your independence?

**P02:** No, no. I don't think so. I still take the medicine myself. I only need help remembering sometimes. My family already asks me sometimes, so this is not very different.

**Interviewer:** So it felt more like trust?

**P02:** Yes, trust. If I am forgetting, then family can help. That is normal.

### Section 2: Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** When the agent wanted to alert your family about a missed dose, did you ever use the option to stop it?

**P02:** Yes, sometimes. If I knew I had taken the medicine, but maybe I had not confirmed it on the phone, then I stopped it. I don't want my family to worry unnecessarily.

**Interviewer:** What was your reaction when the agent pushed back on the veto because the risk was becoming higher?

**P02:** Hmm, I felt a little irritated first. I thought, I already said I will handle it. But then, yes, if I really have not taken it and I am not responding, then maybe it should ask again. Medicine is important.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel that the agent was taking control away from you?

**P02:** No. Not if it gives me the first chance. First remind me, then wait. If I don't respond, then it can involve the family. That is okay.

**Interviewer:** When you were silent and did not respond, how did the agent handle it?

**P02:** It reminded me again. Sometimes I am outside or talking with people, so I may not answer. It is not always that I forgot. Sometimes I am just busy.

**Interviewer:** Did you feel that the agent understood your silence as part of your routine?

**P02:** Mostly, yes. It gave me some time. That is good. But if it keeps reminding too many times, then it becomes irritating. Sometimes I may be doing something and I will come back to it.

### Section 3: Care Collectivism and Checking as Love

**Interviewer:** Before using the agent, your family sometimes checked whether you had taken your medicine. How has that changed?

**P02:** They still ask sometimes. But now the agent reminds me first, so maybe they don't have to ask every time. Before, if they saw I was busy, somebody might say, did you take your medicine. Now the phone can do that part.

**Interviewer:** Do you miss the manual check-ins from your family?

**P02:** No, not really. I don't need someone asking me every time. But if they ask normally, I don't mind. They are family.

**Interviewer:** Does the agent's notification give your family a new reason to talk to you and show care?

**P02:** Yes, sometimes. If I don't respond, then they ask me. So it can start a conversation. But it should not make them worry about every small delay.

**Interviewer:** Do you feel the agent has reduced the care from your family?

**P02:** No, no. It has only changed the way they check. Family is still there. If I have a problem, I will tell them.

**Interviewer:** Think about a time when a prescription was unclear or you had to make a sudden health decision. Did the agent help in the family discussion or get in the way?

**P02:** There was one time when I was not completely sure about some instruction. The agent could tell me what was already entered, but I did not want to make a decision only from that. I asked my family. If it is something important, I would ask the doctor also.

**Interviewer:** So the agent can help with information, but it should not decide for you?

**P02:** Yes, exactly. It can remind me. It can tell me what the instruction says if it is clear. But if something changes, I will ask someone.

### Section 4: Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** When you reached a high score or a long streak, how did your family react?

**P02:** They noticed it. They said, good, you are taking it properly. I was also happy. But I don't think about the score too much.

**Interviewer:** Did anyone use the score as a reason to praise or encourage you?

**P02:** Yes, sometimes. They would say, keep it going. It was nice. Not a big thing, but good.

**Interviewer:** Think about a time when the streak was broken. Did losing it cause anxiety, or did your family encourage you?

**P02:** I was a little disappointed. If you keep something for many days and then it breaks, you feel bad a little. But my family said, okay, it happened, take it properly next time. So it was encouragement.

**Interviewer:** Would you prefer keeping the score private, or does sharing it with your family make the experience better?

**P02:** With family, I don't mind sharing. It can be encouraging. But I don't want them checking it all the time. The medicine is important, the score is not the main thing.

### Section 5: Probationary Trust and Retraining

**Interviewer:** When the agent was first introduced, did you put it on a probationary period before trusting it?

**P02:** Yes, yes. I wanted to see whether it was giving the reminder at the right time. I have been taking the medicine myself for a long time, so I know my routine. First I compared the phone with what I normally do.

**Interviewer:** How did you test whether it was trustworthy?

**P02:** For some days I watched it. I knew around what time I normally take the medicine, and then I saw when it reminded me. If it was correct again and again, then I became more comfortable.

**Interviewer:** Did your family also help you check it?

**P02:** Yes, they helped with the phone part. Younger people understand these things better. If there was something I did not understand, I asked them.

**Interviewer:** Over the past few weeks, do you feel relying on the agent has replaced your own memory, or has it retrained your internal sense of when the dose is due?

**P02:** I don't think it replaced my memory. I still know the usual time. But now I also expect the reminder. Sometimes I know it is nearly time before the phone says anything. So maybe it has made the habit stronger.

**Interviewer:** If the agent stopped working tomorrow, would you still know when to take your medicine?

**P02:** Yes. I managed before. At home, because I have only one medicine and it is kept in the same place, I know the routine. The agent just makes it easier when something changes.

### Section 6: Closing and the Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** If you had to summarize, who do you feel this AI agent ultimately works for, you, your family, or both?

**P02:** Both, I think. Mainly it is helping me remember. But my family is also involved because if I forget, they can help. So both.

**Interviewer:** Under what conditions is it acceptable for the agent to stop acting as your private tool and start acting as your family's advocate?

**P02:** If I miss the medicine and I don't respond, then it can tell my family. Especially if I am not answering after another reminder. But if I am only a little late, then give me some time first.

**Interviewer:** So you would want the agent to give you a chance to manage it yourself before involving the family?

**P02:** Yes, yes. First tell me. Then wait. If I don't do anything, then tell them. That is better.

**Interviewer:** What would make that balance feel right for you?

**P02:** It should not be too much. One reminder, then another if I don't respond, then family if needed. I can still do it myself, but family is there if there is a problem.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about the agent that you would change?

**P02:** Keep it simple. I don't need many things because I only take one regular medicine. Just tell me the time clearly. If I am outside, maybe a small vibration is better than speaking loudly.

**Interviewer:** Would you want it to speak in Bangla?

**P02:** Yes, Bangla would be easier. Simple Bangla. No complicated words.

**Interviewer:** And would you want your family to be able to see your medicine information?

**P02:** My family, yes, that is okay. They already know. But other people don't need to know.

**Interviewer:** What would make you stop trusting or using the agent?

**P02:** If it gives the wrong medicine information, then I would stop trusting it. Or if it keeps disturbing me too much. It should remind me, not make things difficult.

**Interviewer:** Any final thoughts?

**P02:** Hmm, no. I think it is useful. I don't need too much. Just remind me properly, and if I don't respond, let my family know. That is enough.

**Interviewer:** Thank you very much.

**P02:** You're welcome.

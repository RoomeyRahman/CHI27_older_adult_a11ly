# Phase 2 Follow-Up Interview Transcript: Medication Management

**Participant:** Borhan Uddin Rana  
**Age:** 25  
**Family context:** Grandmother lives with the family; mother has diabetes

---

## 1. Affiliation Ledger & Negotiating Allegiance

**Interviewer:** "After using the medication support system, how did you feel about who the system was helping?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Mainly it was helping my grandmother and my mother, but it was also helping me because I am the one who manages their medicines. I have to make sure they take them regularly."

**Interviewer:** "Did it feel clear what the system was doing, or did it feel like it was getting too involved?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "No, it was clear. If it is just reminding them about the medicine, then that is useful. I don't think it needs to get involved in other family matters."

**Interviewer:** "Did you ever move from using it yourself to sharing its information with your family?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes, if there was a missed dose, then I would want my family to know, especially because sometimes I am not at home. Normally I am the one supervising, but I cannot always be there."

**Interviewer:** "Would you prefer the system to work independently or together with you?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Together. It can give the reminder by itself, but I should still be able to check what is happening. The system should support me, not replace me."

---

## 2. Veto Power, Risk, and Silence

**Interviewer:** "If the system told your family to take a medicine that did not match the prescription, what would you do?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "I would check the prescription and the medicine strip first. If it is not matching, I would not give it just because the system said it."

**Interviewer:** "What if the system gave a stronger warning because a dose seemed to have been missed?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "That would be helpful. If I am not there or I am busy, I may not know that they missed it. So if the system tells me, then I can check."

**Interviewer:** "What should happen if nobody responds to the first reminder?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "It should remind again after some time. Sometimes they may be doing something or I may be asleep. If there is still no response, then it should tell me."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want it to keep reminding continuously?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "No no, that would be irritating. One reminder and then another after some time is enough. If it is still not taken, then I should know."

**Interviewer:** "How would you tell the difference between a normal reminder and a real problem?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Normal is just the alarm at the medicine time. If the dose is still not taken after reminders, then that becomes a problem. Especially if I am not home, I need to know."

---

## 3. Care Collectivism & "Checking as Love"

**Interviewer:** "Has the system changed how your family checks on your grandmother and mother's medicines?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes, a little. Before I had to remind them myself. Now the system can remind them first. So it reduces some of the work for me."

**Interviewer:** "Do you miss having to remind them manually?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "No, not really. I still check on them. The system just helps when I am busy or not at home."

**Interviewer:** "If there was a new prescription or sudden medicine change, would you let the system handle it?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "No. I would check with the doctor or pharmacist. The prescriptions are sometimes difficult to understand because of handwriting. So I would want a person to confirm it."

**Interviewer:** "Would the family discuss an important medicine change together?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes. If it is a new medicine or something changes, I would check it properly before giving it. The system can show the information, but I would still confirm it."

**Interviewer:** "Has the reminder changed the way your family cares for them?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "It makes the checking easier. It does not replace the care. We still need to bring the medicine and make sure they are taking it."

---

## 4. Gamification as a Relational Trigger

**Interviewer:** "If the system showed a high score or a long streak for taking medicines properly, how would your family react?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "They might feel good that the medicines are being taken properly. But I don't think the score itself is very important."

**Interviewer:** "Would praise or encouragement be useful?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes, maybe. If it says the medicines are being taken regularly, that is good. But it should be simple."

**Interviewer:** "What if a streak was broken because someone missed a dose?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "I would be more concerned about the missed medicine than the streak. We should find out why it was missed and make sure it does not happen again."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want the progress to be shared with your family?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Basic progress, yes. If I am managing their medicines, it can be useful for me to know. But there is no need for too many details."

---

## 5. Probationary Trust & Retraining

**Interviewer:** "When you first started using the system, did you feel you had to test it before trusting it?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes. I would check whether the reminder matches the medicine strip and prescription. Especially because they take several medicines, I need to know it is giving the right information."

**Interviewer:** "What would make you trust it more?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "If it keeps giving the correct medicine name and correct time, then I can depend on it more. In the beginning I would check everything myself."

**Interviewer:** "Did the system replace your memory of the schedule?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "It reduced the amount I have to remember. I still know the routine, but I don't have to keep thinking about every medicine time when I am busy."

**Interviewer:** "Would you still keep the original medicine instructions and prescription?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes, definitely. Those are still important. Especially if there is a new medicine or a change."

**Interviewer:** "What about the problem with doctor handwriting?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "That would still need some help. If the system could clearly identify the medicine from the prescription, that would be useful. But I would want it to be correct."

---

## 6. Closing & Plural Principal

**Interviewer:** "After using the system, who do you think it ultimately works for—you, your grandmother, your mother, or the whole family?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "The whole family, I think. It helps them take their medicines and it helps me manage things. If I am not there, the reminder can still help them."

**Interviewer:** "Would you be comfortable with the system becoming a family advocate when there is a problem?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes, if there is a real problem. If they miss a dose and do not respond to the reminder, then I should get an alert. That would be useful."

**Interviewer:** "Would you want the system to involve the family for every normal reminder?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "No no. That would be too much. They can just hear the alarm and take the medicine. I only need to know if there is a problem."

**Interviewer:** "When would it be acceptable for the system to become more involved?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "If there are repeated missed doses, if the medicine information is unclear, or if there is some change in the prescription. Then I would want to know and check it."

**Interviewer:** "What kind of reminder would work best for your family?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "An alarm with the medicine name would be very helpful. If it just rings, they may not know which medicine it is. If it says the name, then it is much clearer."

**Interviewer:** "Would you prefer an alarm, phone call, or text?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Alarm. That would be easiest for them because they use button phones. They are not very interested in smartphones."

**Interviewer:** "Would generic medicine names be useful?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes, definitely. Brand names can be different, so sometimes it becomes confusing. If the system uses the generic name, it would be more consistent."

**Interviewer:** "Overall, has the system made medication management easier for you?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes. Especially when I am busy, working, or sleeping. I cannot supervise them all the time. If the system reminds them and tells me when something is missed, then it reduces the chance of missing medicine."

**Interviewer:** "Would you still want to confirm important medicine information with a pharmacist or doctor?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Yes yes. Especially if there is a new medicine or something I am not sure about. I would check with the pharmacist or doctor."

**Interviewer:** "Anything else you would like to add?"

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Just keep it simple. Alarm, medicine name, correct timing. And if they miss it, tell me. Also generic names would be better. That is what I think would be useful."

**Interviewer:** "Thank you for your time."

**Borhan Uddin Rana:** "Thank you."

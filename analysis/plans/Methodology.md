## Directive Intake

**Directive:** Design the Methodology section and subsection hierarchy for the completed two-phase qualitative process: Phase 1 interviews; prototype development from Phase 1 findings; participant prototype review; Phase 2 interviews for deeper needs; use the two named interview-question files; reflexive thematic analysis; machine-assisted Bangla transcription and English translation, each followed by full verification by two fluent Bangla-speaking researchers. Produce a solid CHI-standard plan with section, subsection, and paragraph titles.

**Typed operations:**

- Scope: Method, covering both interview phases, prototype derivation and review, corpus preparation, and reflexive thematic analysis.
- Emphasis: reproducibility, chronological study logic, researcher reflexivity, and machine assistance as a drafting aid rather than an analytical authority.
- Theory: reflexive thematic analysis must carry the analytic account; prototype theory and cross-language qualitative research must delimit the claims.
- Evidence: `supplementary/Interviews/phase-1/interview_questions.md`, `supplementary/Interviews/phase-2/interview_questions.md`, filed participant records, prototype records, ethics records, and analysis artifacts where available.

- Process: the standard eight-phase protocol, with no phase skipped.
- Output: this file. No paper prose goes into `/output/`.

**Changes to default phases:** Phase 2 and Phase 5 represent the user-specified two-phase sequence and provide paragraph titles. Phase 4 prioritizes reflexive thematic analysis, multilingual transcription and translation, machine-assisted corpus preparation, and prototype-mediated elicitation.

## Section Purpose

The section should let a reader reconstruct how the researchers moved from situated accounts of medication work, through an evidence-linked prototype, to a prototype-mediated follow-up inquiry, and how they produced interpretations from Bangla data. It should establish the warrant for C1 and the formative part of C3. It cannot yet establish the paper's RQ2 allegiance contribution because no household deployment records are filed.

Use the title **4 Method**, not **Methodology** or **Experimental Setup**. The study is a sequential qualitative inquiry with a prototype-mediated second phase. It is not a controlled experiment, so hypotheses, null-hypothesis testing, independent variables, and within-subjects or between-subjects terminology would misdescribe it.

Target length when drafted: approximately 2,000 to 2,500 words, excluding the participant table and any study-flow figure. Method prose should be flat, chronological, and exact. Use first person for research actions. Use no more than two honest contrast frames in the section.

## Recommended Section Architecture

### 4.1 Study Design and Research Orientation

**Purpose:** Name the design, explain the sequence, connect each phase to the canonical RQs, and delimit what the design can answer.

1. **Paragraph M1, "A Sequential Qualitative and Prototype-Mediated Inquiry."** State the research design in one sentence, then give the chronological arc: Phase 1 interviews and situated artifact walkthroughs, analysis-informed prototype development, prototype exposure, and Phase 2 follow-up interviews. Do not call this an experiment.
2. **Paragraph M2, "Why the Phases Build on One Another."** Explain what each phase adds. Phase 1 examines existing care-network practices for RQ1. The prototype makes selected design propositions concrete. Phase 2 elicits reactions to those propositions and can inform RQ3. RQ2 requires evidence of an agent acting within a care network and remains outside the filed two-phase corpus.
3. **Paragraph M3, "Care Networks as the Unit of Inquiry."** Name the asset-based stance once and explain that sampling, interviewing, and analysis attend to distributed medication work rather than treating memory as an individual deficit. Cite the practice and interdependence literature already filed.
4. **Paragraph M4, "Research Setting and Language."** Report the sites in Bangladesh, whether sessions occurred in homes or elsewhere, the interview language, and any relevant urban or rural context. Keep claims bounded to Bangladesh.

### 4.2 Participants and Recruitment

**Purpose:** Give one auditable account of who took part in each phase and how the samples relate.

5. **Paragraph M5, "Sampling Logic and Eligibility."** State the sampling strategy, inclusion and exclusion criteria, the definitions used for older adult and caregiver, and why those roles were needed for the RQs.
6. **Paragraph M6, "Recruitment and Consent Contact."** Identify who approached participants, through which organizations or personal networks, how prior relationships were handled, and when consent was sought.
7. **Paragraph M7, "Sample Composition Across Phases."** Refer to a compact participant table. Report exact N, age range, gender, role, medication-management context, and phase participation once. State explicitly whether Phase 2 involved returning Phase 1 participants, new participants, or both.
8. **Paragraph M8, "Compensation and Participation Burden."** Report compensation, travel reimbursement, scheduling, and any accessibility accommodation. If there was no compensation, say so plainly.

**Required participant table:** one row per anonymized participant or one row per participant category, depending final page budget. Columns should include ID, role in the care network, age, gender, phase participation, relevant medication-management responsibility, and session format. Do not include diagnoses unless consent and analytic relevance justify them.

### 4.3 Phase 1: Formative Interviews and Situated Artifact Walkthroughs

**Purpose:** Show how Phase 1 generated an account of existing medication work and the evidence base for the prototype.

9. **Paragraph M9, "Phase 1 Aim and Interview Domains."** Describe the instrument's substantive domains: medication routines, disruptions, artifacts, technology use, accessibility, care-network coordination, and design reflection. Do not reproduce the guide's deficit-oriented wording or retired RQs.
10. **Paragraph M10, "Situated Interview Materials."** Explain the use of semi-structured questions, in-situ observation, medication artifacts, and participant demonstrations. Distinguish what researchers actually observed from what the guide invited them to observe.
11. **Paragraph M11, "Phase 1 Session Procedure."** Give the participant journey in order: consent, context observation, interview, artifact walkthrough, closing reflection, and debrief. Report facilitator count, language, location, recording, notes, and duration.
12. **Paragraph M12, "What Phase 1 Could Establish."** Close by delimiting Phase 1 as evidence about current practices and expressed design concerns. It does not establish that a prototype feature worked or that an autonomous agent's allegiance was negotiated.

### 4.4 From Phase 1 Findings to the Prototype

**Purpose:** Make the design derivation inspectable and prevent the prototype from appearing as an unexplained artifact.

13. **Paragraph M13, "Analytic Findings Became Design Requirements."** Describe the decision process used to convert Phase 1 interpretations into requirements. Refer to a design-traceability table rather than summarizing findings in Method.
14. **Paragraph M14, "Selecting and Rejecting Design Propositions."** State who made design decisions, how competing interpretations were handled, which propositions were deferred or rejected, and how the care-network framing constrained the design.
15. **Paragraph M15, "Prototype Scope, Fidelity, and Tested Qualities."** Describe only the prototype qualities participants could encounter. Report its form, language, interaction flow, data shown, and functional boundaries. Refer detailed implementation to the System section. Do not list programming languages or computer specifications.
16. **Paragraph M16, "Prototype Review Materials."** Identify screenshots, scenarios, scripted prompts, devices, and any facilitator demonstration. State which qualities the prototype filtered for review and which parts were nonfunctional, simulated, or omitted.

**Required design-traceability table:** `Phase 1 evidence | analytic interpretation | design requirement | prototype element | Phase 2 probe | status`. Every row needs a Phase 1 transcript or analysis-artifact source. A user request does not become a finding merely because the prototype implemented it.

### 4.5 Phase 2: Prototype-Mediated Follow-up Interviews

**Purpose:** Explain how the prototype was used to deepen inquiry without overstating prototype exposure as deployment evidence.

17. **Paragraph M17, "Phase 2 Aim and Epistemic Status."** State whether Phase 2 was a demonstration interview, hands-on review, usability session, or post-deployment interview. Use exactly one label consistently. If participants only saw the prototype, describe their responses as elicited judgments or expectations, not lived use.
18. **Paragraph M18, "Prototype Exposure and Session Sequence."** Report what participants saw or did, task and scenario order, facilitator actions, think-aloud instructions if any, breaks, total duration, and when the interview began.
19. **Paragraph M19, "Follow-up Interview Domains."** Describe the actual guide's domains: allegiance declarations, role change, veto and silence, family checking, scores and streaks, trust verification, and role legitimacy. State whether all participants received the same core questions and how probes varied.
20. **Paragraph M20, "Instrument Evolution and Prompting Risk."** Explain how Phase 1 and prototype observations shaped Phase 2 questions. Disclose that several questions present an interpretation before requesting a response, then distinguish spontaneous comments from responses to theory-laden probes in analysis.
21. **Paragraph M21, "What Phase 2 Could Establish."** Delimit claims to participants' reactions and accounts under the actual exposure condition. Prototype review cannot establish long-term habituation, health outcomes, household negotiation, or the behavior of an unimplemented capability.

### 4.6 Corpus Preparation: Transcription and Translation

**Purpose:** Make the Bangla-to-English evidence chain auditable and clarify the limited role of automated tools.

22. **Paragraph M22, "The Source Corpus Remained Bangla."** State which recordings were in Bangla, how code-switching was handled, what constituted the canonical transcript, and which language researchers used during coding and theme development.
23. **Paragraph M23, "Machine-Assisted Bangla Transcription with Full Human Verification."** Identify the automatic speech recognition service and version, deployment mode, privacy setting, and output format. Then state that two fluent Bangla-speaking researchers replayed each complete recording and corrected errors and omissions. Report how disagreements and inaudible segments were handled.
24. **Paragraph M24, "Machine-Assisted English Translation with Bilingual Review."** State that a machine-generated draft was made from the verified Bangla transcript, after which two researchers compared the complete English text against the Bangla source and corrected differences in meaning. Explain how idioms, kinship terms, honorifics, and culturally specific expressions were recorded.
25. **Paragraph M25, "Automation as Drafting Aid, Not Analytical Authority."** Land the distinction plainly. Automated systems produced drafts only. Researchers retained the audio, verified the Bangla transcript, reviewed the English rendering, and made all analytic decisions.

**Required language-process artifact:** preserve a private decision log for consequential translation choices and a public, anonymized methods summary. The paper need not expose participant text beyond consented quotations.

### 4.7 Reflexive Thematic Analysis

**Purpose:** Report a coherent reflexive thematic analysis rather than combining interpretive analysis with coding-reliability language.

26. **Paragraph M26, "Analytic Orientation and Epistemological Position."** Name Braun and Clarke's reflexive thematic analysis, its experiential or critical orientation, semantic or latent emphasis, inductive-first and RQ-aligned-second logic, and the researchers' view of themes as constructed interpretations rather than entities that emerged from the data.
27. **Paragraph M27, "Familiarization Through Individual Engagement."** Describe repeated reading, annotation, memoing, and each researcher's independent initial engagement with the interviews. Clarify that multiple readings enlarged interpretation rather than serving as duplicate coding for agreement.
28. **Paragraph M28, "Iterative Coding and Collective Interpretation."** Explain how researchers generated and revised codes, compared interpretations, challenged assumptions, and documented changes. State explicitly that meetings were not used to calculate inter-rater reliability or force consensus.
29. **Paragraph M29, "Developing, Reviewing, and Naming Themes."** Report how candidate themes were built from patterned meaning across data items, checked against transcripts, revised, bounded, and named. Identify who participated at each stage and how Bangla source text was revisited.
30. **Paragraph M30, "Analysis Across the Two Phases."** State whether Phase 1 was analyzed before prototype construction, whether Phase 2 was analyzed separately, and how cross-phase interpretation was subsequently conducted. Keep Phase 1 findings, design decisions, and Phase 2 responses traceable rather than collapsing them into one corpus.
31. **Paragraph M31, "RQ Alignment, Negative Cases, and Analytic Boundaries."** Explain the second, RQ-aligned pass and the treatment of disconfirming cases. Preserve the relevant tensions: memory restored versus replaced, trust through self-verification, checking as intimacy or intrusion, and streak encouragement or grief. Delegated dependence cannot become a finding without Study 3 evidence.
32. **Paragraph M32, "Analytic Records and Reporting Quality."** Identify software if it materially shaped coding, the memo and codebook artifacts retained, the theme map, and how the team maintained an audit trail. Do not claim saturation unless the sampling rationale and analytic approach support that term. Reflexive thematic analysis normally does not use saturation as a universal stopping rule.

### 4.8 Ethics, Consent, Data Governance, and Positionality

**Purpose:** Report the conditions under which the data were produced and interpreted.

33. **Paragraph M33, "Ethics Review and Informed Consent."** Give approving body, approval identifier, consent mode, recording consent, withdrawal process, and handling of family members present during home sessions.
34. **Paragraph M34, "Consent for Automated Processing."** State whether participants were told that automated transcription and translation tools would process recordings or transcripts, where processing occurred, whether vendors retained data, and what ethics approval covered.
35. **Paragraph M35, "Confidentiality and Anonymization."** Describe the public participant IDs, removal of direct identifiers, storage and access controls, retention schedule, and treatment of rare contextual details that could enable re-identification.
36. **Paragraph M36, "Researcher Positionality in Practice."** Give the researchers' relationships to Bangladesh, Bangla, older adulthood, caregiving, medicine, and technology only where each changed recruitment, interviewing, translation, or interpretation. Avoid a demographic inventory detached from analytic consequences.
37. **Paragraph M37, "Methodological Scope."** State the boundaries in flat prose: one setting, interview and prototype-review evidence rather than health outcomes, the exact exposure duration, and the inability of the filed two-phase data to support household allegiance claims reserved for Study 3.

## Theory Alignment

### Primary Framework 1: Reflexive Thematic Analysis

**Status:** `[PROPOSED, not yet in ledger as this more specific formulation]`

Key constructs are researcher subjectivity as an analytic resource, recursive familiarization and coding, themes as constructed patterns of shared meaning, and methodological coherence. It carries M26 through M32. It explains why independent engagement and collective discussion deepen interpretation without functioning as reliability testing.

**Rival considered:** coding-reliability thematic analysis. It loses because the described team process did not seek stable code application, agreement scores, or adjudicated consensus. Importing those criteria after the fact would contradict the work performed.

### Primary Framework 2: Prototype as Filter and Elicitation Object

**Status:** `[PROPOSED, not yet in ledger]`

Key constructs are prototype filtering, manifestation, scope, and experienced qualities. It carries M15 through M21. It forces the draft to state what the prototype exposed and prevents judgments about a demonstration from becoming claims about a deployed system.

**Rival considered:** usability evaluation. It loses unless the study records contain defined tasks, observable usability measures, and a protocol designed to evaluate interaction performance. The filed Phase 2 guide seeks accounts of meaning and legitimacy rather than performance metrics.

### Primary Framework 3: Translation as Interpretive Work

**Status:** `[PROPOSED, not yet in ledger]`

Key constructs are the visibility of translation, the source-language meaning chain, translator or researcher positionality, and documented decisions about non-equivalent terms. It carries M22 through M25 and M29. It prevents machine-generated English from being treated as neutral data.

**Rival considered:** back-translation as a universal validity check. It loses because semantic equivalence alone cannot preserve contextual and relational meaning. Side-by-side bilingual review and a decision trail better match the stated workflow, provided the researchers file that process.

### R1, Domain Expert Review

**Pre-revision verdict:** The first outline would not have survived. Its chronological structure was sound, but it risked treating the samples as individuals reviewing a reminder tool rather than members of care networks, and it did not confront the leading framing inside the Phase 2 guide.

Ranked defects and fixes:

1. **Phase 2 guide presupposes its findings.** Sections 1, 3, 4, and 5 name loyalty, checking as affection, relational scores, probation, and retrained memory before participants answer. **Fix:** M20 now requires disclosure of prompting and separate treatment of spontaneous and probe-elicited material.
2. **Phase 1 guide retains deficit-oriented language and retired topics.** **Fix:** M3 and M9 require the draft to report neutral interview domains while holding the care network and assets as the analytic unit. The Method will not rewrite what was asked, but it will not reproduce the superseded framing as its rationale.
3. **Care-network scope could slide into individual technology acceptance.** **Fix:** M2, M3, M5, M7, and M12 require role-aware sampling and exact boundaries on what each phase supports.
4. **Checking could be assumed benign.** **Fix:** M19 and M31 require the intimacy and intrusion readings, plus negative cases.
5. **Bangladesh could be used as a regional proxy.** **Fix:** M4 and M37 bind empirical claims to the actual sites in Bangladesh.

**False alarm rejected:** A request to remove all accessibility questions would be wrong. The retired accessibility RQ cannot organize findings, but exact interaction conditions remain legitimate context for prototype design and procedure.

### R2, Methods and Evidence Review

**Pre-revision verdict:** The outline would have failed on evidence transparency.

Ranked defects and fixes:

1. **Participant count contradiction.** The canonical framing says 26 formative participants, including 17 older adults and 9 caregivers, but only P1 through P9 are filed. **Fix:** M7 and the evidence audit block drafting until the recruitment register and complete corpus reconcile the count.
2. **Phase 2 completion is unsupported.** Only an interview guide is filed. **Fix:** M17, M18, M21, and the evidence audit require transcripts, participant records, duration, and exposure evidence.
3. **Presentation versus deployment is unresolved.** The directive says the prototype was presented, while the guide says post-deployment and asks about events over weeks. **Fix:** M17 makes study-type resolution a blocking decision and M21 limits claims to the verified exposure.
4. **Prototype capabilities are unsupported.** No `/system/` records exist. **Fix:** M15 and M16 prohibit feature and autonomy claims until the prototype specification is filed.
5. **The language workflow is supplied but unaudited.** **Fix:** M22 to M25 require tool, privacy, correction, disagreement, and translation-decision records before drafting.
6. **RTA is described but has no audit trail.** **Fix:** M27 to M32 require memos, code revisions, theme maps, phase timeline, and a negative-case register.
7. **Ethics and automated-processing consent are absent.** **Fix:** M33 and M34 create explicit missing-data gates.

**False alarm rejected:** The absence of inter-rater reliability is not a flaw under reflexive thematic analysis. M28 now states why agreement was not the purpose of collective interpretation.

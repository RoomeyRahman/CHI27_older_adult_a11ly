One sentence: establish that four independent literatures have each brought the field to the edge of the same unstudied case, a family deciding whom an agent works for, and show that this study is positioned exactly there.

**Title in the paper:** *Related Work*.

**Heading convention, revisions 2 and 3.** Each subsection carries a short noun-phrase title of four to six words, in the register CHI proceedings use, and each title names what its subsection covers rather than asserting the subsection's claim. The argument the heading used to carry moves into an explicit **headline claim** recorded under each title, which `/draft` renders as the subsection's opening sentence. Nothing is lost: the claim still opens the paragraph, per `Training/writing-style.md`, and the heading stops competing with it. The four titles are *Medication Work in Family Care Networks*, *Caregiver-Facing Systems and Their Limits*, *Autonomous Assistants and Whom They Serve*, and *Contestable Design and the Cost of Visibility*. Three follow one shape, a body of systems paired with the analytic issue it raises, which is the shape CHI subsections most often take; the first pairs a practice with its setting. Two carry anchor terms fixed by CLAUDE.md Section 2.5, *care network* and *caregiver*, and the remaining anchor terms *allegiance* and *dignity* sit in the headline claims and the beats. Section 2.5 fixes terminology for prose, not for headings, so a heading may name a literature by the term that literature uses for itself, as *contestable design* does for [6].

**Target length:** 1,800 to 2,100 words, sixteen beats. Related Work leans hardest of any section on the gap structure and on citation integration. Citation brackets at 11 to 14 per thousand words means roughly 20 to 28 brackets, most carrying two to four references. If a sentence argues the paper's stakes, it belongs in the Introduction. If it characterises what a body of work found, it belongs here. The Introduction cites the nearest three or four works; this section cites the corpus.

Where a beat carries both, Phase 5 records them in separate columns. A gap says what the literature has not done; a tension says where it argues with itself. ### RW1. Medication Work in Family Care Networks

**Headline claim, rendered as the opening sentence:** medication work in these households is distributed across family members before any system arrives, and the technology built for it is still addressed to one person.

**Purpose.** Establish the unit-of-analysis mismatch before any agent enters the argument. Two literatures converge here, chronic-illness self-management and intermediated technology use, and they converge on a finding neither designs for.

**Which of CLAUDE.md Section 2.6's non-negotiables it carries:** Bangladesh and Global South HCI on proxy use, intermediated use, and family mediation, in its practice half.

1. **L1, reminder design and the routine problem.** Apps optimise for alerting and neglect routine integration [107]; existing medication apps were largely unusable for older adults on their own terms [35]; for people with several conditions the hard work is reconciling conflicting advice, not remembering [27]. **Tension:** [108] finds that reminders supported repetition while undermining the cue learning that makes a behaviour automatic, so the same stream that treats better reminder design as the remedy [107, 35] also reports reminding as the impediment. Gap: the stream's design guidance presumes a system that informs one person.
2. **L2, chronic-illness work as family work.** Chronic-disease management is organised as interrelated collective family routines with distinct roles for family, patient, and artifacts [92]; self-management is jointly produced with parents long before formal transition [120]; a nominally personal health behaviour is entangled with other members' routines [102]; self-care is collaborative in its ordinary operation [cand-19]. **Tension:** the same stream that diagnoses the work as collective prescribes interfaces addressed to the individual, as when a conversational check-in is designed to compensate for one person's changing ability and never to decide whom else to tell [74]. Gap: the family is the documented unit of the work and is still not the unit of the system.
3. **L3, collective use as arrangement.** Intermediated use names surrogate, proximate enabling, and proximate translation as the shapes technology reach actually takes [98]; users voluntarily relinquish custody of an information resource to sustain intermediation [33]; collaborative interface design fits low-literate use better than an individual model [1]; healthcare access in rural Bangladesh runs through the same relational channels [cand-15]; health tracking in South Asia is a sociocultural rather than individual practice [cand-16]. **Tension:** the closeness that makes intermediation work is the same closeness that produces the privacy strain [3] a tiered shared-account model was designed to absorb [4], so the relation is resource and risk at once. Gap: in all of it the object is a mediated tool, and a tool has no side to take. **Framing guard:** this paragraph states an asset, never a deficit. Proxy operation is the household's competence, not its limitation.
4. **L4, a structural rather than local mismatch.** Bangladesh is ageing rapidly with almost no residential or formal home-care infrastructure, so care falls to families by default [5]; caregiver burden and structural difficulty adopting health technology recur across the subcontinent [90]; formal services misfit the family-based arrangements Bangladeshi carers actually operate [77]; care interventions that address the individual leave the structural conditions of care untouched [53]. **Tension:** the reviews that document reciprocal filial obligation as the organising structure also report it under strain from changing family structure and out-migration [51], so the care network is the asset this work builds on and the arrangement that is eroding. Gap and close: the mismatch between individualist adherence tools and this arrangement is structural rather than a matter of localisation, which is why translating an interface does not fix it.

### RW2. Caregiver-Facing Systems and Their Limits

**Headline claim:** the systems HCI has built for family caregiving make care legible to the people around the older adult, and each of them leaves the decision about what to disclose, and to whom, outside the system.

**Purpose.** Give the caregiver framework a full account, generous first, and then state its limitations precisely. This is the subsection the directive names as the caregiver-framework subsection, and it carries the paper's load-bearing contrast.

**Which non-negotiable it carries:** CSCW caregiver dashboards and shared health monitoring as passive systems without initiative.

5. **L5, the caregiver framework and what it built.** The founding awareness system gave extended family peace of mind through a qualitative visualisation of a parent's day [82], and its year-long field trial showed sustained value [94]; the term *care network* enters HCI as a design target that is the network rather than the elder [22]; caregiving is organised by filial obligation and constant role-switching, much of it invisible [47]; caregiver support has been designed through community health equity rather than individual burden [36]; the current state of the art carries context between staff and family with collaborative AI tools [81]. **Tension:** the same coordination logic that promises awareness also reports that growing a care network makes staying informed harder rather than easier [118], so the framework's central benefit scales against itself. Gap: every one of these renders a state, and a person decides what to do about it.
6. **L6, visibility reorganises the family.** Privacy in a care setting is cooperatively negotiated rather than individually held [23], and is performed as articulation work rather than set as a preference [87]. Making work visible is itself an intervention with costs [106], a warning telemonitoring research has confirmed [85, 8]. **Tension:** [115] reports, within one study, that sharing care-recipient information improved coordination *and* fuelled conflict and privacy breach for the recipient, and [111] reports the same data read as support by one stakeholder and as surveillance by another. The literature does not disagree about visibility because its studies disagree; single studies carry both readings. Gap: the field knows disclosure changes relationships and still treats the disclosure decision as outside the system.
7. **L7, one side of each episode.** Onboarding language positioned care recipients as collaborators while the system disempowered them [24]; recipients performed relation work the system was not designed to receive [65]; shared decision-making about safeguards was largely illusory, with safeguards installed rather than agreed [76]. Older adults respond actively to passive monitoring, adapting, resisting, and discontinuing it, with priorities that differ from those of staff and family [cand-12], a pattern a recent scoping review finds field-wide [cand-14]. **Tension:** caregiver-centred studies report coordination gains and recipient-centred studies report lost standing, and the split tracks who was interviewed rather than which system was studied; [cand-13] closes that loop by measuring the disagreement directly, showing older adults and their adult children diverge in patterned ways about in-home sensors and tracking. Gap: paired accounts of one episode are rare. **This is the published warrant for the two-sided design, so the Method points back to it.**
8. **L8, the limit of the caregiver framework.** Family presence in routine medical visits is common and changes the consultation measurably [cand-11]; the triadic literature maps companion roles, attitudes toward involvement, and patterns of triadic decision-making across dozens of studies [cand-8], extends the mapping to partners [cand-9], and has instruments for measuring involvement in older patients with several conditions [cand-10]; the TRIO framework maps caregiver involvement and influence across the stages of a treatment decision [63]. **Tension:** clinical research treats a companion's involvement in a health decision as ordinary and beneficial, while HCI's monitoring literature reads the same involvement as an encroachment on the older adult's independence [24, 76]; two fields hold opposite defaults about one act. Gap and close: neither field has a system that holds the disclosure decision itself, and neither settles what the arrangement costs the older adult's standing. **Bracket, first half:** this closing sentence names the standing question that L13 reopens. Carries the load-bearing frame, **dashboards report, whereas an agent must decide whom to tell and when**, and the checking-as-care frame stated as an open question the data answers case by case.

### RW3. Autonomous Assistants and Whom They Serve

**Headline claim:** the assistants now fielded in homes and clinics act rather than report, and every one of them, from the global platforms down to the systems deployed in the Global South, answers to whoever configured it.

**Purpose.** Move the argument from systems that inform to systems that act, using the tools that exist rather than the formalism alone, and run the funnel the directive asks for: global first, then narrowed to deployments in the Global South. Close on the convergence of all four literatures.

**Which non-negotiable it carries:** AI alignment and agentic AI under the single-principal assumption, and the deployment half of Global South HCI.

9. **L9, the assistants that exist, and the principal they are configured for.** Deployed health assistants are real and are addressed to one person: an LLM voice assistant mediates between providers and older adults [117]; a conversational check-in supports medication management for older adults with mild cognitive impairment [74]; voice assistants opened computing to older adults with low prior technology use over a three-week field deployment [89]. The formalism underneath is explicit about the choice: cooperative inverse reinforcement learning frames alignment as a shared game with one human [38]; aligning to instructions, intentions, revealed preferences, interests, or values are materially different targets [32]; the limit is named from inside the field, as misalignment along interacting axes once the principal is plural [62], as the inadequacy of preferences for representing values across plural stakeholders [121], and as a design space with distinct pluralistic targets [105] approached technically as model composition [29] and formally as preference aggregation [cand-7]. **Tension:** the formal literature concludes that a *less* obedient agent serves an imperfectly rational human better [79] and that revocability must be engineered against the agent's own incentives [39], while the contestability and oversight literature concludes that human intervention must be preserved at every step [6, 25]; both are defensible and they pull a veto design in opposite directions. Gap: aggregation treats a plural principal as a distribution to be summed, and a household is a relation to be negotiated.
10. **L10, proactivity outran its account.** Initiative has been a design variable since mixed-initiative interfaces coupled automation with direct manipulation under the agent's own confidence [45]; generative proactivity now sustains engagement where reactive agents do not [70]; exposing a plan before executing it shifts both trust and team performance [42]; oversight of such agents is improvised heuristic work rather than governance procedure [25]. **Tension:** [52] finds that agents acting unprompted must explain more, because people want to know why it acted at all, while [70] finds that proactivity is valued precisely for not asking; more disclosure and less interruption are both the reported remedy. Gap: none of this has been studied where the unprompted act is telling one family member about another.
11. **L11, households are not single-user, and the answer so far has been access control.** Multi-user smart homes produce conflicting demands, tenuous power dynamics, and interfaces built for a single account holder [cand-1]; the technical response has been multi-user access control with automated policy negotiation [cand-2], now extended to delegation and authorisation for agents themselves [cand-22]; voice interfaces in the home are used as multi-party conversational resources rather than one-to-one assistants [cand-4], and family use produces breakdowns that are social as much as technical [cand-5]. **Tension:** the security literature's remedy is finer-grained per-user control, while the interaction literature finds household devices are used collectively and not individually addressable at all; personalising the account and matching the practice pull against each other. Gap: these systems arbitrate permissions between users, and none holds a loyalty a household can see, move, or take back.
12. **L12, narrowing to the Global South, where the operator is often not the beneficiary.** Systems that decide rather than inform have been fielded in the region and the record is instructive: rural clinicians adopted an AI clinical decision-support system and then worked around it, so adoption never became use [114]. The conditions are documented: explainability work in the Global South is systematically reviewed and found thin [84]; Global South stakeholders occupy critical but under-recognised roles in AI governance [88]; community-based innovations fail to sustain for structural rather than design reasons [96]; and who may operate a device is shaped by household power rather than by preference [110]. Design frameworks travel badly without historical and structural analysis [49, 26]. **Tension:** intermediated use establishes that the person operating the device is routinely not the person the system is about [98, 33], and the alignment literature has no vocabulary for a principal who is not the operator, while the ICTD literature that does have the vocabulary has studied no system with initiative. Gap: the two bodies of work each hold half of the problem and neither has the case.
13. **L13, the convergence.** Short beat, four to five sentences, the argumentative hinge of the section. Each literature stops at the same place: chronic-illness work is collective but its systems address one person; caregiver frameworks make care legible and leave the disclosure decision to a person; assistants act and answer to whoever configured them; multi-user computing arbitrates access rather than allegiance. **The one sanctioned formulation goes here and nowhere else:** to our knowledge, no empirical account exists of a household assigning, contesting, and revoking an autonomous agent's allegiance in daily life. Close on what the study did, in the vocabulary CLAUDE.md Section 3.3 licenses: we deployed an agent that announced in Bangla whom it was currently serving and asked the older adult before involving a family member, for two weeks in Bangladeshi households where an older adult and a family caregiver each accounted for the same period. **`/draft` may not add a capability to that sentence that is absent from the deployed list, and may not imply that logs record any of it.**

### RW4. Contestable Design and the Cost of Visibility

**Headline claim:** the standing question caregiver frameworks leave open is a design problem with a documented cost, because every instrument that would make an agent's allegiance contestable also makes it visible, and visible aid is what this population pays for in dignity.

**Purpose.** Take up RW2's unresolved problem and show that the design response runs into a documented trade-off with no care-domain instance. This is the subsection C3 answers to, and it seeds the paper's earned surprises.

**Bracket, second half.** L14 opens by naming the standing question L8 closed on, in those words, so a reader crossing RW3 arrives with the thread intact.

14. **L14, delegation as contested agency.** Picking up the standing question: caregiver frameworks decide the older adult's position for them, whether through onboarding language [24] or through safeguards installed rather than agreed [76]. The alternative reading is well developed. Interdependence reframes access as collaboratively produced and independence-centred design as erasure [13]; care and dependency research describe the same phenomenon, and interdependence is the better frame [30]; autonomy is socially constituted, so ceding control can itself be autonomous [72]; independence is fluid and continually renegotiated [101]; autonomy is achieved through social dependency rather than against it [78]; low-literate users have been observed relinquishing custody as competent strategy [33]. **Tension:** that account and the autonomy-preservation tradition the critical turn documents and criticises [66] make opposite predictions about the same act, and the field has not adjudicated between them. Gap: whether delegation is agency remains contested rather than settled. **Seeded tension carried:** delegated dependence as agency. `/draft` states the split as the literature's own and does not resolve it; Findings must supply a named counter-case before this anchors a claim.
15. **L15, visible aid and standing.** Adoption fails when a device signals decline [17]; devices are chosen to save face, and visible aid threatens social standing [69]; perceived visibility predicts self-stigma and interaction anxiety [20]; deficit-framed home technology reifies dependency and loss of self [73]; the critical turn makes non-use and refusal describable as reasoned positions [58, 11, 48], with distrust itself a bid for accountability rather than a deficit of understanding [57], and the founding critique of ageing discourse supplies the standard [cand-18]. Dignity, autonomy, and style of company are already offered as the dimensions older adults weigh in a home agent [21], the sharpest existing test of the four this paper proposes. **Tension:** contestability requires that a system's decisions be legible before they can be disputed [6, 7], and seamful design goes further and asks that the seam be shown rather than smoothed [28], while the stigma literature establishes that legibility is exactly what costs this population standing [20, 17, 69]. The mechanism must be visible to be contestable and is penalised for being visible. Gap: whether allegiance can be made legible without paying that cost, and under what ceremony. **Guard:** no sentence here may frame an older adult as forgetful or as a problem to repair.
16. **L16, consent instruments without a care instance.** Individual one-time consent is inadequate once data and decisions are distributed across related people [71]; a feminist model gives the vocabulary, freely given, reversible, informed, specific, and ongoing [109]; consent works as an interaction mechanic rather than a dialog box [83]; household data profiling is read as useful and as surveillance by the same participants [104]; privacy settings for shared sensing devices can be scaffolded as an explicit negotiation [122]; one-click consent misfits older adults' shifting boundaries [50]. **Tension:** [109] and [71] argue that consent must be ongoing, explicit, and repeated, while [100] finds that voice-forward consent is more usable and *less* meaningful, because brevity undermines comprehension; a ceremony repeated often enough to be ongoing is a ceremony people stop attending to. One sentence, no more, relocates the reward mechanism: shared family metrics trigger interaction rather than drive behaviour [68, 97], and streak loss carries relational meaning [46, 19], which is why this paper treats a symbolic streak as a relational trigger and not a behavioural lever [31 as the position argued against]. **Seeded tension carried:** streak grief, stated as the literature's own conflict between gamification-as-encouragement [97, 31] and streaks-as-relational-obligation [46, 91], never as our discovery. Gap and close of the section: these instruments have precedents in energy, games, social networks, and shared sensing, and none where the thing consented to is an agent's loyalty inside a household. End on that consequence, with no summary sentence.

**Beat tally.** RW1 four, RW2 four, RW3 five, RW4 three. Sixteen.

### Primary 1: Principal and agent framing, with relative principals

**Ledger status:** committed, row exists (keys [62], [79], [39], [38]). **Carries:** L9, L11, L13.

Constructs: principal, agent, the obedience and inferred-benefit trade-off, relative principals, structural misalignment. **Work it does:** it predicts a specific failure rather than a general worry. An agent configured for one principal does not fail loudly when the principal turns out to be plural; it defaults silently to whoever configured it, and the trade-off literature says that default is not neutral. Delete the framework and L9 becomes an observation that families are complicated, which motivates nothing.

**Rival:** pluralistic alignment as preference aggregation ([105], [29], [cand-7]). It loses the organising job because aggregation reduces a plural principal to a distribution over preferences, and this case is a standing relation with direction, visibility, revocability, and ceremony. Retained inside L9 as the named alternative, which is also what keeps the paragraph generous.

### Primary 2: Intermediated use

**Ledger status:** committed, row exists (keys [98], [33], [1]). **Carries:** L3, L12, and the framing guard on L4.

Constructs: surrogate use, proximate enabling, proximate translation, voluntary relinquishment of custody. **Work it does:** it converts proxy operation from a barrier into a documented arrangement with named forms, which is what lets L3 be an asset paragraph. Without it, the only available reading of household smartphone operation is limited access, which contradicts CLAUDE.md Section 2.4.1.

**Rival:** technology acceptance and localisation framings, under which collective use is friction to design away. Loses because it cannot describe [33]'s finding, in which relinquishing custody is the competent move.

**Extension in revision 3.** L12 uses this framework to state the sharpest form of the paper's problem: intermediated use establishes that the operator and the beneficiary are routinely different people, and alignment has no term for a principal who is not the operator. That sentence is the reason RW3's narrowing half is not a detour into regional context; it is where the two literatures are shown to hold complementary halves of one problem.

### Primary 3: Articulation work and invisible work

**Ledger status:** committed, row exists (keys [106], [85], [8]). **Carries:** L2, L6.

Constructs: articulation work, the ecology of visible and invisible work, visibility as produced by indicators and audiences. **Work it does:** it changes what RW2's gap is. Without it, L6's gap is "these systems are passive", a feature complaint any reviewer answers with "so build an active one". With it, the gap is that announcing an allegiance is itself an intervention in the household's labour, which is a claim about consequences and sets up C3's burden.

**Rival:** an information-flow or contextual-integrity reading. Loses here because it explains who should see what, not what the seeing does to the work.

### Primary 4: Interdependence and relational autonomy, against autonomy preservation

**Ledger status:** committed, rows exist (keys [13], [30], [72]). **Carries:** L14, and the framing of L15.

Constructs: interdependence, relational autonomy, fluid independence. **Work it does:** it makes "the older adult handed the agent to their son" describable as an exercise of agency rather than as a loss, which is the only way L14's tension is a live split rather than a contrarian reading of our own. Delete it and the tension collapses into the autonomy-preservation account it is meant to sit against.

**Rival, named in the beat:** independence-centred assistive design, as documented and criticised in [66]. Retained because the split is the point.

### Primary 5: Postcolonial and decolonial computing

**Ledger status:** committed, row exists (keys [49], [26]). **Carries:** L12 only.

Constructs: the colonial impulse in ubiquitous computing, design frameworks as situated rather than universal. **Work it does:** it licenses the narrowing move without letting it become a claim about a region, and it is why L12 reports [114]'s worked-around decision-support deployment as a structural result rather than as an adoption failure. Delete it and L12's evidence reads as a list of things that went wrong in the Global South, which is the framing CLAUDE.md Section 2.4 forbids and which would also invite the reviewer objection that we generalise from Bangladesh outward.

**Rival:** a plain localisation reading, under which the deployments failed for want of translation or training. Loses because [96] finds the failures structural rather than design-level and [110] finds device operation shaped by household power rather than by preference.

### Supporting: contestability, with seamful design as rival

**Ledger status:** committed, rows exist (keys [6], [7], [28]). **Carries:** L15 and L16.

In L15 it supplies one half of the beat's tension, because contestability requires the legibility the stigma literature penalises; in L16 it supplies the standard the missing consent instrument would have to meet. Marked supporting because outside RW4 it would start decorating.

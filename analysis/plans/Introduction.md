**Six-part recipe as fetched (2026-09-07, via a text-extraction proxy because the Medium page returned 403 to a direct fetch).** 1. *The Drop*: make the reader care before defining anything; the first sentence carries disproportionate weight; use a recognisable situation, statistic, or brief narrative that reveals stakes. 2. *World Building*: the problem's concepts, causes, setting, affected people, stakeholders, and constraining assumptions; definitions come after the reader knows why they are needed. 3. *The Players*: credit prior work fairly; "because of what these people taught us, we can now ask the next question"; for interdisciplinary work, introduce each community and how the paper bridges them; the reader should see an empty space. 4. *Player One*: the specific gap, the approach, and why the study is necessary, in plain confident verbs ("we argue", "we propose"), no hedging about one's own contribution, no "explore" where a precise verb exists. 5. *The Deal*: the research questions, in the introduction, feeling inevitable, telling one story in a logical progression (phenomenon, then how and why, then design). 6. *The Loot*: what the findings contribute and to whom, concretely: the insight, the audience, the implication; never "provides insights into". The recipe is a scaffold, not a paragraph count.

## Section Purpose

The Introduction must let an Associate Chair restate the contribution after page 2: a two-phase household study in Bangladesh, with a two-week prototype deployment between the phases, of how older adults and family caregivers assign, bound, and refuse an AI agent's allegiance when medication work is already collective; the tool, coach, and advocate roles and the four dimensions (direction, visibility, revocability, ceremony) as the vocabulary for that negotiation; and the Affiliation Ledger plus the reframing of streaks as relational triggers as the design consequence. It serves C1, C2, and C3 and previews all three RQs. It argues (dense contrast frames, longest sentences in the paper) and never reports a finding as established; foreshadowing is the ceiling.

**Target length.** 1,000 to 1,300 words, eight to ten paragraphs, one contribution list. At that length the style guide's targets translate to 7 to 10 contrast frames, 12 to 17 citation brackets, 6 to 9 first-person mentions, and at most one sentence over 35 words.

**Standing constraints carried from `output/Method.md`, A2, and CLAUDE.md Sections 2.4, 2.5, 3.** 25 participants, 17 older adults and 8 family caregivers; two weeks of deployment in every household; Bangla; participants' homes; urban and rural; January to June 2026. The word "agent" for the conceptual object, "prototype" only if the Introduction describes the built thing. The only decision the agent took on its own that prose may name is the request to notify a family member after an unconfirmed dose. The four unbuilt mechanisms (risk-graded weakening veto, patterned silence, probationary mode, shared family scores) are described designs; the Introduction may name them only as such. No "polyadic". No dashes. No priority claim. No caregiver name. No claim that an older adult and a caregiver described the same episode. No decision log. No causal verb. The caregiver phase-participation figure stays out of the Introduction entirely (open blocker 6).

## Phase 2: Beat Decomposition (six-part recipe)

Beats are numbered I1 to I10. Each names its recipe part, its purpose, and the CLAUDE.md checklist item it discharges.

### Part 1, The Drop (I1)

- **I1. The decision that answers an unasked question.** One paragraph, three to five sentences. Open on the agent's situation, not on ageing statistics and not on a participant story: a scheduled dose goes unconfirmed, the agent can remind again, stay silent, or tell a family member, and whichever it does it has answered whom it works for. Then the stakes in both directions (an agent that tells everyone becomes surveillance and costs the older adult dignity; an agent that guards the older adult absolutely can hide danger from the people who would respond). Land on a short flat sentence that the rest of the paper makes inevitable: in a Bangladeshi household that question has no settled answer, because medication work there is already collective. Discharges "hook and stakes" and states the problem as CLAUDE.md 2.2 and `proposal.md` Section 1 phrase it. **Recommendation against a participant-story hook:** the C02 wedding account and the C03 hospital account exist in Phase 1 (`phase-1/Caregiver/C02.md` L81 and L85; `C03.md` L33), but `proposal.md` Section 3 conflates them into one "diabetic mother hospitalized after a dose forgotten at a wedding", which no transcript says; A2 grades both as temporal accounts, not causal evidence; and the style guide forbids citing our own findings in the Introduction. If `/draft` wants a vignette it belongs in I3 as "in our formative interviews, a caregiver described ...", one account, by ID, no causal verb.

### Part 2, World Building (I2, I3, I4)

- **I2. Agents now act, and every act has a direction.** Reminders have become agents that decide when to speak, when to stay silent, and when to escalate; that initiative is technically ordinary now (mixed-initiative and proactive systems). Each such act prioritises someone's authority or interest, today by silent default and usually in favour of whoever configured the device. Introduces "the agent" as anchor term. Cites [45], [52], [70], [42].
- **I3. Medication work in a Bangladeshi household is a collective practice.** The care network as the unit: relatives remind, hold the prescription, buy medicine, interpret handwriting, operate the phone by proxy, and check; checking reads as care as well as oversight; doctors keep authority over changes. Bangladesh is ageing with family-based eldercare and shared, intermediated device use. Frames this as the household's existing assets, not a deficit. Introduces "care network" (provenance [22]) and the collectivist-versus-individualist contrast that generalises the paper. Cites [3], [4], [98], [33] for shared and intermediated use; [5], [51], [90], [12] for ageing and family-based eldercare; [92], [102], [9] for chronic-illness work as family practice; [106] for coordination labour in one clause. May carry one Phase 1 A2 fact stated as ours ("our formative interviews documented ...") without a quotation. Discharges "medication work as a collective practice rather than a private one" and the framing commitment "design gap, not a memory problem".
- **I4. Define allegiance, because the reader now needs it.** Allegiance is whose interests or authority an agent's action appears to prioritise at a particular moment (verbatim from `output/Related-Work.md`, Negotiating Who the Agent Serves); narrower than alignment, different from access control. Two directions of mismatch: individualist adherence tools assume one user with a private device defending autonomy against others; that assumption is structural, not a localisation gap. Cites [32], [62] for the alignment vocabulary and [107], [108], [35] for the individual-user assumption in adherence tools. Discharges the anchor-term rule and one standing contrast frame.

### Part 3, The Players (I5)

- **I5. Four literatures, credited, each leaving the same space empty.** One paragraph, or two if the citation density demands it, ordered exactly as `output/Related-Work.md` orders its subsections. (a) Aging HCI's critical turn and interdependence scholarship show that receiving help and exercising agency are not opposites, and caregiving research shows family involvement redistributes visibility and responsibility [66], [13], [30], [24], [115], [47]. (b) Global South HCI shows device use in Bangladesh is shared and intermediated and warns against reading it through individualist assumptions [3], [98], [33], [49]. (c) Alignment and agent research names the plural-principal problem and the obedience trade-off, with one principal by construction [38], [79], [62], [121]. (d) Contestable AI and consent research make system state disputable and permission revisable, in settings where a human still makes the disclosure choice [6], [28], [71], [50]. Close on the load-bearing contrast: care dashboards and awareness systems report to whoever set them up, whereas an agent that escalates must itself decide whom to tell and when, and that decision is what a family negotiates [82], [94], [81], [111]. The gap sentence is descriptive, in Related-Work.md's own shape ("what remains unclear is how ..."), and carries no "first", "only", or "no prior work". Discharges "the gap stated as Related-Work.md states it, never as an earlier three-literature framing" (the four-literature ordering replaces `proposal.md` Section 2's three).

### Part 4, Player One (I6, I7)

- **I6. What we did.** Flat declaratives. We conducted a two-phase qualitative household study in Bangladesh with 17 older adults and 8 family caregivers, interviewing each in their own right about the same household. Phase 1 documented existing medication work before any technology; we then built a prototype whose deployed behaviour was scheduled Bangla reminders, a logbook, a streak, a heat map, a spoken announcement of whom it was currently serving, and, after an unconfirmed dose, a request asking whether a family member should be notified, which the older adult could decline. Households used it for two weeks; Phase 2 interviews asked older adults and caregivers separately about that period, and about four further mechanisms we described but did not build. Source: `output/Method.md` throughout. Precise agency: the request to notify is the one act the agent initiated on its own; schedules and changes of whom it served stayed under human control.
- **I7. What we argue.** Plain verbs, no hedge on our own contribution (recipe rule). We argue that an agent inside a care network cannot keep one master, and we read its position through three roles, tool (aligned with the older adult), coach (shared between older adult and caregiver), and advocate (aligned with the family), moving along four dimensions: direction, visibility, revocability, and ceremony. We propose that the design target is not a stronger reminder but an agent whose allegiance a family can inspect and move. This beat carries the paper's thesis contrast frame (agent that negotiates rather than system that notifies). Source: `proposal.md` Section 1 for the roles and dimensions (uncontradicted by `output/`); A2 Phase 2 older-adult theme 1 and caregiver theme 1 for the empirical warrant that families accepted bounded, visible, revocable involvement.

### Part 5, The Deal (I8)

- **I8. Three research questions, in order.** Use the `rqs.md` wording verbatim `[DIRECTIVE]`, with the `proposal.md` Section 4 wording held in this plan for comparison (see Contradictions, item 4). The progression is phenomenon (RQ1: how care networks distribute, negotiate, and morally account for medication work before an agent enters), then how and why (RQ2: through what practices older adults and caregivers assign, contest, and change the agent's allegiance, and under what conditions shifts are accepted or resisted), then design (RQ3: which roles are legitimate under what conditions, and what mechanisms make shifts visible, revocable, negotiable, and dignity-preserving). One short lead sentence makes the sequence feel inevitable: the first question asks what the household already does, the second what it does with an agent, the third what the agent must therefore make possible. `rqs.md` is preferred because it drops "genuine initiative" from RQ2, which the deployed autonomy record cannot back, and adds "revocable" to RQ3, which Related-Work.md's fourth gap already promises.

### Part 6, The Loot (I9, I10)

- **I9. Contributions, restated to what the study supports.** A three-item list, each naming the insight, the audience, and the implication (recipe rule against "provides insights into").
  - **C1, empirical.** For researchers of ageing, care, and Global South HCI: an account of how Bangladeshi care networks distribute medication work through differentiated roles before an agent arrives (A2 Phase 1 themes 1 to 4), and of how older adults and caregivers, interviewed separately about the same two-week deployment, accepted the agent's involvement of family only when it announced itself, asked first, allowed refusal, and reached one chosen relative, while self-managers refused routine disclosure altogether (A2 Phase 2 older-adult themes 1, 4, 5; caregiver theme 1). Negative cases retained. **Not** "paired episode accounts" and **not** "the agent's logged decision" (unfiled; see Contradictions 1).
  - **C2, conceptual.** For alignment and agent researchers: allegiance as a situated relation, the tool, coach, and advocate roles, and the four dimensions, offered as a vocabulary for any agent that serves a plural principal, whether a household, a shared budget, or a classroom; generalised along the collectivist-versus-individualist axis, with every empirical claim bounded to Bangladesh.
  - **C3, design.** For designers of agents in shared settings: the Affiliation Ledger, of which the spoken announcement and the request-and-grant were deployed and the weakening veto, silence-as-participation, and probationary mode were described to participants only; and the reframing of streaks and heat maps as relational triggers, grounded in caregivers reading the streak as a record of routine care work and older adults reporting both small interest and grief at a broken streak (A2 caregiver theme 2; older-adult theme 2).
- **I10. The surprise foreshadowed, then the principle.** One closing paragraph. Foreshadow, without quoting, the finding that violates the autonomy-preservation expectation: several older adults treated the agent's move toward family, when they had granted it, as trust rather than lost independence, and described being checked on as care; but this held only when they stayed in control, and one self-manager refused any family involvement in ordinary reminders (A2 Phase 2 older-adult theme 1 and theme 4; counter-cases "Routine disclosure is refused", P17, and "Performance pressure revokes value", P11). One clause foreshadows the second surprise: caregivers read the deployed streak as a record of their own care work rather than as motivation for the older adult (A2 caregiver theme 2). Close on the design principle as a short flat sentence: make the agent's loyalty something families can see and move. No summary, no restatement of the contributions.

**Single most surprising A2 result worth foreshadowing.** Delegated dependence as agency, anchored to A2 Phase 2 older-adult theme 1 ("Older adults accept family involvement when they stay in control") and subtheme "Family setup can preserve older adult control" (theme 4). It is chosen over streak grief because it answers the title question directly, is claimable from lived use (the request-to-notify ran for two weeks), and sets up C2. Streak grief and the caregivers' care-work reading are the secondary foreshadow because the streak was deployed and the reading runs against gamification-as-encouragement.

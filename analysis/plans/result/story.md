# Findings Story: When Care Loses Its Timing

## The Story in One Sentence

Medication care became fragile when routines moved; families asked the agent to carry timing and expose a bounded handoff, but its records created new uncertainty, so older adults and caregivers repeatedly adjusted whom it served, what it showed, and when people resumed control.

## Narrative Principle

The story begins with a small medication moment, not with a broad claim that care already works. An older adult leaves home, the familiar meal and storage cues recede, and nobody nearby asks about the dose. A caregiver elsewhere cannot tell whether silence means the medicine was taken, delayed, unheard, or forgotten. Calling can offer reassurance and also become another intrusive check. This is the motivating tension.

The narrative then broadens one layer at a time:

1. A disrupted dose reveals a fragile handoff.
2. The handoff reveals differentiated care work and the limits of existing tools.
3. Those limits become expectations for a bounded agent.
4. Deployment shows where the agent fits and where its own records create new work.
5. Repeated adjustment becomes a care framework for movable allegiance.

This order preserves the asset-based framing without resolving the research problem in the opening. Care exists, but its coordination is contingent.

## Act I: Care Became Fragile When Routines Moved

### The opening scene

P01 supplies the first movement: “At home, everything is fixed. I know where the medicine is, I know the time. When travelling, everything changes” (P01, Phase 1, L95). The problem is not introduced as generalized forgetting. The routine has lost the places, meals, people, and objects that normally carry timing.

The caregiver enters the same tension from a different position. C03 says, “If nobody checks then I worry. So it is pressure” (C03, Phase 1, L49). P05 then supplies the relational cost of that response: “Sometimes I get irritated also because she asks so much. But later I think okay she is helping” (P05, Phase 1, L223). These are not paired household accounts. Their analytic relation is the uncertain handoff: caregivers lack evidence, while older adults live with the questions that uncertainty produces.

### Broadening to the care network

Only after the tension is established does the story widen into the Phase 1 theme `Medication work is distributed through differentiated household roles`. Older adults take and recognize; relatives keep prescriptions, buy medicine, configure devices, and check; pharmacists interpret difficult writing; doctors authorize changes. P08's “Taking is easy. Knowing which medicine is little difficult” keeps older adult action distinct from written knowledge. P10's “we don't live in isolation” locates care in family and community. P17's “My memory is very sharp” prevents interdependence from becoming compulsory.

Paper prescriptions, packages, fixed locations, notes, alarms, button phones, personal smartphones, and shared smartphones belong to the same network. They carry care when aligned and move work to another person when they become difficult to read, configure, or operate. The Bangladesh finding is therefore not that older adults lack care. It is that care depends on a shifting alignment among people, devices, documents, places, and time.

### Exit tension

Once that alignment is visible, the next question is concrete: why could current tools signal a dose yet fail to complete the household handoff?

## Act II: Existing Tools Signaled Time but Could Not Complete the Handoff

### Why task-level tools left coordination unfinished

Breakdown names specific events: travel removed situated cues; work and visitors displaced attention; stock ran out; packages changed; several family members became busy; distance removed verification; alarms rang without naming the medicine; pillboxes added refilling; and multi-step interaction moved device work to relatives. Notes and ordinary alarms remained sufficient in some accounts. The problem is therefore fit across a handoff, not universal tool failure.

This act explicitly discusses the remaining Phase 1 themes:

- `Routine transitions expose weak coordination and uncertain verification` explains why the established division of work becomes fragile.
- `Existing aids fit tasks but miss household handoffs` explains why another alarm cannot solve supply, interpretation, or family coordination.
- `Families expect technology to support bounded care network roles` converts each observed tension into an elicited condition for an agent.

### Frustration becomes expectation

Participants expected semantic Bangla reminders, few interaction steps, another opportunity before escalation, visible family involvement, medicine-only access, kin-supported configuration, human verification, and continuing clinical authority. The expectation is not a more insistent reminder. It is a bounded handoff whose direction and visibility people can understand.

The deployed agent responded only to part of that expectation: manually configured reminders with medicine name and timing, a logbook, daily heat map, symbolic streak, spoken announcement of whom it served, and a permission request before family notification after nonconfirmation. Prescription capture, stock sensing, selected-recipient configuration, retry schedules, patterned silence, and risk-graded refusal were not deployed.

### Exit tension

The agent now has a precise place in the story. It enters at the fragile handoff to carry timing, make a possible move to family visible, and record a response. Phase 2 asks whether doing so changes care without taking it over.

## Act III: The Agent Carried Time, but Families Still Carried Care

### What participants described as better

The Phase 2 theme `The agent supports routines while people retain care` returns to the opening scene. P06 says, “At home my wife can tell me. In the field there is nobody asking” (P06, Phase 2, L179). The reminder travels where the spouse and household routine do not. Participants also describe anticipating the prompt, while the leading interview question limits this to felt timing rather than cognitive improvement.

Reported improvement remains coordination-specific:

- reminders provide a portable cue during disrupted routines;
- relatives can configure while older adults listen, recognize, confirm, question, and take medicine;
- caregivers describe less continuous clock watching and fewer routine questions;
- spoken allegiance clarifies whom the agent addresses;
- permission makes family involvement perceptible and refusable.

These are participant-reported changes, not significant effects. No logs, adherence measures, workload measures, or clinical outcomes exist.

### Who receives the first prompt

The theme `Older adults control when the agent involves family` prevents a universal sequence. A self-managing older adult expects the first opportunity to respond. C05 instead reports, “Mostly it reminded me, because I am the one managing their medicines” (C05, Phase 2, L17). Direction follows the next medication task, not age or device ownership.

Shared-device and proxy-use accounts deepen this finding. P05's daughter configures the system, but P05 receives and acts on the reminder. This arrangement preserves participation while inspection, correction, refusal, and fallback remain available. It becomes hidden dependence when every change requires an unavailable relative.

### Exit tension

The agent has moved timing and caregiver attention, but its record cannot observe ingestion. The next act begins where this apparent improvement produces its own problem: an unanswered prompt becomes a family concern.

## Act IV: The Agent Made Care Visible, but Not Certain

### The record gap

The theme `Caregivers act on uncertain exceptions, not automatic judgments` starts with P05: “I had actually taken it, I just forgot the phone part” (P05, Phase 2, L19). A missing confirmation can reflect a taken dose, work, travel, conversation, delay, or an unheard prompt. The alert can direct useful attention and still misrepresent the event. Older adults risk being treated as having missed medicine; caregivers risk receiving unnecessary worry.

Participants respond by checking rather than accepting the record as truth. Prescriptions, medicine strips, notes, relatives, pharmacists, doctors, and established routines remain verification sources. Reliance grows around one stable, checked schedule and reopens after a change or error. This is the trust-through-self-verification tension.

The next expectation follows directly: separate confirmed taken, reported not taken, and unresolved; explain why attention is requested; allow time and correction; retry; then contact one chosen person. Only the uncertainty and permissioned notification were experienced. The fuller sequence remains elicited or hypothetical.

### Gamification inherits the same uncertainty

The theme `Streaks show care work, while medicine remains primary` is not a separate gamification detour. The streak is another representation built from confirmations, so the same gap between record and medicine applies. It can make otherwise quiet medication coordination visible and invite modest family recognition. It can also produce grief, blame, pressure, or false interpretation.

P10's broken streak supplies both tension and adjustment. He is upset by losing accumulated progress, and his son redirects attention to the next dose. The family repairs the metric's social consequence. Medicine remains primary, while the coach role stays acceptable only when the streak is optional, quiet, recoverable, and visible within chosen relationships. Shared family scores were not deployed.

### Collectivism widens, but authority does not flatten

The theme `Friends and community support care, while doctors guide medicine` extends the care network beyond a one-way caregiver relation. Older adults remind spouses and peers, receive reminders, and turn health talk into questions. P06 says, “I also ask her about her medicine. So it is both ways” (P06, Phase 2, L79).

Community participation also carries risk. Peer experience can cue remembering or technology trust, but conflicting advice can confuse. Social conversation does not authorize digital sharing, and doctors or pharmacists retain medicine authority. These community mechanisms were mainly elicited and were not agent features during deployment.

### Exit tension

Every representation exposes some care and hides something else. A reminder hides context, a confirmation hides ingestion, a streak hides unrecorded care, and community trust hides technical uncertainty. The final act therefore cannot prescribe more visibility or more autonomy. It must organize how families keep the agent's position changeable.

## Act V: A Care Framework for Moving the Agent's Allegiance

The framework describes a continuous adjustment cycle rather than a ladder of agent capability:

1. **Locate existing work.** Identify who takes, recognizes, configures, supplies, checks, interprets, and authorizes, together with the devices and records involved.
2. **Identify disruption.** Specify which cue, person, artifact, supply, or record no longer aligns.
3. **Assign a bounded role.** The agent acts as tool for a verified routine, coach for chosen recognition, or advocate for a proposed handoff.
4. **Make allegiance perceptible.** Direction, visibility, revocability, and ceremony reveal whom the agent serves and how that position can change.
5. **Verify and revise.** People compare the agent's representation with situated knowledge, grant or refuse the handoff, and return to fallback practices when needed.

The deployed announcement and permission request form the experienced portion of the Affiliation Ledger. Unimplemented features remain at the future edge of the framework.

The ending returns to the opening dose. The older adult travels; the familiar cue recedes; the agent reminds; a response remains absent; and family involvement becomes possible. What changes across the story is not a steady increase in agent authority. Families repeatedly move its allegiance according to the task, evidence, relationship, and social cost present at that moment.

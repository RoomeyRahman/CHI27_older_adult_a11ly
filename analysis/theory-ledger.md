## Analysis note, 2026-09-06 (slot A2 Phase 1 reconciliation)

The A2 source level audit commits articulation work [106] as the carrying structure for Phase 1. It accounts for medication work distributed across older adults, kin, pharmacists, doctors, artifacts, and routines, and it changes the design conclusion from stronger reminders to accountable handoffs. Interdependence [13], [30] and intermediated use [98], [33], [1] remain supporting frames for delegated information work that preserves direct older adult participation.

The logic of care [80] remains useful but does not become the spine. It helps interpret checking and delegated setup, while independent self managers and the consistent authority granted to prescriptions and clinicians exceed that lens. Individual autonomy remains the strongest rival because it explains refusal, privacy boundaries, and competence claims. No new framework was introduced. The resulting Phase 1 framework, distribution of work, conditions of handoff, and accountability boundary, is an empirical analytic synthesis rather than an imported theory. The revision makes relational interdependence explicit but treats it as a situated Bangladeshi pattern, not a regional cultural rule. Gamification theory remains unloaded in Phase 1 because the formative corpus contains no direct assessment of game-like features.

## Introduction drafting note, 2026-09-07

The Introduction commits principal and agent framing with plural principals ([62], supported by [32], [38], and [79]) as its primary account of why an agent's action must be read in relation to more than one legitimate source of authority. Interdependence ([13], [30]) carries the older-adult agency argument: accepting bounded family involvement can express agency when inspection and refusal remain available. Intermediated use ([98], [33]) and postcolonial computing ([49]) position shared and proxy device use as sociotechnical arrangements rather than user deficits. Contestability by design ([6]) carries the design consequence, with seamful design ([28]) retained as the account of how a role change becomes perceptible.

Mol's logic of care ([80]) remains open and does not appear in the Introduction because it does not explain the corpus's self-managers or its reliance on prescriptions and clinicians. Articulation work ([106]) remains committed elsewhere, but it is excluded from this Introduction: `reference.bib` contains the entry while no corresponding filed PDF is present, so the drafting protocol does not permit it to carry a citation here. The deletion test removed [38], [79], [28], and [30] from any paragraph where their absence would not alter the conclusion; they remain available only where the final prose makes their distinct theoretical work explicit.
